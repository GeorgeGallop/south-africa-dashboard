🏕 Eskom Power Parks Programme

A new State-led initiative introduced by the Minister of Electricity and Energy, Dr. Kgosientsho Ramokgopa, to lower the barriers to entry for emerging power developers, widen ownership in the energy sector, and cluster new generation on strategic sites that have transmission, battery storage and local economic development built in.

What was announced

On 7 October 2026, Minister Kgosientsho Ramokgopa announced a package of measures to expand South Africa's generation capacity over the 2026–2037 planning horizon. The announcement was delivered together with two instruments the Power Parks Programme sits inside:

  • A new Section 34 Determination for significant additional generation capacity, covering a diverse energy mix — Wind Solar Battery storage Gas-to-power.
  • A new multi-year procurement pipeline designed to give the market long-term visibility and stability, so developers and financiers can plan beyond a single bid window.
  • The State-led Power Parks Programme — the focus of this page.
Why it matters: South Africa's grid has been dominated by very large, single-site projects. A State-led "park" model clusters multiple generators — and the grid, storage and services they need — onto prepared strategic sites, which lowers the cost and complexity for smaller and emerging developers who cannot build that infrastructure alone.

The Power Parks Programme

The programme is State-led: government prepares and de-risks strategic sites so that generation can be built on them. As set out in the announcement, it aims to:

Lower barriers to entry

Reduce the hurdles for emerging developers, especially new and local players who struggle to fund grid, land and infrastructure preparation on their own.

Broaden ownership

Promote broader ownership in the energy sector — widening participation beyond the established large independent power producers.

Cluster on strategic sites

Use strategic sites to co-locate generation with the transmission and battery storage it needs, and to anchor local economic development around the park.

Design principles behind a "power park"

Read together, the announced elements point to a delivery model with four recurring themes. These are the design principles the programme is being built around:

  1. Prepared sites. Government identifies and readies strategic land so that developers arrive at a site that is already serviced and consented, rather than starting from greenfield.
  2. Transmission first. Sites are chosen where grid capacity can be made available, directly addressing the transmission constraint that has bottlenecked new generation.
  3. Storage integrated. Battery energy storage is treated as part of the park's core infrastructure, not an afterthought — helping firm up variable wind and solar.
  4. Local economic development. Parks are intended to anchor jobs, skills and local supply chains in the surrounding area, echoing the "just transition" logic applied elsewhere in the power sector.

How it fits into the wider procurement picture

The Power Parks Programme does not replace the existing procurement route. It is one part of a broader three-part package announced on the same day:

What is not yet known

Read with care: The public announcement confirmed the intent and shape of the programme. Many operational details that would normally accompany a procurement round are still to be published. Treat the following as open until confirmed in the official gazette, determination notice or bid documentation.
  • Sites and capacity. The specific strategic sites, their locations and the megawatt capacity to be allocated to each park.
  • Determination specifics. The exact megawatts, technologies and timelines named in the Section 34 Determination.
  • Procurement windows. The sequencing of bid windows under the new multi-year pipeline and how the parks will be allocated (auction, tender or direct State development).
  • Ownership rules. The precise mechanism — local content, equity or developer requirements — used to deliver "broader ownership".
  • Funding model. How the State funds site preparation, grid connection and battery storage, and how costs are recovered.
