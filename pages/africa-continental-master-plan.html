What it is

The Continental Power System Master Plan (CMP) is Africa’s long-term electricity infrastructure plan. Energy ministers tasked AUDA-NEPAD with it in 2019. The African Union Commission and AFREC lead the political and data side, with AfDB, the EU, IRENA, Power Africa and Germany’s BMZ as the main technical and money partners.

It is the physical layer of the continental energy project: generation, transmission, and a short list of cross-border interconnectors that would let surplus hydro, solar and gas move between regions. The market layer is the African Single Electricity Market (AfSEM). Together they are Agenda 2063 energy flagships and a named priority of the AU–EU Green Energy Initiative and Global Gateway.

The five power pools — SAPP, WAPP, CAPP/PEAC, EAPP and COMELEC — are the regional building blocks. The CMP is supposed to join them.

Keep the two 2040 numbers apart. The CMP demand case runs from 878 TWh in 2021 to 2,368 TWh in 2040. AUDA-NEPAD’s 2025 annual report separately says the CMP aims to increase Africa’s energy capacity fivefold by 2040. Demand and installed capacity are different rulers.

The stack

Three layers. The CMP is the wires. The pools are the regions. AfSEM is the market.

1 · PLAN

CMP

Continental generation and transmission masterplan. Priority interconnectors, project preparation, and the CEPA planning desk that is supposed to cut study time in half.

2 · REGIONS

Power pools

SAPP (Gaborone), WAPP (Cotonou), CAPP/PEAC (Brazzaville), EAPP (Addis Ababa), COMELEC (Algiers). Dual members sit in more than one pool. South Africa’s pool is SAPP.

3 · MARKET

AfSEM

Single African electricity market: regulatory, technical and market readiness, plus independent regulator / TSO / DSO as continental guidelines. Target: operational by 2040.

The preparation bottleneck the CMP is trying to break: typical interconnector studies take 6–8 years. The CEPA / Phase III pitch is to cut that toward about four years — roughly half.

Who runs it

  • AUDA-NEPAD — implementing agency for the CMP. Owns the masterplan, the priority list, and the project-preparation pipeline.
  • AUC + AFREC — political and energy-data lead. AfSEM policy sits here.
  • STC-TTIIE — AU Specialised Technical Committee on Transport, Transcontinental and Interregional Infrastructure, and Energy. Energy ministers are the political council.
  • CEPA — Continental Energy Planning Agency, the new desk meant to keep studies moving. Workshop in Addis Ababa 3–5 June 2026; inaugural Steering Committee on 5 June 2026.
  • HTOC — Harmonisation of Transmission Operations Committee, AfSEM’s technical body. Inaugural meeting 30 June–1 July 2025; formalised 28–30 September 2026 in Addis with a governance manual and technical working groups.
  • Partners — AfDB (feasibility follow-on), EU / Global Gateway, IRENA, Power Africa, BMZ.

The five power pools remain the operators of their own markets. CMP and AfSEM sit above them as continental planning and rules, not as a sixth pool.

Priority interconnectors

Up to twenty CMP priority interconnectors are on the books with the power pools. The first two pre-feasibility studies were validated in June 2026: Burundi–Tanzania and DRC–Angola. One of those already has AfDB follow-on funding into full feasibility. Four more were in drafting at the same workshop: Egypt–Libya, Rwanda–Tanzania, Angola–Zambia, Botswana–Namibia.

SAPP’s named four in the 2025–26 CEPA work are BONA, DRC–Angola, Angola–Zambia (~2,000 MW / ~1,200 km) and DRC–South Africa (Inga 3, 3,000+ km / ~3,000 MW). A Gaborone workshop in August 2026 bundled BONA with BOSA and BOZA. ANNA (Angola–Namibia, Baynes) is the PIDA western seam.

Where it is now (2025–2026)

The strategy decade is over. The live work is packaging interconnectors so a bank will look at them, and standing up the two desks that are supposed to keep studies from dying in drawers.

  • November 2025, Luanda — Africa Energy Bankability Forum session on CMP interconnectors.
  • June 2026, Addis — CEPA workshop 3–5 June; inaugural CEPA Steering Committee 5 June. First two pre-feasibility studies validated (Burundi–Tanzania, DRC–Angola).
  • June 2025 then September 2026 — HTOC inaugural meeting, then formalisation in Addis with a governance manual and working groups. That is AfSEM’s operating committee, not a new pool.
  • August 2026, Gaborone — SAPP implementation-framework workshop covering BONA, BOSA and BOZA together.

What is not done: a live continental market, Grand Inga at generation scale, or a completed DRC–South Africa HVDC. Those remain studies and politics. The honest 2026 picture is a ratified plan, a short validated corridor list, and institutions that have just started meeting.

South Africa’s place in it

Pretoria’s regional plug is SAPP. The domestic market being stood up is SAWEM, with NTCSA as the transmission system operator. AfSEM is the continental layer those two would eventually sit under. The CMP flagship that actually bites Pretoria is Grand Inga / DRC–South Africa — a 3,000+ km, ~3,000 MW transfer of Inga hydropower via Zambia and Zimbabwe.

South Africa already trades on live SAPP paths: Cahora Bassa HVDC from Mozambique, the Namibia 400 kV, Botswana 400 kV, and the constrained Zimbabwe–Matimba line. BOSA is the CMP strengthening of the Botswana path. ZTK (~2029) is the north-bound seam that would eventually let East African hydro and geothermal show up in SAPP.

AfCFTA is ratified. The African Passport is not. Energy integration can move on interconnectors and pool rules without free movement of people; it cannot move without a creditworthy offtaker, a functioning TSO, and a transmission code that neighbouring pools will accept. That is the NTCSA / SAWEM / SAPP problem, not an Addis communiqué problem.
