Province profile

KwaZulu-Natal

KwaZulu-Natal pairs a major Indian Ocean port economy with rich Zulu heritage, humid coastal tourism, and productive inland agriculture stretching from Durban to the Drakensberg.

At a glance

  • Capital: Pietermaritzburg
  • Population: About 12.4 million
  • Largest city: Durban
  • Known for: Ports, tourism, and sugarcane

Population

The province has about 12.4 million residents. Dense settlement along the coast and in the urban core contrasts with smaller inland towns and rural traditional areas.

Brief history

KwaZulu-Natal developed from the Zulu kingdom, colonial port settlement, and later industrial expansion around Durban. Its history is closely tied to trade, migration, and political struggle.

Key industries

Port logistics, manufacturing, tourism, sugarcane, forestry, retail, and agriculture are among the province’s most important sectors.

Major cities & towns

Durban, Pietermaritzburg, Richards Bay, Newcastle, Ladysmith, Ulundi, and Empangeni serve as major urban centres.

Climate

The coast is humid and subtropical with summer rainfall, while the interior becomes cooler and more varied as altitude rises toward the Drakensberg and northern hills.

Natural resources

Sugarcane fields, timber plantations, coal, fertile soils, beaches, marine resources, and strong port access underpin the province’s economy.

Future outlook

Future growth is linked to port expansion, tourism recovery, industrial diversification, and climate-resilient agriculture. Investment in transport and flood resilience will be increasingly important.
