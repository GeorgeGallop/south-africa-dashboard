Concentration

The beneficiation stage that upgrades run-of-mine ore into a high-grade concentrate for smelting and downstream processing.

What concentration is

Concentration, also called mineral beneficiation or ore dressing, is the set of processes that separate valuable minerals from waste rock (gangue) after mining. It transforms low-grade run-of-mine ore into a concentrated product with a much higher metal or mineral content, making subsequent smelting and refining economically viable.

Without concentration, most ores would be too dilute to transport or smelt profitably. It is the essential first processing step that sits between mining and the manufacturing chain.

The concentration workflow
  • Crushing and screening: primary crushers reduce run-of-mine ore to manageable size; screens classify material for the next stage.
  • Grinding and milling: rod mills, ball mills and semi-autogenous grinding (SAG) mills reduce ore to a fine powder, liberating mineral particles from gangue.
  • Classification: hydrocyclones and screens separate particles by size and density, recycling oversize material back to the mill.
  • Physical separation: gravity spirals, shaking tables, jigs and dense media separation exploit density differences between minerals and waste.
  • Froth flotation: the most widely used method — reagents render target minerals hydrophobic; rising air bubbles collect them into a froth, which is skimmed off as concentrate.
  • Magnetic and electrostatic separation: high-intensity magnets and electrostatic plates separate minerals based on magnetic susceptibility or conductivity.
  • Dewatering and drying: thickeners, filters and rotary dryers remove water from the concentrate to produce a dry, transportable product for smelters.
Key concentration technologies
Technology How it works Common applications
Froth flotation Air bubbles attach to hydrophobic mineral particles and rise to form a froth layer Copper, PGMs, coal, zinc, nickel, phosphate
Gravity separation Dense minerals settle faster in water or slurry flows Gold, diamonds, tin, iron ore, chromite
Magnetic separation Magnetic minerals are pulled from non-magnetic gangue by strong magnets Iron ore, manganese, vanadium, ilmenite
Dense media separation Ore is immersed in a heavy liquid or slurry; lighter gangue floats, heavy minerals sink Diamonds, chromite, ferrosilicon recovery
Electrostatic separation Charged particles are deflected by an electric field, separating conductors from non-conductors Mineral sands (zircon, rutile, ilmenite)
Concentration by commodity
Platinum Group Metals (PGMs)

PGM ore is crushed, milled and floated in a multi-stage circuit to produce a nickel-copper-PGM sulphide concentrate. This concentrate is then smelted into matte at operations such as those run by Amplats, Impala and Sibanye-Stillwater.

Gold

Gold ore is crushed, milled and concentrated using gravity (centrifugal concentrators, shaking tables) and flotation, followed by cyanide leaching or direct smelting to produce gold doré.

Copper and base metals

Copper sulphide ores are crushed, ground and floated in a selective flotation circuit to produce a copper concentrate typically grading 20–30% copper, which is then smelted and refined. Zinc and nickel follow similar flotation-based routes.

Iron ore

Iron ore is crushed, screened and beneficiated through gravity, magnetic separation and flotation to remove silica and alumina. The resulting concentrate grades above 64% iron and feeds blast furnaces or DRI plants.

Manganese

Manganese ore is crushed, washed and screened. Dense media separation and magnetic separation upgrade the ore to the grades required for ferromanganese and silicomanganese production.

Chrome

Chromite ore is beneficiated using gravity spirals, shaking tables and magnetic separation to produce a concentrate with a Cr₂O₃ content typically above 40%, which is then smelted into ferrochrome.

Diamonds

Diamond recovery uses dense media separation and X-ray sorting after crushing and screening. Diamonds are not smelted but are concentrated for sorting, cutting and polishing.

Coal

Coal is washed in dense medium cyclones, spirals and flotation cells to separate combustible carbon from ash, sulfur and other impurities. Washed coal has a higher calorific value and lower ash content for metallurgical or power generation use.

Why concentration matters

Concentration is the step that makes the rest of the minerals value chain possible. By upgrading ore close to the mine, it reduces transport costs, minimises energy consumption in smelting, and dramatically lowers waste volumes downstream. A well-designed concentrator can double or triple the metal grade entering a smelter, improving efficiency and profitability across the entire chain.

For South Africa, concentration plants are major industrial facilities in their own right — they employ thousands of workers, consume significant power and water, and are the critical bottleneck that determines how much metal can ultimately be produced.

From concentration to smelting

Once concentrate leaves the mill, it is transported to a smelter for the next stage of the manufacturing chain. The quality, moisture content and chemical composition of the concentrate directly affect smelting efficiency and cost.
