Seriti Resources

South Africa's Biggest Black-Owned Coal Producer — Powering Eskom, Then Powering the Energy Transition

Seriti Resources is South Africa's largest majority black-owned mining company and the largest supplier of coal to Eskom. Created in 2017 through the acquisition of Anglo American's Eskom-tied thermal coal mines, it expanded dramatically in 2021 by absorbing South32's South Africa Energy Coal — making it the country's dominant domestic coal producer. Seriti supplies coal to five major Eskom power stations (Kriel, Tutuka, Lethabo, Kendal and Duvha) and is positioning itself as an "energy business that produces coal" — building the Mpumalanga wind hub and training former mineworkers for renewable energy jobs under the banner of just transition.

History & Origins
  • 2017: Seriti Resources was incorporated in April 2017 as a broad-based, majority black-owned and -controlled mining company, assembled specifically to acquire Anglo American's Eskom-tied domestic thermal coal operations. The consortium was led by CEO Mike Teke, with significant participation from community and employee shareholder trusts.
  • March 2018: The R2.3 billion acquisition of Anglo American's Eskom-tied coal assets completed. Seriti took over the New Vaal, New Denmark, and Kriel collieries — the mines that feed Eskom's Lethabo, Tutuka and Kriel power stations — plus various life-extension coal resources and four closed collieries. This was the single largest transfer of coal assets to black ownership in South Africa's history at the time.
  • June 2021: Seriti acquired South32's South Africa Energy Coal (SAEC) business — the Khutala, Klipspruit and Middelburg mines and related infrastructure — in a deal that made Seriti the largest supplier of coal to Eskom and the biggest black-owned mining company in the country. The acquisition consolidated most of Mpumalanga's domestic coal supply into one majority black-owned producer.
  • 2020s — the energy pivot: Seriti established Seriti Green, its renewable energy arm, to develop wind and solar projects on the company's extensive landholdings in Mpumalanga — the traditional coal heartland. The flagship Ummbila Emoyeni wind project (900 MW+ plan) and the Vunumoya Main Transmission Station, handed over with Eskom in November 2025 and launched in July 2026, position Seriti as a pioneer of the "coal-to-clean" transition in Mpumalanga.
  • 2025-2026 — leadership on coal and transition: Teke chairs FutureCoal (the global coal industry body) while simultaneously championing Seriti's renewable build-out — the core message being that coal remains the bedrock of South African energy security for at least another decade while renewables scale — a position the company calls being "an energy business that produces coal."
Operations — The Mines That Keep Eskom's Lights On
Anglo-acquired core (2018)

New Vaal (Free State/Mpumalanga border): Open-cast mine supplying Eskom's Lethabo power station (one of South Africa's largest).

New Denmark (Mpumalanga): Large underground bord-and-pillar mine feeding Tutuka power station.

Kriel Colliery (Mpumalanga): Open-cast mine dedicated to the Kriel power station. Together with four closed collieries and life-extension resources, these formed the original Seriti asset base.

South32 SAEC assets (2021)

Khutala (Mpumalanga): Key supplier to Eskom's Kendal power station.

Klipspruit & Middelburg (Mpumalanga): Open-cast operations servicing both Eskom (Duvha via Middelburg Mine) and the export market via the Richards Bay Coal Terminal.

Impact: The 2021 acquisition roughly doubled Seriti's coal output, consolidating it as the dominant domestic thermal-coal supplier — the anchor of Eskom's Mpumalanga coal fleet.

⚡ The Eskom relationship

Seriti is the single largest private supplier of coal to Eskom, delivering to five power stations — Kriel, Tutuka, Lethabo, Kendal and Duvha — plus export volumes. Under long-term coal supply agreements (CSA) with Eskom, Seriti's mines are effectively dedicated to the national grid. This makes Seriti's operational reliability — and its financial health — a matter of national energy security. The mines operate on multi-decade life extensions, but they depend on Eskom's continued operation of ageing coal plants and on the terms of the CSAs, which have been the subject of renegotiation as Eskom's financial position changes.

Seriti Green — The Energy Transition Play

Seriti is unusual among coal miners: it is simultaneously the country's largest coal supplier and the most ambitious coal-to-renewables transitioner. The logic is simple — Seriti owns enormous tracts of land in Mpumalanga (sunny, windy, and with existing grid connections used by coal plants that will retire), and it wants to convert a declining coal workforce into a renewable energy workforce.

Ummbila Emoyeni Wind Energy Facility

Seriti Green's flagship project — the first phase of a planned 900 MW+ wind hub near Bethal, Mpumalanga. Launched 31 July 2026, it is one of the largest wind developments in South Africa and the biggest renewable project built in the coal heartland. Seriti is deliberately training former mineworkers for wind and solar roles, presenting the project as a just-transition model that keeps jobs in Mpumalanga.

Vunumoya Main Transmission Station

Handed over with Eskom in a partnership completed in November 2025, the Vunumoya transmission station integrates up to 900 MW of renewable energy onto the national grid. It is a landmark public-private grid investment — the kind of coordinated delivery that makes the transition physically possible — and was celebrated by Teke as "a significant step for South Africa's energy transition and for the future of Mpumalanga as a renewable-energy hub."

Why Seriti Matters for South Africa
  • National energy security: Seriti supplies coal to five Eskom power stations — a large share of the fuel behind the Mpumalanga baseload fleet. Any disruption to Seriti's operations or Eskom's CSA terms immediately threatens grid stability and the still-fragile end of load shedding.
  • Ownership transformation: Seriti is the largest majority black-owned mining company in South Africa, with broad-based community and employee shareholder trusts. It is the flagship outcome of the mining industry's black economic empowerment commitments — a direct response to the historic exclusion of black South Africans from mining ownership.
  • Employment & regional development: Seriti employs tens of thousands directly and indirectly across Mpumalanga and the Free State — communities built around coal. Its "mine-to-mine" and "mine-to-renewables" workforce plans matter enormously for the Just Transition debate.
  • Export earnings: Beyond Eskom supply, Seriti exports thermal coal through Richards Bay Coal Terminal, contributing to South Africa's balance of payments — though export volumes are constrained by Transnet's rail performance.
  • The just-transition test case: Seriti is effectively the experiment for whether a coal company can transition itself and its workforce to renewables without social devastation. Its success or failure will define whether Mpumalanga's coal regions have a post-coal future.
The Future — Seriti's Own Forecast

Seriti's public position, articulated repeatedly by Mike Teke, is a dual-track strategy:

  • Coal remains central for at least another decade: Teke (also FutureCoal chair) argues coal power will remain "undisputed" for at least 10 more years given South Africa's base-load needs and the slow pace of grid and storage build-out. Seriti will keep supplying Eskom while the coal fleet operates — the revenue that funds the transition.
  • Renewables scale up in parallel: The Ummbila Emoyeni wind hub (900 MW+), further solar projects, and the Vunumoya grid connection put Seriti on track to become one of South Africa's largest independent renewable generators, using its own land, grid access and trained workforce.
  • "Energy business, not just a coal miner": The corporate identity shift — from coal miner to energy company producing coal and electrons — is the strategic endgame. The company is positioning to survive and lead well beyond the coal era, converting the very geography of coal into the geography of wind and sun.
