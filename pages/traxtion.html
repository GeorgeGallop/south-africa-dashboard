Traxtion

Africa's Leading Private Rail Operator — Betting R3.4 Billion on the Future of South African Rail

Traxtion is Africa's largest private rail company and the first private operator to commit serious, long-term capital to South Africa's open-access rail reform. It is building a mainland freight rail business from scratch — importing, refurbishing and upgrading locomotives and wagons, raising R1.5 billion in equity from South African institutional investors, and positioning itself to run container trains on the vital Durban–Gauteng corridor under third-party access to Transnet's network. With the first eight Wabtec C28 locomotives arriving in Durban in August 2026, Traxtion is the clearest proof yet that the reforms underpinning the Transnet Rail Infrastructure Manager (TRIM) are attracting real private investment — and that the "rail of the future" the user read about is no longer a policy paper, but locomotives on the docks.

History & Origins
  • African rail roots: Traxtion grew out of a pan-African rail services group with a long track record of supplying locomotives, wagons, and rail maintenance services to mining, agricultural and industrial customers across Africa — from West Africa to East and Southern Africa. Its South African presence centres on the Traxtion Rail Services Hub in Pretoria, a heavy-maintenance and refurbishment facility.
  • The pilot era (2022): Traxtion (through Traxtion Sheltam joint-venture) was selected in 2022 for one of Transnet's first pilot third-party access awards — the Kroonstad–East London corridor on the Cape corridor. The award was ultimately cancelled (announced October 2023) because negotiations were too protracted and both parties chose to align with the broader reform programme instead. The cancellation, far from a setback, positioned Traxtion to enter properly once the Network Statement and open access were formalised.
  • 2023-2025 — positioning for open access: As Transnet established the Interim Rail Infrastructure Manager (TRIM) (November 2023) and published the final Network Statement (December 2024), Traxtion moved aggressively: it raised capital, imported 40 locomotives ahead of schedule, secured access for general freight on the Durban–Gauteng corridor, and began planning container trains between City Deep (Johannesburg) and the Durban Container Terminal.
  • December 2025 — the R3.4 billion commitment: Traxtion announced its R3.4 billion rolling stock investment programme — the largest private freight rail investment in South African history in fleet size and value — and said it expected to begin operating on the mainline by the third quarter of 2026. The programme comprises ~R1.8 billion in locomotives and ~R1.6 billion in wagons, with a minimum 60% local content target and 662 direct jobs projected during build and deployment.
  • May 2026 — R1.5 billion equity close: Traxtion closed a R1.5 billion equity subscription underwritten by Harith, Stanlib and Standard Bank — an emphatic vote of confidence by South African institutional capital in both Traxtion and the rail reform programme.
  • August 2026 — first locomotives land: The first eight Wabtec C28 locomotives arrived at the Port of Durban, shipped from New Zealand, marking the first of four planned shipments that will see 46 locomotives introduced over the next two years. They will be transported to the Pretoria hub, with refurbishment and upgrades — including new-generation 3,300 hp Wabtec engines — scheduled to begin in September 2026 before entering commercial service.
The R3.4 Billion Programme — What Traxtion Is Building
🚂 46 Locomotives (~R1.8 billion)

Four shipments over two years of Wabtec C28 locomotives, acquired from New Zealand and refurbished/upgraded at Traxtion's Pretoria hub with new-generation 3,300 hp Wabtec engines (reducing emissions versus older units).

First shipment: 8 units arrived Port of Durban, August 2026; refurbishment from September 2026.

🚃 920 Wagons (~R1.6 billion)

A planned acquisition of 920 wagons to complement the locomotive fleet — the rolling stock needed to move containerised and general freight at scale on the mainline network, including the capacity to haul roughly 4.5 million tonnes per year of freight once fully deployed.

💼 Local Content & Jobs

78% local procurement, a 60% local content target, and 665 new permanent, highly skilled jobs — the investment is explicitly designed to build South African engineering capability, refurbishment expertise and skilled employment, not just to import trains.

💵 The Funding Stack

R1.5 billion equity closed May 2026 (underwritten by Harith, Stanlib and Standard Bank). As CEO James Holley puts it: policy reform provides the framework, but "equity investors into the sector need certainty which encourages long-term commitments" — and once equity is raised, "debt capital markets open up." The model: equity first, then debt, then the rolling stock itself.

Why Traxtion Matters — The "Rail of the Future"
  • The first-mover proof point: Traxtion's arrival is the single most important demonstration that South Africa's rail reform is real. For years, private operators were promised open access; Traxtion has now raised the capital, bought the trains, and put locomotives on the docks. Every other potential entrant (mining majors like Anglo, Kumba, South32; logistics groups) watches Traxtion's entry as the proof-of-concept that determines whether they commit their own capital.
  • It validates the TRIM / open-access architecture: The Traxtion investment only makes sense because TRIM now exists as a neutral infrastructure manager that must allocate train paths non-discriminatorily, and because the Network Statement provides the access certainty that equity and debt investors demand. Traxtion and TRIM are two sides of the same reform coin.
  • Shifting freight off the N3: Traxtion's planned container trains between City Deep and the Durban Container Terminal directly target the ~8,000 trucks per day on the N3 highway. Every train of containers shifted to rail reduces road damage, congestion, emissions and logistics costs — the modal-shift objective at the heart of the National Rail Policy.
  • Capacity for the economy: Government's ambition is to lift annual freight rail volumes from ~160 million tonnes to ~250 million tonnes through public-private collaboration. Traxtion's fleet alone will contribute capacity equivalent to ~4.5 million tonnes — a meaningful step, and a demonstration of the "scale of the opportunity that remains."
  • Competitive pressure on Transnet: With a private operator on the network, Transnet Freight Rail can no longer be the only game in town. The competitive pressure — and the visible comparison on service, cost and reliability that TRIM's access regime enables — is exactly what the reform was designed to create.
The Road Ahead — What Traxtion's Own Words Tell Us

CEO James Holley's public statements (December 2025 – August 2026) map the company's outlook:

  • "The future of freight depends on collaboration between government and the private sector…" — Traxtion frames itself as a partner to, not replacement of, Transnet. Its message is additive capacity, not head-to-head war with the state operator.
  • "…backed by the kind of long-term investment needed to build capacity and unlock economic opportunity." — The R3.4bn is explicitly framed as a multiplier: jobs, supply chains, engineering capability.
  • "While policy reform provides the framework… infrastructure investment is what enables that vision to become an operational reality." — Traxtion's consistent argument: reform without capital is just a document; Traxtion is the capital.
  • "The next version of the Network Statement is expected to be an important step towards creating that certainty." — Version 4 of the Network Statement (expected 2026-2027) is the regulatory catalyst Traxtion and other investors are waiting for to commit even larger sums.
The bottom line: Traxtion is the private-sector answer to the question "will anyone actually invest in the new South African rail?" — and the answer, so far, is yes, to the tune of R3.4 billion. The next milestones to watch: the first commercial mainline trains (targeted Q3 2026), the arrival of the remaining locomotive shipments, the wagons order, and the version-4 Network Statement that will shape the next wave of investment.
