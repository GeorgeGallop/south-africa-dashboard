Province profile

Western Cape

The Western Cape combines the economic strength of Cape Town and the winelands with a Mediterranean climate, strong tourism appeal, and a diversified export-oriented economy.

At a glance

  • Capital: Cape Town
  • Population: About 7.4 million
  • Largest city: Cape Town
  • Known for: Tourism, wine, technology, and trade

Population

The Western Cape has around 7.4 million residents. Growth is concentrated in the Cape Town metro, surrounding towns, and the productive agricultural regions of the Boland and Overberg.

Brief history

The province grew from the Cape Colony and later developed into a major commercial and cultural centre, shaped by maritime trade, colonial history, and long-standing anti-apartheid activism.

Key industries

Tourism, wine production, agriculture, financial services, film, technology, and port logistics are major contributors to the provincial economy.

Major cities & towns

Cape Town, Stellenbosch, Paarl, George, Worcester, Somerset West, and Mossel Bay are key urban and regional hubs.

Climate

The province has a Mediterranean climate along the southwest coast, with wet winters and dry summers. Inland areas are generally warmer and drier, especially moving toward the Karoo.

Natural resources

Fertile soils, vineyards, fisheries, scenic coastlines, mountain catchments, and wind and solar potential support the province’s diverse economy.

Future outlook

The Western Cape is positioned for growth in green energy, export agriculture, tourism, digital services, and logistics, with continued investment in water resilience and transport infrastructure.
