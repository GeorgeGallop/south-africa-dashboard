🌐 SADC Regional Gas Master Plan — Phase 1

DBSA-sponsored draft for the Southern African Development Community. Consolidated report, 2020.

This is the regional counterpart to South Africa’s national Gas Master Plan. The 37th and 38th SADC Summits (2017–2018) directed the Secretariat to operationalise a Regional Gas Committee and write a Regional Gas Master Plan (RGMP). Phase 1 is the supply, demand, infrastructure, and institutional study — 289 pages, a 30-year horizon, and a map of how Mozambique, Angola, Tanzania, Namibia, and South Africa could become one gas market rather than a set of stranded national projects.

Why a regional plan

SADC member states need energy that is secure, available, and affordable — and a transition fuel that can also be a chemical feedstock (ammonia/urea, methanol, GTL diesel) and a transport fuel. Country-level volumes are often too small to FID midstream infrastructure on their own. The RGMP’s answer is demand aggregation across geographic nodes, plus LNG as the short-term stimulus while pipelines and domestic fields catch up.

Enablement is framed as molecule access and affordability; infrastructure tariffs, third-party access, and regional aggregation; and security of supply from multiple sources (LNG plus domestic). The study also scores capital, policy, institutions, skills, and energy-system structure in each member state.

Supply and demand (Phase 1 numbers)

Supply

Mozambique sits in front with more than 100 tcf proven. Current production (Mozambique, Angola, Tanzania, South Africa) was about 9.6 bcm / 360 PJ/a. The study then forecast production to ~50 bcm (~2,035 PJ/a) by 2026, driven mainly by LNG export trains. Angola was the only SADC LNG producer at 5.2 mtpa; regional liquefaction was expected to reach 36.7 mtpa once Mozambique Area 1 and Area 4 trains come on. Most of that cargo is committed to international markets; domestic allocations would have to be traded as molecules or as downstream products (power, fertiliser).

Demand

Forecasts cover ten SADC states plus Ethiopia and Kenya. South Africa is the region’s largest consumer and the outlier that already imports via ROMPCO. Base case: ~400 PJ/a of gas-to-power by 2030 (about half of non-petrochemical demand) as the anchor, then total aggregated demand ~1,427 PJ/a by 2050. High case is ~41% above base (Sasol coal-to-liquids switching, more GTP, three urea plants). Low case is ~30% below (no methanol plant, one urea plant, weaker GTP after 2030).

Infrastructure: hub-and-spoke first, pipelines later

Regional gas infrastructure was described as nascent. Short-term stimulus is imported LNG into planned or discussed terminals at Maputo, Coega, Richards Bay, Saldanha, Walvis Bay, and Port Louis. The preferred near-term architecture is a hub-and-spoke: conventional LNG into an FSRU, then small-scale LNG by ship, road, or rail as a virtual pipeline until volumes justify a conventional terminal and steel pipe.

Existing backbone: ROMPCO plus Sasol and Transnet Lilly in South Africa — the base for linking Richards Bay / Maputo LNG into Gauteng, KZN, and Mpumalanga. Longer term: a ~2,600 km Mozambique north–south line from Rovuma to Temane/ROMPCO; Mtwara–Dar extended inland and possibly toward Kenya; and a west-coast link from producing sites through Botswana and Namibia toward Angola.

Power overlay: ~8,800 MW of planned new gas-to-power in the region, plus ~14,000 MW of existing plant that could switch to gas, including ~5,500 MW of Mpumalanga coal (Camden, Komati, Grootvlei) as a fast-track CCGT offtake that would justify a conventional LNG train and ROMPCO looping. SAPP still needs Angola, Malawi, and Tanzania connected.

South Africa route-to-market in the report
  • Loop ROMPCO to support new Mpumalanga gas-to-power demand.
  • Import LNG first at Coega, then Richards Bay, where coastal GTP sits — complement with pipeline, including possible reversal of the Lilly line into Mpumalanga.
  • Connect the existing grid toward Mozambique Rovuma over the longer term.

Recommendations

Regional

Harmonise energy law and policy; build east, west, and northern economic corridors; extend SAPP and relieve congestion; create a wholesale aggregator modelled on SAPP brokerage, and eventually a physical gas hub with third-party access. Transform RERA into SARERA with powers over cross-border energy trade and tariffs. The report also floats a Southern African Gas Pool (SAGP) and a Regional Petroleum and Gas Association (REPGA), with a warning not to overlap mandates.

National and market

Adopt SADC Model Energy Guidelines into national IEPs. Protect investors without abandoning state interest. Build gas skills at existing regional training centres. Policy and institutional mandates first, so aggregation can actually happen; GTP and petrochemicals as the first affordable anchors; LNG where volumes do not yet justify pipe; LPG and small-scale LNG for access in thinner markets.
