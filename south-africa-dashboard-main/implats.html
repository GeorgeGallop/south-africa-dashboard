Impala Platinum Holdings Ltd (Implats)

One of the World's Largest Primary Producers of Platinum Group Metals — Integrated Mining, Smelting & Refining

Implats is a leading global producer of platinum group metals (PGMs), with mining, smelting, and refining operations centred in South Africa's Bushveld Igneous Complex and Zimbabwe's Great Dyke. The company's operations include the Impala Rustenburg mine (the world's second-largest platinum mine), the Zimplats operation in Zimbabwe, and Marula Platinum in Limpopo. Implats also owns the Impala Refinery in Springs (Gauteng) — one of the largest PGM refineries in the world.

History & Origins
  • 1969: Impala Platinum was founded to develop PGM mining operations on the western limb of the Bushveld Igneous Complex, near Rustenburg. The Rustenburg mine (often called "Impala Rustenburg") began production in the 1970s and rapidly grew into one of the world's largest platinum mines.
  • 1980s–1990s: Implats expanded through the development of new mines and the construction of the Impala Refinery in Springs (Gauteng), which processes PGM concentrates into refined platinum, palladium, rhodium, and other precious metals. The refinery also treats concentrates from third-party miners.
  • 2000s: Expansion into Zimbabwe through Zimplats (the largest platinum mine in Zimbabwe) and the development of Marula Platinum in Limpopo. Implats also acquired a stake in the Two Rivers mine (joint venture with African Rainbow Minerals).
  • 2010s: Implats faced margin pressure from rising costs (electricity, labour, and deep-level mining complexity) and PGM price volatility. The company restructured, closing its loss-making deep-level shafts at Rustenburg and focusing on higher-grade ore bodies.
  • 2023: Implats completed the acquisition of the remaining shares in the Royal Bafokeng Platinum (RBPlat) mine, consolidating its position on the western limb of the Bushveld. The acquisition was valued at ~R27 billion and made Implats the single largest operator on the Rustenburg platinum belt.
Mining, Smelting & Refining Operations
Impala Rustenburg (North West)

Type: Underground platinum mine — the second-largest in the world. Multiple shafts accessing the Merensky Reef and UG2 Reef.

Output: ~1.4 million ounces of refined PGM per year (total group).

Role: The flagship operation. Ore is processed through the company's concentrators, then smelted at Impala's own smelter complex near Rustenburg before being sent to the refinery in Springs.

Royal Bafokeng Platinum (RBPlat)

Location: Adjacent to Impala Rustenburg, on the western limb.

Assets: Bafokeng Rasimone mine and Styldrift mine — high-quality, long-life PGM reserves.

Acquisition: Implats acquired 100% of RBPlat by 2023, adding ~300,000 ounces of annual PGM production and extending the life of its Rustenburg operations into the 2050s.

Zimplats (Zimbabwe)

Location: Selous, Zimbabwe — on the Great Dyke geological formation.

Operations: Two underground mines (Ngwarati, Rukodzi), a concentrator, and a smelter.

Output: ~300,000 ounces of PGM per year. Implats holds an 87% interest. Zimplats is a focus of expansion, with plans to increase output and build a dedicated base metals refinery.

Impala Refinery (Springs, Gauteng)

Type: Precious metals refinery — one of the largest and most sophisticated in the world.

Process: Receives PGM matte from the Rustenburg smelter, Zimplats, and third-party sources, and separates it into pure platinum, palladium, rhodium, iridium, ruthenium, and osmium. Also recovers nickel and copper as by-products.

Role: The refinery produces "Good Delivery" certified bars and sponge metal that are sold to automotive catalyst manufacturers, jewellery, industrial, and investment markets globally.

Why Implats Matters for South Africa
  • PGM dominance: South Africa holds >90% of the world's platinum reserves and >70% of palladium and rhodium reserves. Implats, together with Anglo American Platinum (Amplats) and Sibanye-Stillwater, controls the bulk of global PGM supply. These metals are essential for automotive catalytic converters (which reduce vehicle emissions), hydrogen electrolysers, fuel cells, jewellery, and industrial catalysts.
  • Downstream processing: Implats' refinery in Springs represents the final, highest-value step in the PGM value chain. If South Africa did not have its own refining capacity, it would export unrefined matte and lose the value-add and employment — the refinery keeps billions of rand of processing value in the country.
  • Employment: ~60,000 employees (direct and contract) across Rustenburg, Springs, and Zimplats. The Rustenburg area is heavily dependent on PGM mining for economic activity.
  • Energy transition metals: Platinum is essential for proton exchange membrane (PEM) hydrogen electrolysers and fuel cells, positioning Implats to benefit from the green hydrogen economy. The company is actively working on technology to reduce the platinum loading in electrolysers while maintaining performance.
  • Export revenue: PGM exports are South Africa's largest mineral export category by value (consistently exceeding coal and gold in recent years).
