🏴 The Patriotic Alliance

A new force in South African politics: from prison yards to cabinet — the rapid rise of the Patriotic Alliance.

📚 Gayton McKenzie, 2024:
“The PA is not a party of politicians. We are a party of the people — the forgotten people, the marginalised, the coloured communities, the prison inmates, the poor. We speak for those who have been left behind by the ANC and the DA.”

The Patriotic Alliance (PA) is one of South Africa's youngest and fastest-growing political parties. Founded in 2013 by former gangsters and ex-convicts Gayton McKenzie and Kenny Kunene, the PA has carved out a distinct niche in South African politics — combining populist rhetoric with a focus on prison reform, coloured community advocacy, and anti-establishment politics. In the 2024 general election, the PA surprised many by winning approximately 2% of the national vote and entering government as part of the Government of National Unity, with McKenzie appointed Minister of Sports, Arts and Culture.

📜 Founding (2013) — From Prison to Politics
2013 — PA Founded

The Patriotic Alliance is founded by Gayton McKenzie and Kenny Kunene, two former prisoners who met while incarcerated. McKenzie served 17 years for armed robbery and bank robbery; Kunene served time for fraud. After their release, both became successful businessmen and writers — McKenzie authored a memoir and became a motivational speaker. The PA is launched with a populist, anti-establishment platform targeting marginalised coloured communities and ex-offenders. [PA Official]

2016 — First Electoral Contest

The PA contests its first local government elections, winning a handful of seats in the Western Cape and Gauteng, particularly in coloured-majority areas. The party gains attention for its unconventional campaign style and McKenzie's charismatic, streetwise oratory. [IEC]

📖 Ideology — Populism, Prison Reform & Community Advocacy

The PA defies easy left-right categorisation. Its ideology blends:

  • Populism: The PA presents itself as the voice of the "forgotten" South Africans — coloured communities in the Cape, ex-offenders, and the urban poor. It uses direct, often confrontational language to criticise both the ANC (for corruption and incompetence) and the DA (for being elitist and disconnected from coloured voters).
  • Prison Reform: The PA's flagship issue. McKenzie and Kunene draw on their own experiences to advocate for better prison conditions, rehabilitation programmes, and the removal of criminal records for non-violent offenders to improve employment prospects. The party calls for the prison system to be transformed from a punitive institution into a rehabilitative one. [PA Policy]
  • Coloured Community Advocacy: The PA has positioned itself as the political champion of South Africa's coloured (mixed-race) population, particularly in the Western Cape and Northern Cape. It argues that coloured South Africans have been marginalised by both the ANC's race-based policies and the DA's perceived focus on white interests.
  • Economic Nationalism: The PA supports localisation, protection for small businesses, and tougher immigration controls. McKenzie has been outspoken about illegal immigration, arguing that undocumented foreigners are taking jobs from South Africans.
  • Tough on Crime: Despite its focus on prison reform, the PA supports the death penalty for the most serious crimes and advocates for harsher sentences for those who commit crimes against women and children.
🧐 Leadership — Gayton McKenzie & Kenny Kunene
Gayton McKenzie — President

Born in 1974 in the Western Cape, McKenzie served 17 years in prison for armed robbery. While incarcerated, he earned a degree and wrote a memoir, The Choice, which became a bestseller. After his release, he became a successful businessman and motivational speaker. McKenzie is the PA's founding president and public face — a charismatic, polarising figure known for his blunt speaking style, sharp suits, and ability to connect with audiences at the grassroots level. In 2024, he was appointed Minister of Sports, Arts and Culture in the GNU.

Kenny Kunene — Deputy President

Born in 1971, Kunene is a former fraud convict who turned his life around to become a successful businessman and restaurateur. He gained notoriety in 2010 for eating a R500 sushi platter at a birthday party — earning the nickname "Sushi King" — which he later said was a deliberate provocation to highlight the absurdity of conspicuous consumption in a poor country. Kunene serves as the PA's deputy president and campaign strategist, known for his sharp analysis and media savvy.

📈 Electoral Performance (2019–2024)
2019 — National Debut: 0.4%

The PA contests its first general election, winning 0.4% of the national vote — approximately 26,000 votes. The party falls short of winning a seat in the National Assembly (the threshold is effectively ~0.5% for a first seat). However, the party establishes a foothold in the Western Cape provincial legislature, winning one seat. [IEC]

2021 — Local Government Breakthrough

The PA makes significant gains in the 2021 local elections, winning over 30 municipal council seats nationally, mainly in the Western Cape. The party becomes the third-largest party in several Western Cape municipalities and enters coalition agreements in multiple councils. [IEC]

2024 — National Breakthrough: ~2%

The PA wins an estimated 2% of the national vote, securing approximately 330,000 votes and 9 seats in the National Assembly. The party's support is concentrated in the Western Cape (where it wins ~7% and becomes the official opposition in the provincial legislature), Northern Cape, and Gauteng. The result makes the PA one of the fastest-growing parties in South Africa. [IEC]

🤝 2024 GNU Role — McKenzie Becomes Minister
📚 Gayton McKenzie, on being appointed Minister of Sports, Arts and Culture, 2024:
“I stand here today as living proof that South Africa is a country of second chances. From a prison cell to the cabinet — this is the promise of our democracy. I will never forget where I come from, and I will never abandon the people who made this possible.”

Following the ANC's loss of its majority in the 2024 election, the Patriotic Alliance joined the Government of National Unity. Gayton McKenzie, as the party's leader, was appointed Minister of Sports, Arts and Culture in President Cyril Ramaphosa's cabinet — a portfolio that oversees sport development, the arts, heritage, cultural institutions, and the national lottery.

McKenzie's appointment was widely seen as one of the more surprising and symbolically significant moments in the GNU's formation. A former armed robber now responsible for South Africa's cultural and sporting life became a powerful narrative of redemption. The PA also secured several key municipal positions and deputy minister roles as part of the GNU coalition agreement.

The PA's participation in the GNU has been controversial within the party's base: some supporters see it as a betrayal of the PA's anti-establishment identity, while McKenzie argues that being inside government is the only way to deliver real change to the communities the party represents.

📖 Key Policies
  • Prison Reform: Transform prisons into rehabilitative institutions. Remove criminal records for non-violent offenders after sentence completion. Improve education and vocational training in prisons. Employ more ex-offenders in government and state-owned enterprises. [PA Policy]
  • Coloured Community Empowerment: Establish a dedicated Coloured Development Agency to address economic marginalisation. Ensure proportional representation of coloured South Africans in government and public institutions. Prioritise coloured-majority areas for infrastructure and social spending. [PA Policy]
  • Immigration Control: Strict enforcement of immigration laws. Deportation of undocumented foreigners. Prioritise South African citizens for jobs and business opportunities. The party argues that uncontrolled immigration has depressed wages and strained public services. [PA Policy]
  • Crime and Safety: Restore the death penalty for the most serious crimes (rape, murder of women and children). Increase police presence in townships and coloured communities. Fast-track courts for violent crime cases. [PA Policy]
  • Economic Empowerment: Support for small and medium enterprises (SMEs). Localisation and "buy South African" policies. Tougher penalties for tender fraud and corruption. Lower taxes for businesses that employ ex-offenders. [PA Policy]
  • Social Welfare: Expand the social grant system while linking it to skills training and job-seeking requirements. Introduce a universal basic income grant — the PA has expressed support for the idea while insisting it be funded by cutting wasteful government expenditure. [PA Policy]
