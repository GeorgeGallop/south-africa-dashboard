📖 About Power Futures Lab

The Power Futures Lab (PFL) is based at the University of Cape Town's Graduate School of Business. Founded in 2001 by Professor Anton Eberhard, it began as the Management Programme in Infrastructure Reform and Regulation (MIR), focused on building regulatory capacity in Africa's newly liberalising power sectors.

Over 25 years, PFL has grown into Africa's preeminent academic research centre on power sector reform, regulation, investment, and the energy transition. Its work spans 30+ African countries, producing over 170 publications covering independent power projects (IPPs), renewable energy auctions, utility performance, electricity market design, and the political economy of power sector reform.

🎯 Core Research Areas

  • Power Sector Reform & Regulation — Hybrid markets, unbundling, institutional design
  • Independent Power Projects (IPPs) — Investment trends, procurement, risk mitigation
  • Renewable Energy Auctions — Design, performance, derisking in Sub-Saharan Africa
  • Utility Performance — Governance, structural incentives, capability analysis
  • Energy Access & Electrification — Rural electrification, universal access strategies
  • Political Economy — State capture, policy failure, governance of SOEs

📜 How PFL's Thinking Has Evolved (2001–2026)

2001–2005
Foundation: Building Regulatory Capacity. PFL began as MIR, training African regulators. Early research focused on the standard model of power sector reform — unbundling, independent regulation, and private participation. Seminal work on South Africa's electrification programme and the emergence of IPPs in Africa.
2006–2010
IPP Deep Dives. Major comparative research on IPP experiences across Africa (Kenya, Tanzania, Uganda, Nigeria, Egypt, Morocco, Tunisia). Eberhard's work established that IPPs could work in Africa but required strong regulatory frameworks and government commitment. Key insight: energy security crises drive IPP adoption more than ideology.
2011–2015
Renewable Energy & Auctions. As costs fell dramatically, PFL shifted focus to renewable energy procurement. Studied South Africa's REIPPPP programme as a global model. Research showed competitive auctions could deliver clean energy at lower cost than feed-in tariffs. Comparative work across Sub-Saharan Africa.
2016–2020
Hybrid Markets & Political Economy. Recognition that the "standard model" of power reform had failed in most African countries. PFL pioneered research on hybrid power markets — where state utilities and private producers coexist. Eberhard's influential commentary on Eskom's crisis, state capture, and the urgent need for unbundling. Research on utility performance and governance failures.
2021–2024
Just Energy Transition & SAWEM. PFL rebranded and expanded into just transition research. Deep analysis of South Africa's new electricity market (SAWEM). Focus on how to attract private investment while protecting public interest. Growing work on the intersection of energy access, economic development, and climate goals.
2025–2026
Future-Facing: Investment & Reform. Latest research tackles the Africa Power Investment Landscape, mitigation of private sector risks, SAWEM implications for municipalities, and the role of carbon taxation in South Africa's energy future. PFL continues to be the authoritative voice on African power sector reform.

⭐ Most Significant Publications

📘 Independent Power Projects in Sub-Saharan Africa (2016)

Landmark study co-authored by Eberhard, Gratwick and Kariuki. Analysed 20+ IPP experiences across Africa, establishing the conditions under which private power investment succeeds. "The definitive reference on African IPPs."

📗 South Africa's Renewable Energy IPP Procurement Programme (REIPPPP)

Multiple PFL publications documenting how SA's auction programme became the world's most successful renewable energy procurement model, delivering 6,000+ MW of clean energy at falling prices.

📙 Hybrid Power Markets in Africa (2018–2020)

Eberhard's influential research documenting the emergence of hybrid markets — where unbundled IPPs coexist with state-owned utilities — as the dominant model across Africa, challenging the orthodox "standard model" of power reform.

📕 Speaking Truth to Power (2023)

Eberhard's collected commentaries documenting South Africa's electricity crisis, the failures of Eskom governance, state capture in the energy sector, and the path to reform through unbundling and private investment.

📒 Africa Needs Context-Relevant Evidence to Shape Its Clean Energy Future (2023, Nature Energy)

Published in the world's leading energy journal, this article argued that Africa's clean energy transition requires locally-grounded research, not imported policy prescriptions from developed countries.

📓 Kenya's Lessons from Two Decades of Power Sector Reform

Longitudinal study of Kenya's experience — one of Africa's most ambitious power reformers — highlighting what worked (IPP framework, geothermal development) and what didn't (utility governance challenges).
