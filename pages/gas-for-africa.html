The imbalance

Africa’s per-capita energy supply has been stagnant for about 40 years. The continent is the least energised in the world, yet holds one of the larger proven gas endowments. OPEC ASB 2024 and the Energy Institute Statistical Review figures used in the report:

Africa (2024)Share / note
Proven reserves17,221 Bcm~8%+ of global proven reserves
Marketed production246.71 Bcm~6% of global production
Consumption178 BcmMost of the rest is exported

African gas use is also skewed: 48.6% goes to power, versus a much larger industrial share in Europe, Asia, and South America. The missed opportunity is industry, mining, transport, fertiliser, and households — not only gas-to-power.

Three action fronts

1. Financial innovation

Tailored instruments, public–private partnerships, and domestic capital from pension funds, sovereign wealth funds, and local markets — not only DFIs and export-credit agencies.

2. Infrastructure expansion

Shift from a sole focus on large-scale export LNG to small- and mid-scale systems that serve local industry, urban centres, clean cooking, and regional energy trade.

3. Policy reform

Clearer regulation, flexible pricing, modernised gas planning, and alignment between energy, industrial, and climate objectives.

South Africa in the report

Hawilti’s pipeline-network snapshot (Figure 3) puts South Africa at 934 km of transmission and 1.4 thousand km of distribution — a thin grid next to Algeria (24.6k / 170.7k km) or Egypt (8.3k / 95.7k km), and comparable in transmission length to Tanzania (793 km) and Mozambique (531 km).

The appendix includes a ROMPCO case study on the Mozambique–Secunda pipeline as a working model of cross-border gas trade and regional industrial offtake — the same corridor the IGUA-SA Gas Roadmap treats as the only short-term LNG route into South Africa.

The report also flags gas as a coal-displacement and renewables-balancing fuel in South Africa, Senegal, and Morocco, and notes new domestic infrastructure under construction in Senegal, Ghana, Nigeria, and South Africa.

What’s in the rest of the book

  1. Africa’s energy context — beyond household electrification; new discoveries; why penetration stays low; pricing; regional dynamics.
  2. Opportunities — local monetisation, intra-African trade, and decarbonisation (including flaring and methane).
  3. Realising the potential — unlocking finance, fit-for-purpose gasification infrastructure, pragmatic policy.
  4. Roadmap and appendix case studies: ROMPCO, Seplat flare elimination, CNG in Nigeria and Tanzania, Eni’s technology and development approach.
