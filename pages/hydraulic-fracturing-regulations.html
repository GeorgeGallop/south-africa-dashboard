🪨 Hydraulic Fracturing Regulations (2022)

Proposed Regulations for the Exploration & Production of Onshore Oil and Gas Requiring Hydraulic Fracturing

These proposed regulations, published for public consultation in July 2022, would create the environmental rulebook for any future hydraulic fracturing ("fracking") of onshore oil and gas in South Africa — most significantly in the Karoo. They establish prohibited activities, a phased authorisation process, and monitoring requirements, and were prepared under the National Environmental Management Act (NEMA).

Why These Regulations Were Needed

The publication came four years after the Supreme Court of Appeal set aside the earlier Regulations for Petroleum Exploration and Production. The court found that those regulations had been managing environmental impacts — but only the Minister responsible for the environment is empowered to make regulations on environmental matters. The earlier rules were therefore invalid, leaving a regulatory vacuum for fracking.

These new regulations close that gap by being issued by the Minister of Forestry, Fisheries and the Environment under NEMA, the correct legal authority for environmental regulation.

Why this matters: Without a valid environmental framework, no commercial fracking could be responsibly authorised. These regulations are the environmental gateway that any Karoo shale gas project would have to pass through — alongside the Gas Act and the MPRDA.

What the Regulations Do — Key Provisions

1. Prohibited Activities & Areas

The Regulations identify specific prohibited activities and prohibited geographic areas for the use of hydraulic fracturing technology — defining where fracking may never take place.

2. No Potable Water for Fracking

The use of potable water (drinking-quality water) for hydraulic fracturing activities is prohibited — a critical constraint in the water-scarce Karoo.

3. No Municipal Waste Disposal

The use of municipal water treatment facilities for the disposal of wastes from hydraulic fracturing operations is prohibited.

4. Phased Environmental Authorisation

An environmental authorisation is required for each phase: seismic survey (without fracking), the hydraulic fracturing phase, and the production phase.

5. Ongoing Monitoring

General and specific requirements are set for ongoing environmental monitoring of hydraulic fracturing and production operations.

6. Inter-Authority Coordination

Effect is given to coordination between decision-making authorities regarding monitoring, environmental assessments, timeframes, conditions of approval, and the decision-making process.

The Step-Wise / Phased Approach

The regulation requires a separate environmental authorisation at each stage of an operation. This deliberate design ensures that information generated at one phase informs the next, and allows for the consideration of cumulative impacts across the whole lifecycle.

1

Seismic Survey

Exploration geophysics without fracking — establishes baseline understanding of the resource.

2

Hydraulic Fracturing

The actual stimulation of the well — the phase with the greatest environmental controversy.

3

Production Phase

Ongoing extraction, subject to continuous monitoring and compliance.

Minimum Information Requirements (MIR)

The Regulations are supported by two Minimum Information Requirements (MIR) documents designed to guide developers and create a "one-stop authorisation process" by consolidating approval requirements across all decision-making authorities.

MIR 1 — Exploration (published May 2022)

"Minimum Requirements for the Submission of Applications for an Authorisation, Right, Permit or Licence for the Onshore Exploration of Oil and Gas Intending to Utilise Hydraulic Fracturing" (Revision 0, May 2022). Covers exploration that anticipates fracking.

MIR 2 — Exploration & Production (to be prepared)

"Minimum Information Requirement for the Submission of Applications for the Authorisation, Right, Permit or Licence for Onshore Exploration and Production of Oil and Gas Utilising Hydraulic Fracturing." Deals with the actual impacts of fracking.

  • Consolidate the authorisation requirements of all decision-making authorities into one guidance document.
  • Facilitate combining information into one consolidated impact assessment report and baseline monitoring plan.
  • Facilitate simultaneous application submission and simultaneous decision-making.
