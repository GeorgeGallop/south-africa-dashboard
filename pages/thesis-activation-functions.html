∿ Activation Functions

The non-linearity that makes a neural network a neural network — and not just a stacked linear regression. Told through the temperature–load curve every South African day-ahead forecast has to bend around.

🍳 This Is the Sauce — Non-Linearity Is the Whole Method
💬 Layman's Introduction

A neuron does two things, in this order:

z = w · x + b    →    a = σ(z)

The first step is a weighted sum. That is ordinary arithmetic: “add 400 MW per degree below 18 °C.” If that were the whole network, you would have invented linear regression and called it a day. ARIMA desks already had that.

The second step, σ, is the activation function. It is a bend. It takes the straight line the weighted sum just drew and kinks it, saturates it, or clips it. Stack those bends and you can draw a U-shaped heating/cooling response, an evening ramp, a holiday rundown — anything continuous. Remove σ and the deepest network in the world collapses back into one matrix multiply. The weights are the ingredients. The activation is the sauce. Without it, there is no dish.

This is not a flourish. It is a theorem. Cybenko (1989) and Hornik (1991): a feedforward net with one hidden layer and a non-linear activation can approximate any continuous function on a compact set, to any accuracy, if you give it enough hidden units. The theorem is silent if σ is linear. Linearity has no approximating power beyond a hyperplane. Load is not a hyperplane.

Weighted sum
The mixer

Combines temperature, hour, last-week load into one number z. Necessary. Not sufficient.

Activation σ
The bend

Turns z into a shape the next layer can compose. This is where capacity comes from.

Stacking
The composition

Bends of bends. A daily load curve is a composition of kinks, not a single slope.

■ Linear Collapse — Why Depth Without σ Is Theatre

Take two layers and drop the activation. Layer 1 computes W1x + b1. Layer 2 computes W2(that) + b2. Substitute:

y = W2(W1x + b1) + b2
y = (W2 W1) x + (W2 b1 + b2)
y = W' x + b'

Two layers, one line. A hundred layers, still one line. You paid for depth and bought a spreadsheet. That is the collapse. It is why every serious network inserts a non-linear σ after each hidden weighted sum:

h1 = σ(W1x + b1)     h2 = σ(W2h1 + b2)     y = W3h2 + b3

Now W2 is multiplying a bent version of x, not x itself. The composition no longer factors. That is the entire difference between “a neural network” and “linear regression with extra steps.” The output layer is often left linear on purpose — you want unbounded megawatts, not a squashed probability. The sauce lives in the hidden layers.

■ The Load U-Shape — The Curve a Line Cannot Draw

South African system load versus temperature is the textbook reason you need a bend. Cold Highveld evenings: heating load rises as temperature falls. Hot January afternoons: air-conditioning rises as temperature rises. In between, a dead band. That is a U, not a slope.

A linear net is forced to pick one slope and live with it. A hidden layer of ReLU units can specialise: one neuron learns max(0, 16 − T) (heating), another learns max(0, T − 24) (cooling), the output mixes them into MW. Toggle the model below. The dots are an illustrative evening-peak response, not a live Eskom series.

What those two ReLU neurons actually compute
hheat = ReLU(16 − T) = max(0, 16 − T)
hcool = ReLU(T − 24) = max(0, T − 24)
D̂ = 28 000 + 420 · hheat + 480 · hcool   (MW, illustrative)

That is the sauce in one picture: the max(0, ·) is the non-linearity. Without it, heating and cooling would cancel into a single slope and the dead band would disappear. With it, each neuron is silent on the side of 18 °C it does not own, and the U appears for free.

■ Plot the Functions — Shape and Slope

Two plots matter. The left is σ(z) — what the neuron emits. The right is σ'(z) — the local slope that back-propagation multiplies through. If that slope is near zero, the weight update dies. That is vanishing gradients, and it is why sigmoid lost the hidden layer to ReLU.

■ The Catalog — What Each Bend Is For
Function σ(z) Range What it is doing, in load-forecasting language
Linear / identity z (−∞, ∞) No bend. Use on the output of a regression net so predicted MW can be any size. Never use as the only hidden activation, or the net collapses.
Sigmoid 1 / (1 + e−z) (0, 1) A smooth on/off switch. Historically the default. Saturates hard: at |z| > 4 the slope is ~0, so early layers stop learning. Fine as a gate (LSTM forget gate), poor as a deep hidden activation.
Tanh (ez − e−z) / (ez + e−z) (−1, 1) Sigmoid, recentred on zero. Hidden units can inhibit as well as excite. Still saturates. Common inside LSTM/GRU cells; rare as the only STLF hidden activation today.
ReLU max(0, z) [0, ∞) A hinge. Zero on the left, identity on the right. Cheap, sparse, and the slope is 1 wherever it is on, so gradients survive depth. This is the default hidden activation for MLP load models.
Leaky ReLU / PReLU max(αz, z) (−∞, ∞) ReLU with a drip on the negative side (α ~ 0.01). Stops a neuron from dying if it falls permanently left of zero. A cheap insurance policy on small STLF nets.
GELU z · Φ(z) (−∞, ∞) A smooth ReLU: multiplies z by the Gaussian CDF. Default in transformers. Use this if your day-ahead model is an attention net, not an MLP.
SiLU / Swish z · sigmoid(z) (−∞, ∞) Smooth, non-monotonic dip below zero. A GELU cousin. Shows up in some modern residual towers; not required for a first-cut BRP forecast.
Softmax ezi / Σj ezj simplex Turns a vector into probabilities that sum to 1. Classification, mixture weights, attention. Not how you emit megawatts. A quantile net uses several linear outputs (or pinball heads), not a softmax.

Softplus, ELU, SELU, Mish and the rest are variations on the same idea: keep a non-linear kink, keep a usable derivative, keep the computation cheap. The industry argument is almost never “which exotic σ” and almost always “ReLU vs GELU, and is the output linear.”

■ Why ReLU Won the Hidden Layer

ReLU looks too simple to be the sauce. It is a hinge. That is the point. A hinge is non-linear (the second derivative is a Dirac at zero; the function is not a straight line through the origin in both directions), it is cheap (one comparison), and its derivative is either 0 or 1 — so when a unit is on, the gradient passes through undamped.

Piecewise-linear universal approximation

One ReLU is a hockey stick. A hidden layer of them is a set of hinges at learned locations (the bias sets the kink; the incoming weights set the slope). The output layer mixes those sticks into a piecewise-linear polyline. Give it enough pieces and it traces the evening ramp, the lunch trough, the U-shaped temperature response. Deep ReLU nets just make more pieces, more cheaply, than a wide shallow one.

Sparsity is a feature

On any given hour, many ReLU units sit at 0. The heating neuron is silent in January. The cooling neuron is silent in July. That is specialisation: the network is not averaging heating and cooling into mush, it is routing the example through the units that own it. Sigmoid never quite turns off, so every unit meddles in every hour.

The remaining tax is dying ReLU: if a unit's incoming weights shove z permanently negative, σ'(z) = 0 forever and that neuron is a brick. A sensible initialisation, a lower learning rate, or a leaky ReLU drip fixes most of it. It is a real failure mode. It is not a reason to go back to sigmoid.

■ What a Load-Forecasting Desk Actually Puts Where

Architecture from the overview is not enough. You also have to pick σ per layer, because the output of a day-ahead model is megawatts, not a class label.

Layer Typical σ Why
MLP hidden ReLU (or leaky ReLU) Default. Builds the piecewise load shape. Fast enough to retrain every week before DAM gate.
LSTM / GRU gates Sigmoid (gates) + tanh (cell) Gates must be (0, 1) interpolators: how much of yesterday's ramp to keep. The cell needs a signed, bounded write. This is the one place sigmoid is still the right tool.
Transformer hidden GELU (or SiLU) What BERT / GPT / temporal fusion transformers ship. If your STLF model is attention, copy the activation they trained at scale.
Point-forecast output Linear Predicted MW has no natural upper bound and is not a probability. Squashing through sigmoid would cap the evening peak at 1. Do not do this.
Quantile heads (P10 / P50 / P90) Linear, one output per quantile Pinball loss trains each head. Softmax would force the quantiles to sum to 1, which is nonsense in MW.
Mixture-density output Softmax on mixture weights; linear / softplus on means and variances If you emit a full density for reserve sizing. Softmax belongs on the weights of the mixture, not on the megawatts.

A SAWEM BRP rule of thumb. Hidden ReLU, linear output, pinball or MSE loss, trained on weather forecasts available at 10:00 SAST. That is a complete, shippable short-term load model. Fancy activations do not rescue a leaked temperature feature or a load-shedding-contaminated target.

■ Gradients & Failure Modes — The Sauce Can Also Burn

Back-propagation multiplies σ'(z) at every layer. Choose a saturating activation and a deep net, and the product of those slopes goes to zero. Early layers stop moving. The network still runs; it just stops learning the features that sit far from the output — hour-of-day Fourier terms, lagged load, the heating kink.

FailureCauseWhat it looks like on a forecast desk
Vanishing gradient Sigmoid / tanh in a deep stack; |z| large, σ' ≈ 0 Training loss plateaus. The net has memorised the mean daily shape and will not learn the U-shaped temperature response sitting in layer 1.
Exploding gradient Unconstrained linear / badly scaled ReLU; product of slopes > 1 Loss becomes NaN. Predicted MW jumps to millions. Clip gradients; normalise inputs (temp_norm, not °C raw).
Dying ReLU Unit pushed to z < 0 for every training hour A heating neuron that never fires, even in July… wait, in July it should be silent. Dying is when it is silent in June and July. Watch the fraction of zero activations per unit.
Wrong output σ Sigmoid or ReLU on the MW head Sigmoid: peak load stuck near 1 after scaling mistakes. ReLU on output: the net cannot emit a negative residual if you are forecasting a deviation from last week, and it clips the left tail of a residual model.

None of these is an argument against non-linearity. They are arguments for putting the right bend in the right place. The linear collapse is fatal. A dead ReLU is a single lost hinge, and you still have the rest of the layer.
