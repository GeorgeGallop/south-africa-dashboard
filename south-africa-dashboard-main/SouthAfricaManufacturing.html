Manufacturing & Smelting

A deep-dive guide to how South Africa transforms raw minerals into metals, alloys, fuels and finished products.

What this page covers

This page explains the manufacturing processes behind South Africa’s major mining commodities. It includes a broad smelting overview as well as commodity-specific manufacturing pathways for platinum group metals, coal, manganese, gold, chrome, iron ore, diamonds and vanadium.

Use this page as a reference for the industrial steps that follow extraction: beneficiation, smelting or refining, alloy production, and the value-added manufacturing that turns minerals into export-ready products and industrial feedstock.

Manufacturing overview

Manufacturing in the mining sector is the full scope of turning raw materials into finished or semi-finished products. It includes ore preparation, concentration, smelting, refining, alloying, casting, finishing and certification.

This broader view makes it clear that smelting is one important manufacturing pathway, but not the only one. Some commodities are largely refined chemically, others are shaped mechanically, and some require downstream alloy production or product finishing.

Manufacturing therefore covers the full conversion from raw mineral to marketable product, including:

  • ore preparation and beneficiation
  • smelting and reduction
  • refining and purification
  • alloying and casting
  • finishing, polishing and certification
Smelting

Smelting is the metallurgical process in which heat and chemical reactions are used to extract a metal from its ore or concentrate. It usually involves melting the feedstock, separating slag, and converting metal oxides or sulphides into a liquid metal that can be refined.

Smelting is therefore a subsection of manufacturing—critical for many metals, but only one part of a broader industrial chain that also includes concentration, refining, alloying and product manufacture.

The smelting process explained

At a high level, smelting involves these core steps:

  • Concentration: increase the metal grade by crushing, grinding and separating minerals from waste rock.
  • Roasting/Calcining: oxidise or decompose certain ores to remove water, sulfur, and volatile contaminants.
  • Reduction: chemically reduce metal oxides or sulphides using carbon, hydrogen, natural gas or electricity.
  • Fluxing: add limestone, silica, dolomite or other fluxes to form slag and capture impurities.
  • Smelting furnace: melt the concentrate in a high-temperature furnace and separate metal from slag.
  • Refining: further purify the metal through electrolysis, converting, vacuum refining, or chemical treatment.

Furnace types vary by material and scale: blast furnaces, submerged arc furnaces, electric arc furnaces, reverberatory furnaces, flash smelters, and autoclaves are all part of the industrial toolkit.

Smelting and manufacturing workflow
  1. Mine to concentrator: ores are delivered to processing plants where mineralogical and metallurgical characteristics are defined.
  2. Concentrate production: valuable minerals are upgraded through flotation, gravity, magnetic or electrostatic methods.
  3. Smelting or chemical processing: concentrates are converted to metal, alloy or chemical intermediates.
  4. Refining and finishing: the product is purified and cast into marketable forms.
  5. Distribution and use: metals are shipped to steelmakers, manufacturers, jewellers or battery producers.
Commodity-specific manufacturing
Platinum Group Metals (PGMs)

South African PGMs are produced from nickel-copper sulphide ores. The process begins with flotation of crushed ore to produce a PGM-rich concentrate. This concentrate is smelted into a matte, then converted and refined in a series of chemical and electrolytic steps to separate platinum, palladium, rhodium, iridium and other precious metals.

Key steps include autoclave treatment, furnace matte making, converter blowing, and hydrometallurgical refining. By-products such as nickel, copper and gold are recovered en route, making the process complex and resource intensive. For more on PGMs, see Platinum Group Metals.

Coal

Coal is not smelted like metal ores. Instead, South African coal manufacturing focuses on beneficiation, coking, gasification and chemical conversion. Raw coal is washed and graded, with higher-quality thermal coal sent to power plants and metallurgical coal directed to coke ovens.

In manufacturing terms, coal becomes a feedstock for steelmaking, synthetic fuels and chemicals. Sasol’s coal-to-liquids and gasification plants illustrate how coal can be converted into liquids, ammonia and petrochemical intermediates. For details, see Coal mining in South Africa.

Manganese

Manganese ore is transformed into ferroalloys through submerged arc furnace smelting. Concentrate is pelletised and reduced with carbon to yield ferromanganese or silicomanganese, essential inputs for steel deoxidation and strength. The process depends on careful control of slag chemistry, carbon content and furnace temperature.

South Africa’s manganese smelters are a global reference point for alloy manufacture, producing both high-carbon and medium-carbon grades. For more information, see Manganese.

Gold

Gold manufacturing begins with ore milling and leaching, typically using cyanide in carbon-in-pulp or carbon-in-leach circuits. Recovered gold is refined by smelting into dore bars, which are further purified using the Miller or Wohlwill processes.

South Africa has a historical advantage in deep-level gold mining and high-purity refining. Modern gold manufacturing also includes recycling and electronics recovery. For further reading, see Gold mining in South Africa.

Chrome

Chromite ore is smelted in submerged arc furnaces to produce ferrochrome, an alloy of chromium and iron used in stainless steel. The process uses coke and flux to reduce chromium oxide and separate molten metal from slag. High chromium recovery and energy efficiency are critical in chrome manufacturing.

South Africa is one of the world’s largest ferrochrome producers, with manufacturing tied closely to global steel markets. See Chromite for technical context.

Iron Ore

Iron ore processing includes crushing, grinding, magnetic separation and pelletising, followed by smelting in blast furnaces or direct reduced iron (DRI) plants. The output is pig iron, hot metal or DRI, which is then converted to steel. The iron-making process is one of the most energy-intensive manufacturing steps in the mining sector.

South Africa supplies ore to both domestic steelmakers and export markets, making iron ore manufacturing a core part of the country’s industrial supply chain. For more, see Iron ore.

Diamonds

Diamonds are unique: they are not smelted. Their manufacturing pathway is mechanical and optical. After recovery, rough stones are sorted, cleaved, cut, polished and graded. Industrial diamonds may also be crushed and processed for abrasives.

South African diamond manufacturing adds value through world-class cutting and grading centres, and by ensuring stones meet international standards for size, clarity and colour. Learn more at Diamond.

Vanadium

Vanadium manufacturing is based on recovery from titaniferous magnetite and other oxide ores. The ore is roasted with sodium or salt, leached to dissolve vanadium, then precipitated and reduced to ferrovanadium or vanadium pentoxide (V2O5). The product is used in high-strength steel and emerging battery technologies.

Vanadium is often produced as a co-product of iron and titanium processing, making it a complex but important manufacturing pathway. For reference, see Vanadium.

Key manufacturing insights
  • Smelting is a high-temperature, energy-intensive core step for many base and precious metals.
  • Each mineral demands a bespoke process: what works for iron ore does not work for diamonds or gold.
  • Value creation comes from refining, alloying and finishing, not just mining.
  • South Africa’s advantage lies in its combination of mineral resources and established metallurgical infrastructure.

A strong manufacturing page should make clear that mining wealth is only real when it reaches the market as a stable, reliable product. That is the goal of the smelting and refining industries profiled here.
