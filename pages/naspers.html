Naspers Limited

Cape Town holding company — from Afrikaner nationalist press to the JSE's internet giant, with a line into Tencent

Naspers is the oldest money still sitting at the centre of South African media, and one of the most valuable companies on the JSE. It began in 1915 as Nasionale Pers in Stellenbosch — newspapers, language, and the National Party. A century later the same holding company owns Media24, a controlling stake in Amsterdam-listed Prosus, and through Prosus roughly a quarter of Tencent. Few private South African firms have that kind of reach: newsrooms and printing plants, ecommerce warehouses, pay-TV lineage, Cape capital networks, and a China-facing technology fortune that dwarfs the original press business.

Why Naspers sits in this dashboard

Mining houses, Eskom and Sasol are the usual “commanding heights.” Naspers is the other kind of old South African power: information, distribution and capital. For most of the twentieth century it printed the papers that organised Afrikaner politics. In the twenty-first it became the vehicle that converted a local media cash pile into a global internet holding — and then brought a slice of that fortune back into South African ecommerce, logistics and digital advertising.

  • Media: Media24 still runs News24, newspapers, magazines, book publishing and printing. That is national agenda-setting, in English and Afrikaans, from Cape Town newsrooms out to every province.
  • Commerce: Takealot, Mr D and Superbalist are the country’s default online retail and food-delivery stack — warehouses in Gauteng, the Western Cape and KwaZulu-Natal, vans on every metro freeway.
  • Capital markets: For years Naspers has been the largest or near-largest weight on the JSE, which means every GEPF, PIC, unit trust and pension fund in the country is tied to Tencent through a Cape Town holding company.
  • Stellenbosch network: The same Cape Afrikaner capital world as Sanlam, Remgro, PSG and Capitec — universities, boards, language, and a century of overlapping directors.
History & Origins — Nasionale Pers
  • 1915: Nasionale Pers is founded in Stellenbosch on 12 May. Jannie Marais and a circle of Afrikaner nationalists launch Die Burger as a language paper and a political project — close to the National Party from the start. This is old money in the Cape sense: wine-farm capital, Dutch-Reformed networks, and a university town that would later incubate Sanlam, Remgro and Naspers itself.
  • 1915–1948: The group becomes the print backbone of Afrikaner nationalism — newspapers, magazines, books, and later educational publishing. Titles such as Die Burger, and later Beeld and Rapport, give it a voice in Cape Town, the Free State and the Transvaal at once.
  • 1948–1980s: After the NP takes office, Nasionale Pers is not a state SOE, but it is as close as a private company gets: the house printer of the ruling culture. Paarl Media and the magazine stables turn a political press into a cash-generating media industrialist.
  • 1985–1986: Koos Bekker’s team launches M-Net, South Africa’s first pay-TV station. Naspers lists on the JSE in 1986. Electronic media becomes the second fortune — SuperSport, later DStv, later MultiChoice.
  • 1994–1997: Democracy breaks the old political monopoly. Bekker is made managing director. The group rebrands as Naspers, builds MIH (Myriad International Holdings), and starts buying internet assets instead of more printing presses.
  • 2001: MIH invests about US$32 million for a large stake in a then-unknown Chinese internet firm, Tencent. That cheque is the event that turned a Cape media house into a global holding company. The residual stake is still the core of group NAV.
  • 2010s: International classifieds (OLX), payments (PayU), food delivery (iFood and others), etail. In South Africa the group backs Takealot, which absorbs Kalahari and becomes the local Amazon analogue. Media24 remains the domestic news and print engine.
  • 2019: Two unbundlings that redraw the map. MultiChoice is listed separately on the JSE (pay-TV leaves the nest). Prosus is listed in Amsterdam as the international internet vehicle. Naspers keeps control of Prosus through a dual-share structure.
  • 2021–2025: Repeated attempts to close the “holding-company discount” — N-share issues, buybacks, and eventually the unwind of the Naspers–Prosus cross-holding. Bob van Dijk exits; Fabricio Bloisi (founder of iFood) becomes group CEO in July 2024.
  • FY2026: Group revenue from continuing operations rises to US$10.8 billion. Free cash flow is US$1.49 billion. The Tencent stake is retained. Takealot grows GMV and revenue inside South Africa. The board still signs letters from Cape Town — Bekker as chair, Bloisi as chief executive.
How the group is wired now

Naspers is a holding company with almost no operating factories of its own. Cash and control sit in a stack of listed vehicles:

Naspers Ltd (JSE: NPN)

Cape Town parent. Controls Prosus. Directly owns the South African consumer-internet and media assets, including Media24 and the Takealot group. This is the share that dominates many JSE index funds.

Prosus N.V. (Amsterdam)

The global internet portfolio: Tencent, iFood, PayU, eMAG, classifieds, edtech and the regional “ecosystems” in Latin America, India and Europe. Naspers holds ~41% of the N shares and 100% of the B shares — about three-quarters of the votes, ~43% of the economics.

Tencent (~22.8%)

WeChat, games, fintech, cloud. Not a South African company — but the dividend and mark-to-market of this stake is what makes Naspers a national-market event every time Shenzhen or Hong Kong moves. The group has said the holding will be kept for the foreseeable future.

MultiChoice (DStv, SuperSport, Showmax) was unbundled in 2019 and is no longer inside Naspers. The cultural footprint of M-Net still is: Bekker’s original pay-TV team is the same generation that placed the Tencent bet.

South African footprint — ties in every direction

The Tencent story is offshore. The domestic machine is still a country-wide network of newsrooms, presses, warehouses and last-mile routes.

Media24 — news, print, language

South Africa’s largest publisher. News24 is the country’s biggest digital newsroom. Afrikaans titles (Die Burger, Beeld, Rapport) still matter in the Western Cape, Gauteng and the Free State. English and tabloid titles, magazines, and Via Afrika educational publishing put the group in classrooms as well as kitchens.

Printing and distribution (the old Paarl Media lineage) mean physical plants and truck routes — not just a website. Media24 CEO: Raj Lalbahadur (from April 2025).

Takealot group — ecommerce & food

Takealot, Mr D Food and Superbalist. Fulfilment centres around Johannesburg, Cape Town and Durban; a private last-mile fleet; and a consumer habit that now sits beside Shoprite and the malls rather than replacing them.

FY2026: GMV up 14%, revenue up 18%. This is Naspers’ most visible everyday presence in Gauteng townships, Cape suburbs and KZN coastal cities at once.

Cape Town & Stellenbosch

Legal home, board letters, and the original political geography. Naspers Centre in Cape Town. Stellenbosch is the founding town and still the social hinterland — university, Afrikaans capital, overlapping directors with other Cape groups. Phuti Mahanyele-Dabengwa (SA CEO) sits on the USB advisory board at Stellenbosch.

JSE, PIC and household savings

Because NPN is a heavy JSE weight, Naspers is wired into the Public Investment Corporation and the Government Employees Pension Fund whether or not anyone in Pretoria thinks of it as a “media company.” A move in Tencent is a move in South African retirement savings. That is a quieter nationwide tie than a mine or a power station, and just as real.

Old money, new stack

Call it old money because the institution is old: 1915 press capital, National Party adjacent, Stellenbosch-rooted, listed for forty years. The operating stack is not a coal mine. It is:

  • Language and narrative — a century of Afrikaans and English publishing, still the loudest commercial newsroom in the country.
  • Pipes that used to be analogue — printing plants, then satellite pay-TV, then fibre, apps and warehouses.
  • A single outsized foreign asset — Tencent — that makes Naspers look like a technology fund listed in Johannesburg rather than a newspaper company that got lucky.
  • A dual-listed control structure — votes in Amsterdam, headlines in Cape Town, fulfilment centres on the Highveld.

The political party that Nasionale Pers once served is no longer in office. The holding company survived by leaving print cash in internet equity. That is the through-line: a Cape institution that kept control of the story, then of the platform, then of a slice of someone else’s platform in Shenzhen.

People
  • Koos Bekker — chair. Founded the M-Net/MultiChoice business in 1985, ran the group through the Tencent years, stepped back from the CEO seat and kept the board. Non-independent chair; the continuity figure of the modern group.
  • Fabricio Bloisi — group CEO since July 2024. Came up through iFood in Brazil. The current strategy language is “AI-first” regional ecommerce ecosystems (LatAm, India, Europe) while leaving the Tencent holding in place.
  • Phuti Mahanyele-Dabengwa — CEO of Naspers in South Africa since 2019; executive director from 2025. Also an independent director of Vodacom. The domestic face of the group in Johannesburg and Cape Town.
  • Nico Marais — group CFO from April 2025.
  • Raj Lalbahadur — CEO of Media24 from April 2025, after time as Media24 CFO.
FY2026 snapshot

Year ended 31 March 2026, signed in Cape Town 27 June 2026:

  • Continuing-operations revenue US$10.8 billion (FY25: US$7.2 billion).
  • Free cash flow US$1.49 billion (FY25: US$968 million).
  • Ecosystem adjusted EBITDA US$1.34 billion.
  • Core headline earnings 455 US cents per N share.
  • Prosus recommended dividend 28 euro cents per N share.
  • Tencent stake retained (~22.8%). Naspers still trades at a steep discount to NAV and to the look-through Tencent value — the long-running “holding company problem.”
