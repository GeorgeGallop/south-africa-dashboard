Anglo American plc

Global Mining Conglomerate Founded in South Africa — Iron Ore, Platinum, Coal, Diamonds & Copper

Anglo American is a British multinational mining company with deep roots in South Africa, where it was founded in 1917. It is one of the world's largest mining companies, with operations spanning iron ore (Kumba Iron Ore), platinum group metals (Anglo American Platinum — Amplats), metallurgical coal (MC Mining), diamonds (De Beers), and copper (Chile, Peru, Brazil). Anglo American's South African operations are a cornerstone of the country's mining industry, contributing billions in export revenue, tax, and employment.

History & Origins
  • 1917: Anglo American Corporation was founded by Sir Ernest Oppenheimer in Johannesburg to mine gold and diamonds on the Witwatersrand. The company was established with significant British and American capital, but its operational heart was always in South Africa.
  • 1920s–1940s: Anglo American became the dominant force in the global diamond industry through its control of De Beers. It also expanded into gold mining (East Rand Proprietary Mines, Vaal Reefs) and coal.
  • 1950s–1970s: The company diversified into platinum (founding Rustenburg Platinum Mines, later Anglo American Platinum) and base metals. Anglo American became the largest and most powerful mining company in South Africa — nicknamed the "Group" for its pervasive influence over the South African economy.
  • 1990s: Post-apartheid, Anglo American restructured. It moved its primary listing to London in 1999 and merged parts of its business with Minorco (the Luxembourg-based offshore arm). The South African operations were reorganised into Anglo American plc, listed in London and Johannesburg.
  • 2000s–2010s: The company expanded globally — acquiring major copper operations in Chile (Los Bronces, Collahuasi) and Brazil (Minas-Rio iron ore, Barro Alto nickel). It divested from gold, paper, and forest products to focus on "sustainable mining" commodities.
  • 2020s: In 2022, BHP launched a £39 billion takeover bid for Anglo American — a bid that failed on structure (BHP wanted to unbundle Amplats and De Beers). The bid triggered a strategic review, leading Anglo American to announce a major restructuring: it plans to demerge or sell De Beers, sell its coal assets, and focus on copper, premium iron ore, and crop nutrients. The company is dramatically simplifying its portfolio.
South African Operations
Kumba Iron Ore (Northern Cape)

Sishen Mine (Kathu): Massive open-pit iron ore mine producing >30 million tonnes per year of high-quality lump and fine iron ore. It is one of the largest open-pit mines in the world.

Kolomela Mine (Postmasburg): A newer open-pit iron ore operation with ~13 million tonnes per year capacity.

Logistics: Kumba ore is transported by the dedicated Sishen–Saldanha railway line to the deep-water port of Saldanha Bay for export. Transnet's performance on this line is a critical constraint on Kumba's output.

Anglo American Platinum (Amplats)

Mogalakwena Mine (Limpopo): The world's largest open-pit PGM mine, producing ~1 million ounces of PGMs per year. Low-cost and with long life.

Amandelbult Mine (Limpopo): Large underground PGM mine, producing ~700,000 ounces per year.

Mototolo Mine (Mpumalanga): A mechanised underground PGM mine, producing ~300,000 ounces per year.

Processing: Amplats operates integrated smelters (Waterval, Polokwane) and the Precious Metals Refinery (Rustenburg) that processes PGM concentrates into refined platinum, palladium, rhodium, and other metals.

De Beers (Diamonds)

Venetia Mine (Limpopo): South Africa's largest diamond mine, now transitioning from open-pit to underground. The underground expansion (VUP) represents one of the largest single mining investments in South Africa.

Other SA operations: Voorspoed and Kimberley mines, though most of De Beers' production now comes from Botswana, Namibia, and Canada.

Anglo's plan: Anglo has announced it will demerge or sell De Beers as part of the strategic simplification following the BHP bid, ending Anglo's century-long involvement in the diamond industry.

Coal (Thermal & Metallurgical)

Thermal coal: Anglo owns coal operations in Mpumalanga (e.g., Zibulo, Greenside, Kriel). These are being sold as part of the decarbonisation strategy — Anglo has committed to exit thermal coal completely.

Metallurgical coal: Operations in Australia (coking coal for steelmaking) are core.

Future: Anglo's South African coal business (Anglo American Coal) has been under review for sale since 2020. A transaction has been delayed by regulatory and market conditions but is expected to conclude by 2025–2026.

Why Anglo American Matters for South Africa
  • Export revenue: Anglo American's South African operations (Kumba iron ore, Amplats, De Beers) generate tens of billions of rand in export earnings. Kumba alone contributes ~R60 billion in annual revenue.
  • Smelting & processing: Amplats' Waterval smelter and the Precious Metals Refinery are critical downstream processing infrastructure. If Anglo discontinues or scales back these facilities, South Africa loses high-value beneficiation and the jobs that go with it.
  • Employment: ~30,000 direct employees in South Africa, plus contractors and suppliers. Kumba's iron ore operations in the Northern Cape and Amplats' mines in Limpopo are major employers in economically depressed regions.
  • Tax & government revenue: Anglo American is one of the largest corporate taxpayers in South Africa. In FY2024, Kumba alone contributed ~R15 billion in taxes and royalties.
  • Infrastructure: Anglo's mines have driven massive infrastructure investment in rail (the Sishen–Saldanha railway line is one of the most important freight corridors in Africa), ports (Saldanha Bay), and power supply.
  • Strategic simplification risk: The post-BHP-bid restructuring — selling De Beers, exiting coal, and focusing on copper and iron ore — means Anglo's South African portfolio will shrink. The company's future is increasingly in Chile and Peru (copper), not South Africa. This has implications for the future of the PGM and diamond industries in the country.
