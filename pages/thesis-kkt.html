📜 Karush-Kuhn-Tucker (KKT) Conditions

The first-order necessary conditions for optimality in a constrained optimisation problem — and the mathematical bridge between dispatch and prices in a wholesale electricity market. In SAWEM, the KKT multipliers are the market prices.

📊 1. The Constrained Optimisation Problem

Almost every question in power-system economics is an optimisation subject to constraints. We can only dispatch as much as the network can carry, run units within their physical limits, and keep energy balance at every instant. Write a generic inequality-constrained problem as:

minimise   f(x)     x ∈ ℝn
subject to   gi(x) ≤ 0,   i = 1, …, m
              hj(x) = 0,   j = 1, …, l

Here f is the objective (e.g. total generation cost), the gi are inequality constraints (e.g.  pg ≤ Pgmax, |fℓ| ≤ Fℓmax ) and the hj are equality constraints (e.g. the nodal power-balance equations).

The core object is the Lagrangian, which attaches a price-like multiplier to every constraint:

L(x, λ, μ) = f(x) + Σi=1m μi · gi(x) + Σj=1l λj · hj(x)
  • λj — multiplier on the equality constraint hj (free in sign).
  • μi — multiplier on the inequality constraint gi (constrained to be non-negative).

✅ 2. The Four KKT Conditions

Suppose x* is a local minimiser of the constrained problem and a constraint qualification holds (e.g. linear independence of the active constraint gradients — LICQ, or Slater's condition for convex problems). Then there exist multipliers (λ*, μ*) such that:

ConditionStatementEconomic meaning
1 Stationarity ∇x L(x*, λ*, μ*) = 0 No profitable infinitesimal re-dispatch: marginal benefit equals the marginal value of the constraints it consumes.
2 Primal feasibility gi(x*) ≤ 0,   hj(x*) = 0 The solution actually respects the physical limits of the system.
3 Dual feasibility μi* ≥ 0 Prices are never negative for a constraint you are relaxing — a transmission limit has a non-negative congestion price.
4 Complementary slackness μi* · gi(x*) = 0 A constraint is either inactive (then its price is zero) or binding (then it has a price). No free lunch — you only pay for what binds.
📚 Intuition: Complementary slackness is what makes a price form inside the optimisation. A slack constraint (gi < 0) contributes nothing to the Lagrangian, so μi must vanish. A binding constraint (gi = 0) can carry a non-zero multiplier — exactly the shadow price we observe as a market signal.

💰 3. Multipliers = Shadow Prices

The KKT multiplier attached to a constraint is its marginal value: the rate at which the optimal objective improves if the constraint is relaxed by one unit. This is the economic content of the whole construction.

μi* = − ∂f(x*) / ∂bi    where   gi(x) ≤ bi

This is why the Methods page lists “λt = SMP (System Marginal Price)” against the power-balance row: the price is not an assumption imposed on the market — it emerges as the dual of the balance constraint in the clearing optimisation.

⚡ 4. KKT in Economic Dispatch

Start with the simplest market-clearing problem — merit-order (no network):

minimise   Σg Cg(pg)
subject to   Σg pg = D    [λ]
              Pgmin ≤ pg ≤ Pgmax    [μgmin, μgmax]

The Lagrangian is

L = Σg Cg(pg) + λ·(D − Σg pg) + Σg [ μgmax(pg − Pgmax) + μgmin(Pgmin − pg) ]

Stationarity w.r.t. pg gives the fundamental dispatch rule:

C'g(pg) − λ − μgmax + μgmin = 0
Position of gKKT implicationMarket reading
Interior: Pgmin < pg < Pgmax μgmax = μgmin = 0  ⇒  C'g(pg) = λ Marginal cost equals the SMP — this unit sets the price.
At upper bound pg = Pgmax μgmax = λ − C'g(Pgmax) > 0 Cheap unit is saturated and earns a capacity rent (it is cheaper than the SMP).
At lower bound pg = Pgmin μgmin = λ − C'g(Pgmin) > 0 Must-run / minimum-load unit dispatched even though expensive.
🚀 The key takeaway: the market price λ is not a free variable; it is pinned down by the stationarity condition together with the balance equality. It equals the marginal cost of the marginal unit (the merit-order rule), and the KKT conditions are simply the formal statement of that rule.

🎯 5. KKT in OPF → Locational Marginal Prices

Add the network. In a DC-OPF the constraints become nodal:

minimise   Σg Cg(pg)
subject to   B·θ = P    (nodal power balance)    [λn]
              |fℓ| ≤ Fℓmax    (line capacity)    [ζℓ+, ζℓ−]

Now every node carries its own multiplier λn, and every congested line its own ζℓ. The nodal price λn decomposes into components:

LMPn = λref  +  Σℓ ζℓ · ∂fℓ/∂Dn  +  loss component
ComponentMultiplierWhat it pays for
EnergyλrefThe system-wide cost of one more MW of demand at the reference bus.
Congestionζℓ+, ζℓ−The cost of the binding line limits — this is where prices diverge between nodes.
Losses∂(loss)/∂DnThe marginal cost of incremental network losses (drops out in the pure DC linearisation).

In the SAWEM framework (see Methods) the congestion duals are listed exactly as “ζℓ,t+, ζℓ,t− = congestion rents”. KKT is the reason those ζ values exist and are priced — they are the shadow prices of the transmission limits.

🌎 6. KKT Across the SAWEM Clearing Problem

The South African Wholesale Electricity Market is cleared by a Security-Constrained Unit Commitment / Economic Dispatch (SCUC/SCED). Each constraint in that MILP carries a dual that the KKT conditions identify as a price. Mapping the framework's constraint table to KKT:

💡 Why the SMP is a KKT multiplier: in a pay-as-clear market every dispatched generator is paid the same price — the dual on the energy-balance equality in the clearing optimisation. Because the market operator solves the KKT system (via the MILP/LP solver), the price is a by-product of the solution, not an input. This is the most direct application of KKT to wholesale electricity markets.

👥 7. KKT & Strategic Bidding (Market Power)

The clearing problem above is the system operator's. A generator that can move the price faces its own constrained optimisation — maximise profit given its residual demand (the price it receives depends on how much it sells):

maximise   π(q) = p(q)·q − C(q)
first-order (KKT) condition:   p(q) + q · ∂p/∂q = C'(q)

Rearranging produces the Lerner index, the standard measure of market power:

L = (p − MC) / p = − 1 / ε     where   ε = (q/p) · ∂p/∂q   (elasticity of residual demand)
  • The generator marks up price above marginal cost by an amount inversely proportional to ε.
  • When ε is large (many substitutes, competitive fringe), the markup is small — close to the KKT point of the competitive dispatch.
  • When ε is small (must-run load, tight system), the markup grows — this is exactly the scarcity pricing the ABM observes as emergent behaviour.

In the Agent-Based Model, agents learn these bid policies; the Lerner index and the gap (SMP − SRMC) are tracked as emergent market-power metrics. The competitive benchmark they are measured against is precisely the KKT solution of the dispatch problem.

⚖️ 8. Convexity, Sufficiency, and the Non-convex Catch

Problem classRole of KKTWholesale-market example
Convex (LP / QP; f and g convex, h affine)KKT is necessary and sufficient; no duality gap.DC-OPF / merit-order dispatch — the SMP and LMPs come straight from the KKT solution.
Linear (LP) — a special convex caseKKT = primal/dual feasibility + complementary slackness (the simplex / interior-point optimality conditions).SCED (security-constrained economic dispatch) with linear bid curves.
Mixed-Integer (MILP) — binary commitment variablesKKT holds on the continuous relaxation; integrality handled by branch-and-cut. Prices come from the relaxed duals (a source of the duality gap).SCUC with vg,t ∈ {0,1}, start-up, min-up/down.
Non-convex (AC-OPF: sinusoidal power flow)KKT only necessary; may find local optima and raise ill-defined prices.AC-OPF / lossy LMP models; often solved with SDP or SOCP relaxations.
⚠️ Practical caveat for SAWEM: because the real clearing problem is a MILP with binary commitment and some non-linearities, the reported SMP / LMPs are identical to KKT multipliers of the corresponding relaxed optimality system. In a tight, scarce system this can under-price flexibility — which is why market designs add explicit scarcity or reserve-price adders (the reserve dual μ above) on top of the energy price.

🔄 9. KKT in the SAWEM Evolutionary Framework

KKT is the connective tissue of the closed-loop framework. It does not sit as a separate module — it is the mathematical engine that turns bid stacks into prices and dispatch, and therefore the signal every agent learns from.

  • Pricing: the SMP, LMPs, congestion rents and reserve prices are the KKT duals of the clearing problem — the same values the Methods page lists in its dual column.
  • Learning: agents receive rewards (profit) whose dependence on dispatch is fully captured by the KKT stationarity conditions; reinforcement learning drives bids toward the KKT/equilibrium point of the competitive case.
  • Benchmarking: the KKT solution of the competitive dispatch is the no-market-power counterfactual against which the ABM's emergent behaviour (Lerner index, SMP uplift) is judged.
  • Inference: the sensitivity (shadow-price) structure is what allows causal statements like “the congestion rent on the Cape corridor is the social value of relieving it.”
