🔥 Natural Gas

Overview of South Africa's natural gas markets, infrastructure, and the national Gas Master Plan.

Overview

Natural gas is positioned as a transition fuel for South Africa, helping the grid balance variable wind and solar while reducing the most carbon-intensive parts of the energy system. It also supports coastal industry, petrochemicals, metals processing, and fertilizer production.

This page focuses on the broad role of gas in the South African energy transition: balancing power, supporting demand-side resilience, and linking domestic supply with imports through infrastructure.

Gas pathways

South Africa’s gas strategy is shaped by three pathways:

  • Domestic and offshore exploration to build local supply.
  • LNG import and regasification terminals to meet near-term demand.
  • Pipeline infrastructure and market reforms to connect supply to industry and power.

Together, these pathways aim to deliver a flexible gas supply that can firm renewables, support industrial clusters, and reduce the country’s reliance on coal-fired generation.

Key takeaways
  • Gas is being framed as a bridge fuel and industrial enabler, not just a power source.
  • Domestic supply is very limited today, so imports and regasification infrastructure are essential.
  • Offshore exploration is valuable, but the volumes are expected to be supplemental rather than the main baseload source.
  • The Gas Master Plan is the blueprint for connecting supply, demand, and regulation.
