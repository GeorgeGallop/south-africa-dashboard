Telkom SA SOC Ltd

State-owned telecommunications company — mobile, wholesale fibre (Openserve) and enterprise ICT (BCX)

Telkom SA SOC Limited is the old posts-and-telecoms monopoly that listed, launched a mobile network, split the copper plant into a wholesale fibre company, and is now growing again on data. It is still a state-owned company in form (SOC Ltd) and still a JSE name (TKG). The South African book is three operating pieces: Telkom Consumer (mobile and home broadband), Openserve (the country’s largest open-access fibre network), and BCX (enterprise IT). The tower company, Swiftnet, was sold in January 2025.

JSE: TKG · Consumer · Openserve · BCX

Fibre, mobile and enterprise ICT

How Telkom got here
  • 1991: The Department of Posts and Telecommunications is split. Telkom is corporatised as the fixed-line operator. It inherits the national copper plant, the exchanges and the international gateways.
  • 1997: 30% is sold to Thintana (SBC Communications and Telekom Malaysia) as a strategic equity partner. The deal is later unwound; Telkom lists on the JSE in 2003.
  • 2010: Telkom Mobile (originally 8.ta) launches. Fifteen years later, mobile is the growth engine — 14 consecutive quarters of market-leading service-revenue growth by FY2026.
  • 2015–22: The wholesale network is branded Openserve, then legally separated as a stand-alone subsidiary (1 September 2022). Copper is wound down; fibre is the replacement plant.
  • 2022–26: Serame Taukobong’s “OneTelkom” and data-led strategy: keep Openserve, grow mobile, fix BCX, sell non-core assets. Swiftnet (masts and towers) is sold to TowerCo BidCo with effect from 31 January 2025 for about R6.6 billion cash. A planned minority sale of Openserve is dropped — fibre is treated as core.
The South African book — three operating pieces
Telkom Consumer

The retail arm: mobile (pre-paid and contract), home fibre sold under the Telkom brand, and small-business connectivity. Mobile service revenue rose 6.8% to R22.4 billion in FY2026; pre-paid service revenue was up 10.3%.

Active mobile subscribers reached 25.7 million in March 2026 (pre-paid 22.6 million). Mobile data subscribers jumped 31.1% to almost 20 million. 4G population coverage is quoted at 97%.

Openserve

South Africa’s largest open-access fibre network operator. Wholesale FTTH, FTTB, carrier and enterprise circuits sold to ISPs and operators — including Telkom’s own retail arm. FY2026 revenue R12.6 billion (+2.3%): the first full-year increase in nine years.

March 2026: 1.54 million homes passed, 817,540 connected, connectivity rate 53.1%. About 9,525 mobile sites sit on Openserve fibre backhaul. In July 2026 Openserve also launched its own ISP, while saying the wholesale open-access model stays.

BCX

Business Connexion — the systems integrator. Cloud, cybersecurity, IoT, data analytics and converged communications for government and enterprise. IT revenue was roughly stable at R6.7 billion in FY2026; cybersecurity grew 21.1%.

The unit is under new leadership and is being repositioned as a connectivity provider for digital services. Hardware/software sales and legacy converged-comms remain the drag; the IT market is price-sensitive and slow.

FY2026 snapshot (year to 31 March 2026)

Group revenue rose 1.4% to R44.5 billion. Data is now the business: group data revenue R26.6 billion (+7.6%), 59.8% of the total. Profit for the year was R3.55 billion (+27.5%). Adjusted EBITDA was about R12.5 billion at a 28.1% margin. The board lifted the dividend payout range to 40–60% of free cash flow and declared at 45%.

Q1 FY2027 (three months to 30 June 2026) kept the same shape: group data revenue +8.8%, Openserve revenue +5.6%, mobile subscribers 25.3 million, fibre homes connected 843,563 at a 53.9% connectivity rate. BCX IT services were flat; hardware sales fell.

Fibre, power and why this sits next to the energy names
  • Openserve’s plant is the national fibre layer that mobile sites, ISPs and a large share of government WAN traffic sit on. Telkom quotes on the order of 180,000 km of fibre. That is infrastructure in the same sense as Transnet’s rail or Eskom’s wires — except the last mile is now FTTH, not copper.
  • Mobile growth is backhauled on that fibre: 9,525 base stations connected by March 2026. Data traffic from 2019 to 2026 rose far faster than data revenue (MyBroadband: traffic +476% vs revenue +200%) — the same “more bits, less rand per bit” problem every operator has.
  • The network is an electricity load. Telkom has an energy-transformation programme (sites, batteries, generators, some renewables) because load shedding and municipal outages take radios and exchanges down. It is not an Eskom-scale customer, but it is a national critical-infrastructure operator that cannot run on diesel forever.
  • Regulation sits with ICASA. Spectrum, facilities leasing, open-access fibre and the copper shutdown are the policy levers. Operation Vulindlela’s digital workstream (spectrum, SKA, digital public infrastructure) is the state’s side of the same story — see OV digital.
What was sold — and what was kept

Swiftnet (masts and towers, previously inside Gyro) was the value-unlock sale. Effective 31 January 2025 Telkom sold 100% to TowerCo BidCo. Cash consideration was about R6.6 billion; the group booked a large disposal gain in FY2025 and treats Swiftnet as a discontinued operation thereafter. Gyro property disposals continue as a smaller non-core programme.

Openserve was not sold. After a 2022–24 market sounding for a minority partner, Taukobong suspended the process. Fibre is the wholesale engine; July 2026’s own-ISP launch is an attempt to lift the ~54% connectivity rate on homes already passed, without (Telkom says) dropping the open-access wholesale contracts. Wholesale ISPs and WAPA have questioned the dual role. That argument will run.
