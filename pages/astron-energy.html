Astron Energy

Glencore's South African fuels company — owner of the Cape Town (Milnerton) refinery

This is the name you were reaching for. The Cape Town crude refinery is not still "Caltex" and it is not Chevron. It is Astron Energy Proprietary Limited, formerly Chevron South Africa. Glencore bought a controlling stake in 2018–19, kept the plant at Milnerton running when SAPREF and Engen died, and is now putting about R6 billion into Clean Fuels II kit so the Western Cape still has a local barrel.

Legal name: Astron Energy (Pty) Ltd — formerly Chevron South Africa (Pty) Ltd
Owner: Glencore (controlling, via Glencore South Africa Oil Investments); BEE / other investors alongside
Acquired: Competition filing 2018; deal completed around 2019, ~$1 billion for Chevron's SA downstream
Refinery: Milnerton, Cape Town — ~100,000 barrels per day
Products: Petrol, diesel, jet, fuel oil, LPG, bitumen
Retail: Ex-Caltex network, rebranded Astron — ~700 forecourts by April 2026

Astron Energy

Milnerton refinery · Cape Town · Glencore subsidiary

What it is

Astron is Glencore's South African downstream oil company. The asset that matters for energy security is the Milnerton refinery in a Cape Town suburb — opened in 1966 as a Caltex plant, run for half a century by Chevron, sold when the US major left Africa. The Competition Tribunal file is explicit: the target was "Chevron South Africa (Pty) Ltd (now known as Astron Energy Proprietary Limited)" and the refinery is the one "owned and operated by Astron in Cape Town."

Glencore did not buy a trading book only. It bought a crude unit, storage, a lubes plant and hundreds of forecourts. That is why Astron shows up next to Shell, BP, Engen and Sasol in the fuels market, and why it is the private counterpart to SAPREF (now CEF) and Natref (Sasol/TotalEnergies).

The Milnerton refinery

Nameplate is about 100,000 bpd. It takes imported crude (the Cape has no local oil field feeding it) and makes petrol, diesel, jet, fuel oil, LPG and bitumen for the Western Cape and the rest of the inland system. After SAPREF (Durban, 180 kb/d) and Engen (Wentworth) shut, national crude distillation collapsed; industry figures put remaining capacity far below the old ~500 kb/d. Astron is one of the two crude refineries still operating — the other coastal story is the state's attempt to revive SAPREF.

  • 2020: A fire at the plant killed two engineers and took the refinery offline.
  • 2023: Restart. Glencore has told investors the unit is cash-generative.
  • 2025–27: R6 billion (~$328 million) Clean Fuels II programme — gasoline hydrotreating so petrol and diesel meet 10 ppm sulphur by the July 2027 deadline (the spec was originally meant for 2017). Foundations for the hydrotreater were already in the ground in 2025. CEO Booley told MPs Astron would supply compliant fuels by the date requested.

Estimates cited around the upgrade put local-economy revenue associated with the plant in the tens of billions of rand and tens of thousands of direct-plus-indirect jobs. Treat those as industry advocacy numbers; the strategic fact does not depend on them: if Milnerton stops, the Cape has no crude refinery.

Retail and lubes

The visible brand is the forecourt. Caltex sites have been converting to Astron since 2022 — on the order of 20 sites a month at peak. By mid-2025 the network had passed 500; by April 2026 it was at 700, putting Astron in the top tier with Shell, BP and Engen (Vitol) / Puma (Trafigura). Most of the cash, though, still comes from the refinery and wholesale, not from selling pies on the N1.

Durban's lubricants plant is the other factory: about 60 million litres a year after Glencore's upgrade. It is not the Cape Town story, but it is why Astron is a full downstream company rather than a single-site refiner.

Ownership

The acquiring vehicle in the Tribunal case was Glencore South Africa Oil Investments (Pty) Ltd, taking 75% of Chevron SA from Off the Shelf Investments (the BEE vehicle that had sat in the Chevron structure). Glencore is the controlling parent. Public descriptions in 2026 still call Astron "Glencore-backed" and note sovereign and private co-investors. There is no listed Astron share. The listed name is Glencore plc.

That is the opposite of SAPREF: there the majors (Shell and BP) sold a dead plant to the state for R1. Here the major (Chevron) sold a living plant to a trader, and the trader is spending real money to keep it alive under Clean Fuels II.

Why it sits under Glencore on this dashboard

Because that is the hierarchy. Astron is not a separate SOE and not an independent JSE oil major. It is Glencore's South African fuels subsidiary, and the Cape Town refinery is its core industrial asset. For the group overview — coal, chrome, the trading book — see Glencore. For the state's Durban refinery, see SAPREF.
