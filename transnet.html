Transnet

South Africa’s State-Owned Freight Logistics Company
Sector: Freight Rail, Ports & Pipelines
Founded: 1990
Owner: Government of South Africa (100%)
Revenue (2024): ~R 78 billion
Employees: ~45,000
What is Transnet?

Transnet is South Africa’s largest freight logistics company and a state-owned enterprise. It is responsible for the movement of the majority of the country’s freight by rail, the operation of the major commercial ports, and the management of petroleum pipelines.

Transnet’s Main Divisions / Operating Units
Transnet Freight Rail (TFR)

The largest division. Operates South Africa’s freight rail network (over 20,000 km of track). Responsible for transporting coal, iron ore, manganese, chrome, and other bulk commodities.

Transnet National Ports Authority (TNPA)

Owns and manages the infrastructure of South Africa’s 8 major commercial ports (harbours, channels, berths, etc.). Acts as the landlord of the ports.

Transnet Port Terminals (TPT)

Operates the actual terminals inside the ports: container terminals, bulk terminals, and automotive terminals. Handles loading/unloading of cargo.

Transnet Pipelines

Owns and operates ~3,000 km of petroleum and gas pipelines. Transports crude oil, refined petroleum products, and gas across the country.

Transnet Engineering

Provides engineering, manufacturing, and maintenance services for rail locomotives, wagons, and port equipment. Also involved in refurbishment and new builds.

Transnet Property

Manages Transnet’s extensive property portfolio (land, buildings, and real estate assets) across the country.

Major Challenges Transnet Has Faced
  • State Capture and corruption (2010s)
  • Chronic underinvestment and ageing infrastructure
  • Widespread theft and vandalism of rail lines and cables
  • Decline in rail freight market share (road transport now dominates)
  • Operational inefficiencies and massive backlogs
  • High debt levels and financial distress
Current Reforms

The South African government is actively trying to restructure Transnet, attract private sector investment (especially in rail and terminals), and improve performance. Transnet remains critical to the export of minerals and the overall economy.
