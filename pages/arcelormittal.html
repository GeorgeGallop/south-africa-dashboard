ArcelorMittal South Africa

South Africa's Largest Steel Producer — Integrated Blast Furnace & Rolling Mill Operations

ArcelorMittal South Africa (AMSA) is the largest steel producer in Sub-Saharan Africa and the primary supplier of flat and long steel products to the domestic market. Listed on the JSE (ACL), it is a subsidiary of ArcelorMittal, the world's largest steel company. The company operates integrated steel mills, blast furnaces, and rolling mills that convert iron ore and scrap into liquid steel and finished steel products for construction, automotive, mining, and industrial customers.

History & Origins
  • 1928: Founded as ISCOR (Iron and Steel Industrial Corporation) — a state-owned entity established to create a domestic steel industry for South Africa, reducing dependence on imported steel. The first integrated steelworks was built at Pretoria (now closed).
  • 1940s–1970s: ISCOR expanded with major works at Vanderbijlpark (flat products) and Newcastle (long products), using blast furnace / basic oxygen furnace (BOF) technology. The company supplied steel for South Africa's industrialisation, including mines, railways, power stations, and construction of the apartheid-era homeland infrastructure.
  • 1989: ISCOR was privatised and listed on the JSE. It remained a large, diversified steelmaker through the 1990s, supplying both domestic and export markets.
  • 2001: Ispat International (Mittal) acquired a controlling stake. The company was renamed Ispat Iscor, then Mittal Steel South Africa, and finally ArcelorMittal South Africa after the 2006 merger of Mittal Steel and Arcelor.
  • 2000s–2010s: AMSA faced growing competitive pressure from low-cost Chinese steel imports, high domestic input costs (electricity, iron ore, labour, logistics), and a weak global steel market. The company closed its Pretoria works and scaled back long steel production.
  • 2020s: AMSA announced a major restructuring — the "Longs Business" (Newcastle, Vereeniging) was placed under review for closure or sale, citing unsustainable losses. This triggered union opposition and government intervention, as the steel industry is deemed strategic. In 2024, crude steel production was ~2.6 million tonnes (well below installed capacity), and the company warned it may need to either wind down or seek a fundamental restructuring partner.
Operating Facilities & Products
Vanderbijlpark Works (Gauteng)

Products: Hot-rolled coil, cold-rolled coil, galvanised sheet, tinplate

Markets: Automotive body panels, appliances, packaging (food cans), construction roofing and cladding. The most modern integrated flat-steel mill in Africa, with a direct reduced iron (DRI) plant that uses natural gas from Mozambique, making it lower-carbon than the blast furnace route.

Saldanha Works (Western Cape)

Products: Hot-rolled coil, plate

Markets: Export-oriented flat steel. Saldanha uses Midrex DRI technology and has a direct ironmaking route, avoiding coking coal. It is one of the few DRI-based steel mills in Africa and strategically located at the deep-water port of Saldanha Bay for export logistics.

Newcastle Works (KwaZulu-Natal)

Products: Long steel — reinforcing bar (rebar), wire rod, light sections

Markets: Construction (rebar for concrete reinforcement), mining (wire mesh, bolts), general engineering. Beset by low utilisation and high costs, it is the facility most at risk of closure.

Vereeniging Works (Gauteng)

Products: Rail, structural sections, special profiles

Markets: Transnet rail replacement programme, mining rail infrastructure, heavy engineering.

Why ArcelorMittal SA Matters

Steel is a foundational input into virtually every sector of a modern economy. AMSA's position is critical for several reasons:

  • Construction: Rebar, structural steel, and sheet are essential for buildings, bridges, roads, dams, and housing. Without a domestic steel industry, South Africa would import these products, exposing construction costs to global price volatility and shipping delays.
  • Automotive: AMSA supplies hot-rolled and cold-rolled steel to South Africa's automotive assembly plants (BMW, Toyota, Nissan, Ford, Volkswagen). Local automotive component supply chains depend on AMSA's flat steel products. Loss of AMSA would force automotive OEMs to import steel, increasing costs and complexity.
  • Mining: Steel grinding media, mine roof bolts, wire mesh, and structural steel are supplied to gold, platinum, and coal mines.
  • Infrastructure: Transnet's rail renewal programme, Eskom's power station construction, and water infrastructure projects all require domestically produced steel sections and rail products.
  • Employment: ~7,000 direct employees, with an estimated 30,000+ jobs dependent on the steel value chain (suppliers, transporters, downstream manufacturers).
Financial Crisis & Restructuring

AMSA has been in structural decline for over a decade. Key challenges include:

  • Chinese steel dumping: Global overcapacity in the Chinese steel industry means low-cost Chinese steel has flooded African markets. South Africa has imposed anti-dumping duties on certain steel products, but enforcement is uneven.
  • High input costs: Electricity tariffs have increased >500% over 15 years (Eskom price escalations). Logistics costs via Transnet rail and ports are high and unreliable. Labour costs are above competitor countries.
  • Weak domestic demand: Low GDP growth, construction activity, and manufacturing output have reduced steel demand. South Africa's per capita steel consumption (approximately 70 kg/year) is far below the global average (~220 kg).
  • Longs Business losses: The Newcastle and Vereeniging works have been loss-making for years. In 2023–2024, AMSA announced it would wind down the Longs Business unless a strategic partner or government support was secured. Unions and the government opposed closure, arguing that imported steel would harm construction and mining.

The company's future hinges on finding a sustainable operating model. Options under discussion include a government-supported restructuring, a joint venture with a steel trading or mining company, or a full privatisation of the Longs Business. The Department of Trade, Industry and Competition (DTIC) has identified steel as a strategic industry and is under pressure to prevent collapse.
