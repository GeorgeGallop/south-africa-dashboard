SANPC

South African National Petroleum Company — CEF's merger of PetroSA, SFF and iGas

SANPC is not a new private oil company. It is a 100% state vehicle, owned through CEF, created to collapse three overlapping subsidiaries into one national petroleum company. Cabinet approved the merger in June 2020. It became operational in 2025. PetroSA, SFF and iGas still exist on paper while people and assets transfer.

Owner: CEF SOC Ltd (100% Republic of South Africa)
Created from: PetroSA + Strategic Fuel Fund + iGas
Cabinet mandate: June 2020
Operational: 1 April / 1 May 2025 (staff and asset transfers)
Model: Lease-and-assign — SANPC operates assets without immediately inheriting PetroSA's legacy liabilities
Seed funding: ~R5 billion phased from CEF
Phase-1 staff: 402 transferred from iGas, PetroSA and SFF (30 April 2025); ~620 PetroSA staff on ring-fenced assets stay for a second wave
Priorities: Strategic stocks and imports, Mossel Bay GTL reinstatement, SAPREF rebuild, Orange Basin / 11B/12B upstream

SANPC

National petroleum company · CEF subsidiary

Why it exists

CEF spent a decade with three oil-and-gas children that duplicated boards, treasuries and mandates. PetroSA was insolvent. SFF had the 2015–16 stock-sale scandal in its past. iGas had a ROMPCO dividend but little else built. President Ramaphosa's 2020 SONA asked for SOEs to be stabilised and repurposed. The ministerial answer was: merge the three into one national oil company so the state can sit in upstream, midstream storage and gas infrastructure as a single commercial counterparty.

The business case CEF took to its boards claimed roughly R95 billion of market opportunity and R1.5–3 billion of synergy. Those are CEF's numbers, not independently audited results.

How the merger is structured

SANPC does not simply absorb PetroSA's R14.5 billion of accumulated losses and SARS bill. The chosen structure is lease-and-assign: tanks, pipes, stock and selected upstream interests are leased or assigned across; PetroSA's distressed GTL and tax fights stay ring-fenced in the "legacy" entity until they are turned around. That is why you still see PetroSA, SFF and iGas in this dashboard's CEF menu — they are the legal shells. SANPC is the operating company the state wants counterparties to deal with.

December 2024 term sheets (approved by all four boards) list SFF's Saldanha and Milnerton tank farms, Montague Gardens, Avedia, NMPP line-fill, South Sudan (conditional), cash and strategic stock (ministerial directive), plus PetroSA's PSA Ghana interest (JV and Ghanaian ministerial consent). From 1 April 2025, ICLG reports, the state's transportation assets previously in iGas and SFF sat inside SANPC, and SANPC took over strategic management of oil imports.

What it is supposed to do

  • Hold and manage strategic crude stocks and storage on behalf of the state (the SFF job, now written into the SANPC Bill language used at the May 2025 launch).
  • Be the state's upstream participant in the Orange Basin and Block 11B/12B (South Coast), rather than watching TotalEnergies and others explore alone.
  • Try to restart Mossel Bay GTL — still PetroSA's plant, now a SANPC/CEF turnaround project.
  • Support the SAPREF restart in Durban (the CEF-owned former Shell/BP refinery).
  • Collect the ROMPCO gas-pipeline economics that used to sit in iGas.

Who it answers to

SANPC is a CEF subsidiary. CEF is 100% owned by the Republic; the Minister of Mineral and Petroleum Resources holds the shares. There is no listed SANPC stock. Private oil companies may partner with it (Ghana JV, future Orange Basin, traders using tanks). They do not own it. PASA remains the licensor; AEMFC remains the miner — neither is inside this merger.

The risk

Merging three weak entities does not automatically create a strong one. PetroSA still has no audited financials since 2020 and a SARS claim against refinery assets. SAPREF is a flood-damaged plant bought for R1 because the liabilities were the point. Strategic stocks only work if the tanks are full and the crude is actually strategic, not a trading inventory. SANPC's test over the rest of the decade is whether it can import, store and eventually refine without repeating SFF's stock sale or PetroSA's GTL collapse — with CEF as the parent that has to fund the gap.
