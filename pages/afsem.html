What it is

AfSEM is the AU’s project to treat Africa as one electricity market. The 34th Assembly adopted a policy paper, a roadmap and a governance structure in February 2021. The public launch followed on 3 June 2021. The design covers all 55 AU member states.

Two jobs sit under the slogan. First, harmonise regulation so a megawatt in one pool can be scheduled, metered and paid for in another. Second, integrate generation, transmission and distribution masterplans so the CMP’s interconnectors actually have a market to land in. Continental guidelines call for an independent regulator, an independent transmission system operator and an independent distribution system operator — as a model, not as a single company in Addis running every grid.

AfSEM is an Agenda 2063 flagship under Aspiration 2 (an integrated Africa) and a named STYIP / Decade of Acceleration priority alongside AfCFTA, SAATM and the African Passport. The CMP is the sister project: physical planning. See Africa Continental Master Plan.

CMP is the backbone. AfSEM is the market. A ratified masterplan without tradable capacity is a PDF. A market without interconnectors is five pools with a continental logo. Both have to move, and they move on different clocks.

Five readiness tracks

The 2021 package is organised around five strategic documents / work areas. They are the checklist, not a claim that each one is finished.

1

Regulatory readiness

Licensing, codes, third-party access, dispute resolution. A continental independent-regulator guideline that national regulators and pool secretariats can actually use.

2

Technical / operational readiness

Grid codes, frequency and voltage, scheduling, metering, system operations across pool seams. HTOC sits here.

3

Market readiness

Day-ahead and balancing products, wheeling, credit and collateral, how a SAPP trade becomes an EAPP or WAPP trade.

4

Liberalisation / unbundling

Utility restructuring so generation, transmission and supply can contract with each other. Vertically integrated monopolies cannot host a continental market on paper alone.

5

Institutions and governance

Who decides, who monitors, who sanctions. STC-TTIIE is the political council. HTOC is the operators’ committee. CEPA is the planning desk on the CMP side.

GUIDELINE

Independent TSO / DSO / regulator

Continental model, not a single African ISO. South Africa’s domestic version of the TSO piece is NTCSA. SAWEM is the domestic market piece.

Roadmap

Phase What the 2021 roadmap said What 2025–26 actually looks like
2020–2025 Preparation and governance. First market phase “as early as 2023.” Institutions are standing up (HTOC, CEPA). The 2023 first-phase date was roadmap language, not a completed market.
2025–2040 Market build-out on the back of CMP interconnectors and pool coupling. Live work is corridor bankability plus operator committees. Full AfSEM remains a 2040 target.
2040 Fully operational single market. Still the official destination. Delivery is measured in energised lines and tradable capacity, not communiqués.
Treat “as early as 2023” as the 2021 paper’s optimism, not as a claim that phase 1 closed on time. The 2025–2026 record is HTOC and CEPA meetings, two validated pre-feasibility studies, and a 2040 operational target that has not moved.

HTOC and the political council

The Harmonisation of Transmission Operations Committee (HTOC) is AfSEM’s technical body. Pool operators and TSOs sit there to align codes, scheduling and the seams between regions.

  • Inaugural meeting — 30 June–1 July 2025.
  • Formalisation — 28–30 September 2026, Addis Ababa: governance manual and technical working groups.
  • STC-TTIIE — energy ministers, the political council above HTOC.
  • CEPA — Continental Energy Planning Agency, the CMP planning desk (workshop 3–5 June 2026; Steering Committee 5 June 2026). HTOC operates the market rules; CEPA is supposed to keep the studies moving.

Five pools remain the day-to-day operators. Dual membership (Angola and DRC in SAPP+CAPP; Tanzania and DRC in SAPP+EAPP; Libya in EAPP+COMELEC) is a feature of the map and a headache for a single set of rules.

How South Africa plugs in

Three nested markets, one country:

  • SAWEM — the domestic wholesale market being stood up. System marginal price, vesting, market code. See SAWEM and Market Structure.
  • NTCSA — the independent transmission system operator. This is Pretoria’s version of the AfSEM TSO guideline. See NTCSA.
  • SAPP — the regional pool. Day-ahead and other SAPP products are the existing cross-border market South Africa already sits in. See SAPP.
  • AfSEM — the continental layer those three would eventually report into. Not live. 2040 is the target.

The CMP corridor that makes this more than a governance diagram is DRC–South Africa (Inga 3): ~3,000 MW over 3,000+ km. Until that line, or ZTK plus the East African spine, carries firm energy, AfSEM for Pretoria is SAPP plus a continental committee. Creditworthy offtake, a working TSO and a transmission code neighbours will accept are the binding constraints — the same ones this dashboard already tracks under SAWEM and NTCSA.

What still blocks a single market

Wires

Most pool seams are thin or missing. ZTK is due around 2029. Inga 3 transmission is still pre-feasibility. North Core and Kenya–Tanzania are the exceptions already in construction.

Utilities

Many members still run vertically integrated, cash-strapped utilities. AfSEM’s unbundling track is the politically hardest of the five.

Credit

A continental market needs counterparties that pay. Offtaker risk is why CEPA’s first job is bankability of two interconnectors, not a day-ahead screen in Addis.

Rules

Five pools, overlapping membership, different codes. HTOC exists because the codes do not yet agree. Harmonisation is a multi-year operators’ fight, not a summit decision.
