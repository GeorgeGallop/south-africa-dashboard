📈 The Big Picture: Demand Hasn't Fallen — It Shifted

Eskom reports declining electricity sales, but the country's actual electricity consumption may not have fallen at all. A growing share is simply generated behind the meter — invisible to Eskom's data.

⚠️ The Hidden Demand

When a mine or factory installs rooftop solar and reduces its Eskom purchases by 50%, that looks like a "demand reduction" in Eskom's sales data. But the mine is still consuming the same amount of electricity — it's just generating it on-site. The apparent decline in electricity demand is largely a measurement artifact.

Eskom-reported demand (2025): ~185 TWh/yr (down from ~225 TWh peak in 2010)
Estimated true consumption: ~210–220 TWh/yr when adding ~25–35 TWh of behind-the-meter generation
✅ Why This Changes Everything

The move to private generation fundamentally reshapes Eskom's business model, the financial health of municipalities, and the planning assumptions in the IRP and TDP. Key effects:

  • Eskom's "death spiral": As large customers leave the grid, fixed costs are spread over fewer remaining customers, pushing tariffs higher
  • Municipal revenue loss: Municipalities rely on electricity resale margins for ~30% of their revenue. Private generation erodes this.
  • Grid stability: Behind-the-meter solar reduces daytime load but creates steep evening ramps (the "duck curve")
  • IRP assumptions: Official demand forecasts may significantly underestimate true consumption, leading to under-investment
📈 Explosion of Private Generation: NERSA Registered Capacity

Before 2021, private generation >1 MW required a licence. The 2021 reform raised the threshold to 100 MW, unleashing a flood of registered projects. By mid-2026, over 19,600 MW of private capacity had been registered — equivalent to ~43% of Eskom's total installed coal fleet.

Source: NERSA registration data, SAPVIA. Note: registered capacity includes projects at various stages (pre-licensed, under construction, operational). Behind-the-meter solar (NTCSA estimate) is separate and may partially overlap with NERSA figures.
📈 The Eskom "Death Spiral"

Eskom itself has used the term "death spiral" to describe its revenue trajectory. The mechanism is straightforward and self-reinforcing:

⚠️ 1. Eskom tariffs rise sharply (900% since 2008)
↓
🌎 2. Large customers invest in solar + battery (LCOE ~R0.50–0.60 vs Eskom R2+/kWh)
↓
📈 3. Customers reduce Eskom purchases → Eskom revenue declines
↓
💰 4. Fixed grid costs spread over fewer kWh → tariffs rise further
↓
📈 5. More customers defect → spiral accelerates
Key metric: The levelized cost of solar in South Africa is now R0.50–0.60/kWh vs Eskom's tariffs of R2.00+/kWh. The economics are a "complete commercial no-brainer" — Richard Doyle, JUWI South Africa MD.
📅 Policy & Market Reform Timeline
2011

REIPPPP launched — First bid window for utility-scale renewables. Government procurement drives initial growth. Solar PV prices fall from ~R3.50/kWh to ~R0.50/kWh by BW6.

2019

Section 34 Ministerial Determinations — Allowed municipalities and private offtakers to procure their own power directly from IPPs. Broke Eskom's monopsony.

2021

Licensing threshold raised to 100 MW — The trigger event. Private generation projects up to 100 MW no longer required a generation licence. This was the single most impactful reform, unleashing the embedded generation boom.

2022

Eskom unbundling announced — Legal separation into Generation, Transmission (NTCSA), and Distribution entities. Paves way for third-party access to the grid and competitive electricity trading.

2023

Discovery Green launched — A renewable-energy trading platform enabling corporate wheeling. Discovery (insurance giant) enters the electricity market. Impala Platinum becomes anchor customer.

2024

NERSA registrations surpass 10,000 MW — Private generation capacity registered exceeds Eskom's Medupi + Kusile combined. Eskom Green launched, targeting 6,000 MW utility-scale renewables.

2025–2026

Behind-the-meter solar reaches 8,400+ MW — NTCSA estimate. NERSA registered capacity surpasses 19,600 MW. 40+ JSE-listed companies announce renewable energy plans. The market is fundamentally transformed.

💰 Cost Comparison: Eskom vs Private Generation
📈 Why the economics are irreversible:
  • Solar LCOE (2026): R0.50–0.60/kWh — and falling as module prices decline
  • Eskom peak tariff (2025/26): R6.85/kWh (under Megaflex, <500V, high season)
  • Eskom blended average: ~R1.40–1.80/kWh for large industrial customers
  • Solar + battery (24/7): R0.90–1.20/kWh — still cheaper than Eskom peak
  • Payback period: 3–5 years for commercial solar installations
  • Certainty premium: Solar costs rise with CPI only (2–6% pa). Eskom tariffs rise at ~12.5% pa. The gap widens every year.
🌎 The Duck Curve: Grid Impact of Behind-the-Meter Solar

As more businesses and homes generate solar power during the day, Eskom faces a structural shift in its load profile:

  • Midday trough: Solar generation depresses net demand by up to 5,000–8,000 MW during peak sun hours
  • Evening ramp: When the sun sets (5–8 PM), solar drops off rapidly just as household demand peaks. This creates a steep "ramp" requiring 4,000–6,000 MW of fast-ramping capacity in just 2–3 hours
  • Coal inflexibility: Eskom's coal fleet cannot ramp quickly. Result: over-generation midday (coal plants can't shut down), and gas/diesel peakers must cover the evening ramp

Key implication: The duck curve means that even as total Eskom sales decline, the peaking capacity requirements may not decline proportionally. This is the central challenge of integrating high shares of behind-the-meter solar into a coal-dominated grid.
