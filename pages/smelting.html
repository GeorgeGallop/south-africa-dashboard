Smelting

The metallurgical subsection of manufacturing that converts mineral concentrates into liquid metal and alloy feedstock.

What smelting is

Smelting is the high-temperature process that separates valuable metal from mineral ore or concentrate. It is one of the core subsections of South Africa’s metals manufacturing chain and the first stage in turning mining output into refined alloys and finished products.

Smelting typically combines heat, reductants and fluxes to melt the feedstock, drive chemical reduction, and separate metal from slag and other impurities.

Core smelting steps
  • Concentration: ore is crushed, milled and upgraded into a higher-grade concentrate.
  • Roasting and drying: heats the concentrate to remove water, sulfur and volatile contaminants.
  • Reduction: metal oxides and sulphides are reduced using carbon, natural gas, electricity or other reducing agents.
  • Fluxing: limestone, silica, dolomite and other fluxes are added to form slag and capture impurities.
  • Melting and separation: the furnace melts the material, separating the denser metal from lighter slag.
  • Refining preparation: the crude metal is prepared for downstream refining and alloying.
Furnace types and material flows

Different metals use different furnace technologies. In South Africa, the typical smelting equipment includes blast furnaces for iron, submerged arc furnaces for ferroalloys, flash smelters for copper and PGM matte, and reverberatory or electric furnaces for specialty metals.

The process flow is always similar: feedstock enters the furnace, chemical reactions occur, slag is removed, and a crude metal or matte product is prepared for refining.

Commodity-specific smelting
Platinum Group Metals (PGMs)

PGM smelting begins with concentrated nickel-copper sulphide ores. The concentrate is smelted into matte, then converted and refined to separate platinum, palladium, rhodium and other precious metals.

Manganese

Manganese ore is smelted in submerged arc furnaces to produce ferromanganese and silicomanganese alloys used by the steel industry.

Chrome

Chromite concentrate is smelted into ferrochrome, a key input for stainless steel, using coke and fluxes to reduce chromium oxide.

Iron Ore

Iron ore is smelted in blast furnaces or direct reduced iron plants to make pig iron or DRI, which are then converted into steel.

Vanadium

Vanadium is recovered from titania magnetite and converted into ferrovanadium or vanadium pentoxide through roasting, leaching and reduction steps that follow smelting principles.

Why smelting matters

Smelting is the gateway between mining and manufacturing. It produces the metal and alloy feedstocks that are essential for refining, steelmaking, petrochemicals, automotive components and international exports.

For South Africa, strong smelting capability is a strategic advantage because it captures more value domestically before products leave the country.
