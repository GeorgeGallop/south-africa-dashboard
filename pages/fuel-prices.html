⚖️ How South Africa Regulates Fuel Prices

South Africa is one of the few countries in the world that sets petrol prices by law. You cannot shop around for cheaper petrol — every station in a zone charges the same regulated price. Here's how the system works, who runs it, and what it means for you.

🏛️ Who Sets the Price & the Legal Basis

Fuel prices in South Africa are governed by the Petroleum Products Act (Act 120 of 1977) and administered by the Department of Mineral and Petroleum Resources (DMPR) — formerly the DMRE. The Minister of Mineral and Petroleum Resources is legally responsible for setting the maximum prices of petrol, diesel, illuminating paraffin, and LPG. The Minister is advised by the Petroleum Products Pricing Committee and the Petroleum Consumer Liaison Committee. The price schedule is officially published in the Government Gazette.

🧮 The Formula: The Basic Fuel Price (BFP)

The core of the price is the Basic Fuel Price (BFP) — the cost of importing fuel into South Africa. The country uses import parity pricing: the price is set as if every drop of fuel were imported at the international benchmark rate, even for locally-refined fuel. The BFP is calculated daily using:

  • International oil prices (Platts & Argus benchmarks for petrol, diesel, jet, paraffin)
  • The rand/US dollar exchange rate — a weaker rand automatically raises fuel prices
  • Ocean freight, insurance, and shipping costs to the coastal ports
💰 What's Added On Top: Taxes & Levies

Once the BFP is calculated, the government adds a stack of taxes and levies. These make up roughly 35–40% of the pump price. They include:

  • Fuel Levy — a general national levy (goes to the fiscus)
  • Road Accident Fund (RAF) Levy — funds compensation for road crash victims
  • Customs & Excise Duty
  • Oil Pipeline Levy
  • Petroleum Products Levy / IP Levy for diesel
  • Then the wholesale & retail margins and transport/zone costs are added
📍 Coastal vs Inland (Gauteng) — Why the Two Zones

South Africa divides the country into pricing zones. The two main ones on this data are Coastal (ocean ports where fuel lands) and Inland / Gauteng (the economic heartland, 1,500+ km from the coast). Inland prices are higher because the cost of transporting fuel by pipeline and road from the ports to inland depots is added to the price. That's why 95 ULP in Gauteng is a couple of hundred cents more per litre than at the coast.

Historically, the coast used 95 ULP and the inland used both 93 ULP and 95 ULP. Petrol comes in 93 and 95 octane; LRP (Lead Replacement Petrol) was for older engines, and ULP (Unleaded Petrol) is the modern standard.

📅 When Prices Are Announced

Fuel prices are adjusted on the first Wednesday of every month and take effect at 00:01 that morning. The announcement comes from the DMPR Minister (or a senior departmental official) in the last week of the preceding month — usually on the Wednesday or Thursday before the new prices kick in. The date is fixed in the regulations, so there is no "surprise" adjustment mid-month. The schedule below shows the actual effective dates for 2024–2026.

🔍 What It Means For You
  • It's a maximum, not an exact price. The law sets a maximum retail price. Retailers may charge less (rarely) but never more.
  • Diesel & paraffin are set at wholesale list price (per the notes), while petrol is at retail price and LPG at maximum retail price.
  • You can't bargain at the pump. Because the price is regulated, competition is on location and service, not price.
  • The rand and world oil price are the big drivers. When the rand weakens or crude spikes (like the 2022 Russia-Ukraine surge), pump prices jump — you see it in the 2022 spike on the charts below.
  • Government gets a big slice. Around a third to 40% of what you pay is tax and levies.
  • LPG is measured in cents per kilogram, not per litre, because it's sold by weight in cylinders and bulk.
