Eskom Restructuring Task Team (ERTT)

The Presidency's cross-government task team driving the break-up of Eskom and the creation of an independent Transmission System Operator (TSO)

The ERTT is the coordinating body that turns President Ramaphosa's political promise — a fully independent, state-owned transmission company — into a technically detailed, financially survivable restructuring plan. It sits at the centre of the puzzle: it connects the Presidency, National Treasury, the electricity ministry, Eskom, and the transmission company, and its job is to sequence the unbundling so that Eskom's R400bn+ debt, its lenders, its municipalities, and the broader market reform all move together without breaking anything.

Established: Early March 2026
Announced: 2026 State of the Nation Address
Chair: Director-General of National Treasury
Members: The Presidency, National Treasury, Department of Electricity and Energy, Eskom, NTCSA
Phase I report: Approved by President (July 2026)
Phase II focus: Financial sustainability, municipal arrear debt (R119bn), obligations to lenders
Statutory anchor: Electricity Regulation Act — TSO to be established by no later than 31 December 2029

Who They Are

During the 2026 State of the Nation Address, President Cyril Ramaphosa announced a dedicated task team to oversee the restructuring of Eskom — specifically to establish a fully independent, state-owned Transmission System Operator (TSO) that will own and control transmission assets, operate the electricity market, enable transmission investment at scale, and provide non-discriminatory access to the grid.

The task team was formally established at the beginning of March 2026, comprising senior representatives from:

  • The Presidency — the political mandate and the authority to make the reform stick.
  • National Treasury — the chair; controls the purse strings, the R254bn debt relief conditions, and the country's fiscal risk exposure to Eskom's R400bn+ debt.
  • Department of Electricity and Energy — the policy owner and the minister responsible for electricity market reform (Minister Kgosientsho Ramokgopa).
  • Eskom — the utility being restructured; protective of its financial position and fiduciary duties.
  • National Transmission Company South Africa (NTCSA) — the Eskom subsidiary that currently performs TSO functions and is the institutional seed of the future TSO.

The ERTT is chaired by the Director-General of National Treasury, which signals that this is above all a financial-and-institutional sequencing problem, not merely an engineering one.

Their Mandate

Per the Presidency, the ERTT is tasked with:

  • Developing a detailed proposal and implementation plan for establishing an independent, state-owned TSO separate from Eskom that will assume ownership and control of transmission assets, operate the electricity market, enable transmission investment at scale, and provide non-discriminatory access to the grid.
  • Considering the optimal institutional model for the TSO, drawing on international best practice and ensuring full alignment with the Electricity Regulation Act (ERA).
  • Addressing measures to ensure adequate independence of the NTCSA during the period before the TSO is established.
  • Recommending where responsibility for grid capacity allocation should sit, to guarantee independent and non-discriminatory treatment of grid users — both during the transitional period and once the TSO exists.

Its Terms of Reference also set out the non-negotiables that constrain every option the team can propose:

  • Maintain energy security;
  • Ensure full independence of the TSO from all market participants;
  • Ownership of the transmission network separated from Eskom;
  • Eskom must not be worse off financially; the TSO must be financially sustainable;
  • The TSO must raise the funding required for investment per the Transmission Development Plan;
  • Avoid qualified audit opinions for Eskom, the NTCSA or the TSO and address lender requirements to avoid default;
  • Minimise the impact on South Africa's fiscal position;
  • Prevent undue financial burden on electricity users;
  • Promote electricity market reform, including independent transmission projects (ITPs).
How They Fit Into the Puzzle

South Africa's electricity reform is a multi-track process, and the ERTT is the thread that ties the tracks together:

🏢 The Eskom Unbundling

Eskom is splitting into Generation (GenCo), Transmission (the future TSO / current NTCSA), and Distribution (DistCo). The ERTT is responsible for the transmission leg — the hardest one, because the grid is the shared asset every market participant depends on. Whoever controls the grid controls market entry.

🏛️ The Legal Clock

The Electricity Regulation Act requires a TSO by no later than 31 December 2029. In the interim, NTCSA — an Eskom subsidiary established in July 2024 — carries out the TSO functions. The ERTT has to design the path from "subsidiary of Eskom" to "fully independent state-owned company" before the statutory deadline.

💰 The Debt & Bailout Conditions

Eskom carries R400bn+ of debt and receives a R254bn Treasury bailout (2024–2026) conditional on reforms — including progress on unbundling. The ERTT must restructure the transmission assets without triggering a default, a qualified audit, or a downgrade. That is why Eskom's board insists the reform be "carefully sequenced with clear stage gates", and why Treasury insists on financial sustainability as a hard constraint.

🏘️ Municipal Debt (R119bn)

Municipalities owe Eskom roughly R119 billion in arrear debt — a crisis that prevents Eskom from servicing its own obligations. Phase II of the ERTT proposes a dedicated workstream to solve municipal arrear debt. This is the player that links the electricity reform to local government finance and service delivery.

⚡ The Wholesale Market (SAWEM)

A competitive wholesale electricity market cannot function if the system operator is owned by the dominant generator — it would have an inherent conflict of interest. An independent TSO is the precondition for the SAWEM market structure, open grid access, independent transmission projects (ITPs), and private investment in grid capacity. The ERTT is effectively building the referee for the future market.

🌍 International Precedent

More than 90 countries have reformed their electricity sectors into competitive wholesale markets, and nearly all unbundled transmission and system operation from the incumbent generator. The ERTT is tasked with drawing on this international best practice while adapting it to South Africa's fiscal, legal and political realities.

Two Phases of Work
  • Phase I — High-level proposal for the TSO: Originally due end of May 2026, the deadline was extended to end of June 2026 to allow the approach to be fully detailed and considered through governance structures. The President approved the Phase I Report in July 2026.
  • Phase II — Detailed implementation plan: To be completed within about three months of Phase I. Eskom has confirmed Phase II focuses on financial sustainability, municipal arrear debt, and obligations to lenders, including a dedicated workstream for municipal debt and careful engagement with lenders, since establishing an independent TSO is a material event for Eskom's financing arrangements.
Status at a glance (Aug 2026): Phase I approved by the President. Eskom's board supports the TSO vision but insists on sequencing, stage gates and no erosion of its financial position. Phase II implementation planning underway, with municipal-debt workstream proposed. TSO statutory deadline: 31 December 2029.
