Strategic Fuel Fund (SFF)

The state's strategic crude oil stocks and tank farms — a CEF subsidiary, merging into SANPC

SFF holds South Africa's emergency crude. It is a non-profit company whose shares are non-transferable and held by CEF. The Republic is the only ultimate owner. From 1 April 2025 its operating assets have been folding into the South African National Petroleum Company under a lease-and-assign model.

Legal form: Non-profit company; shares held by CEF SOC Ltd (100% state)
Mandate: Acquire and hold strategic crude stocks and related infrastructure for supply emergencies
Core assets: Saldanha in-ground tanks (6 × 7.5 million barrels = 45 million barrels); Milnerton tank farm; Montague Gardens depot (BP joint asset); Ogies; NMPP line-fill
Also: Oil Pollution Control SA (OPCSA) — port and coastal spill response
Staff: ~200
Audit (2023/24): Unqualified opinion, with PFMA non-compliance findings

SFF

Strategic stocks · Saldanha · Milnerton · OPCSA

What it is

SFF is the successor to the sanctions-era stockpile. When apartheid South Africa could not rely on open oil markets, the state built giant in-ground tanks at Saldanha Bay and filled them with crude. After 1994 the Strategic Fuel Fund Association kept that job: buy, store, rotate and, in a crisis, release crude so inland refineries and the economy do not seize up.

It is not a refiner and not an explorer. It is a storage-and-stocks company. Parliament's 2025 Western Cape oversight report records six Saldanha tanks of 7.5 million barrels each — 45 million barrels of theoretical capacity — plus Milnerton, a Cape Town-area tank farm, the Montague Gardens depot (a BP joint asset), Ogies inland, and NMPP line-fill. OPCSA, often grouped with SFF, does oil-pollution control in Saldanha and other ports under NEMA.

What it is used for

  • Strategic crude stocks — a national insurance policy against import disruption, war, or a tanker strike.
  • Commercial storage — unused tank space has been leased to traders. That commercialisation is how SFF earns fees, and how it got into trouble (see below).
  • Coastal oil-spill response — OPCSA.
  • Feedstock logistics — tanks and line-fill that sit in front of Cape and, via the national pipeline, inland markets.

Who it serves / who "buys"

The customer of last resort is the South African state. In normal times the offtakers are refiners and traders who store crude in SFF tanks. There is no private controlling owner. CEF is the shareholder; the Minister of Mineral and Petroleum Resources sits above CEF.

The 2015–16 stock sale

SFF's public reputation is still defined by the sale of strategic crude in 2015–16, when millions of barrels were disposed of to private buyers at a large loss to the state. Courts later set parts of the transaction aside. It is the SFF version of state-capture-era looting: a strategic stockpile treated as a trading book. That episode is why later boards and the Auditor-General have been so pointed about PFMA compliance, and why the SANPC merger was sold as a clean-up of overlapping, weakly governed CEF oil entities.

SANPC transfer

Term sheets approved in December 2024 by the boards of SFF, iGas, PetroSA and SANPC cover: the Milnerton and Saldanha tank farms, Montague Gardens, Avedia Energy (subject to s54 approval), NMPP line-fill, South Sudan interests (subject to Khartoum/Juba endorsement), cash, and strategic stock (subject to a ministerial directive). Phase-one staff moved to SANPC at the end of April 2025. SFF as a legal NPC still exists while the lease-and-assign completes.
