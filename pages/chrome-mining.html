Chrome Mining

Who mines it, where the pits and shafts are, and what kind of mines they are
Overview Mining Transport Smelting Ferrochrome Industry End Markets

South Africa mines more chromite than any other country — on recent figures around half to 60% of world ore, from a geology that holds about 70% of known reserves. The ore sits in the Bushveld Igneous Complex. The industry has split: mines and exporters are running hard, while the furnaces that used to take that ore are mostly idle. That split is covered on the smelting page. This page is the rock in the ground.

Where it is

Almost all of it is in two limbs of the Bushveld:

  • Eastern limb — Steelpoort, Burgersfort, Lydenburg, Dwarsrivier (Limpopo / Mpumalanga). This is the classic chrome valley: thick LG and MG chromitite seams, a mix of underground and open-pit, and the Lion smelter sitting next to the ore.
  • Western limb — Rustenburg, Brits, Marikana, Boshoek (North West). Here chrome is both a primary product and a UG2 by-product of PGM mining. Tharisa's open pit at Marikana is the flagship integrated chrome-plus-PGM mine.

The seams that matter are the LG6, MG1 and UG2 chromitite layers. LG/MG are mined as chrome. UG2 is mined as a PGM reef that happens to be a chromitite — so every PGM mill on UG2 also produces a chrome concentrate. That is why Sibanye, Implats and the Glencore-Merafe PGM-adjacent plants are chrome miners even when their letterhead says platinum.

What type of mines

Type Where / who What you get
Underground conventional Eastern Chrome Mines (Samancor), Dwarsrivier (ARM), many Glencore-Merafe shafts (Thorncliffe, Helena, Magareng) Narrow, consistent LG/MG seams. Labour-intensive. Lowest-cost ore when the seam is thick and close to infrastructure.
Open pit Tharisa (Marikana); some eastern-limb opencasts; weathered outcrop Bulk mining, chrome plus PGMs in one pit. Hedges the two prices against each other.
UG2 PGM co-product Sibanye-Stillwater, Implats, Glencore-Merafe UG2 plants, Northam Chrome recovered from the PGM concentrator. Volume follows PGM mill feed, not a chrome price signal.
Tailings / dump recovery Sylvania, Sibanye chrome plants (12 recovery plants, six owned), historical dumps Fine chrome from old PGM tailings. Low incremental mining cost; the "DRDGOLD of chrome."

Illegal mining is not a footnote. Estimates put stolen chrome around 600,000 tonnes a year — roughly a tenth of legal output — laundered into the same trucks heading for Durban, Richards Bay or Maputo.

Who mines

The industry is not one company. It is two integrated ferrochrome majors plus a set of ore-only and PGM-chrome players who now often make more money selling ore than the majors make smelting it.

Glencore–Merafe Chrome Venture

The listed South African face is Merafe Resources (~20.5% of the venture); Glencore holds the rest and operates. Mines on both limbs (Kroondal, Thorncliffe, Helena, Magareng and others) feed the Lion, Wonderkop and Boshoek smelters. In 2025 the mines kept producing (~932 kt attributable chrome ore) while ferrochrome collapsed. Chrome-ore revenue, not alloy, carried the profit.

Samancor Chrome

Historically the world's largest ferrochrome name (Gencor/BHP, then a standalone). Eastern Chrome Mines in the Steelpoort valley and Western Chrome Mines on the western limb. Integrated into its own furnaces (Ferrometals/Witbank, Tubatse, Middelburg, Rustenburg). Same 2025–26 story as Glencore: ore running, furnaces mostly not, until the 62c/kWh Eskom tariff.

Tharisa

Open-pit chrome and PGM mine at Marikana, North West. The integrated model: one pit, two products. Not a big smelter. Sells chrome ore into the export market. The type of producer that benefits when China wants ore and South African furnaces are off.

ARM / Assore (Dwarsrivier) and others

African Rainbow Minerals' Dwarsrivier on the eastern limb is a high-quality underground chrome mine. Assore/Assmang historically sat in the same neighbourhood. Non-integrated: they mine and sell ore rather than running a large furnace book.

PGM houses — Sibanye, Implats, Northam

UG2 chrome is a by-product that has become a revenue line. Sibanye has 12 chrome recovery plants (six owned; five Glencore) and a 2025 chrome management agreement with the Glencore–Merafe JV. Chrome rose from ~3% of Sibanye SA revenue in 2020 to ~8% in 2025. The company wants to be a top-five global chrome recovery name — tailings first, not new pits.

Dump retreatment — Sylvania and juniors

Sylvania and similar operators retreat PGM dumps for chrome and PGMs. Bauba, Chrome SA and a long tail of smaller eastern-limb names add tonnes. They are the reason South African chrome supply can keep rising even when deep-level PGM shafts close.

The two-speed industry

Mines are not the problem. Sibanye puts national chrome production around 26 million tonnes (~61% of world supply), of which only about 13% is smelted at home. The rest goes to China and Indonesia as ore. That is a mining success and a beneficiation failure at the same time. Who mines, where, and in what kind of hole is settled. Whether those tonnes see a South African furnace is the smelting page.
