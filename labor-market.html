👷 Labour Market

Employment, unemployment, working-age population, and the structural crisis of joblessness in South Africa — the most unequal labour market in the world.

Overview

South Africa's labour market is one of the most structurally dysfunctional in the world. The official unemployment rate has been above 25% for over two decades and reached 33% by 2024. The expanded (broad) unemployment rate, which includes discouraged workers who have stopped looking, sits at over 42%. Among 15-34 year-olds, the rate exceeds 60%.

The core problem is not that South Africans have lost jobs in aggregate — total employment has grown from ~9 million in 1994 to ~16 million today. The problem is that the working-age population has grown far faster — from ~22 million to over 40 million in the same period. The result: a vast and growing pool of people who have never had a formal job and are increasingly disconnected from the labour market entirely.

This is a crisis of insufficient job creation relative to population growth, not a crisis of mass job destruction (though certain sectors — mining, manufacturing, farming — have seen major losses). The economy simply does not generate enough formal-sector employment to absorb the millions of young South Africans entering the labour market each year.
