Revised Electricity Pricing Policy (EPP) 2026

The Cabinet-approved replacement of the 2008 Electricity Pricing Policy — the price rulebook for SAWEM.

Open for comment

AuthorityDepartment of Electricity and Energy (DEE)
Issued28 August 2026
Comments due28 September 2026 (Energy Council members by 23 September 2026)
ReplacesElectricity Pricing Policy, 2008
Sits next toDEE Market Transformation position paper (21 Aug 2026)
PDFRevised EPP 2026 (Energy Council)

What it is

The EPP tells NERSA how electricity prices must be designed. The 2026 revision is written for a competitive wholesale market, not a vertically integrated Eskom. Minister Ramokgopa’s public framing: if the cost of electricity is not fixed, industry stays uncompetitive and the economy does not grow.

What it changes

  • Transparency and cost reflectivity — tariffs must unbundle energy, network, legacy and subsidy components so customers can see what they pay for.
  • No pass-through of inefficiency — Eskom (and municipal distributors) cannot recover costs NERSA judges inefficient.
  • Ten-year price path — NERSA must publish a 10-year electricity price forecast so industry can plan.
  • Free basic electricity — qualifying poor households move from 50 kWh/month to 200–300 kWh/month. DEE says this is not a new Treasury fiscal line; it is recovered in the tariff structure.

What to watch

Comment window is the live date. After 28 September, this joins the consolidation pile. MYPD 7 (from FY2029) will be the first full revenue determination written against the new EPP. Read it with the wholesale methodology and the vesting framework.
