Import Tariffs

A snapshot of South Africa’s current import tariff landscape, sector charges, recent changes, and revenue implications.

Overview

South Africa applies a combination of customs duties, Value Added Tax (VAT), excise duties, and additional trade measures on imports. Tariffs are designed both to protect local manufacturing and agriculture, and to allow duty-free access for key capital goods and inputs.

The standard VAT rate is 15% on imported goods, charged on the cost, insurance, freight (CIF) value plus duty and other charges. Customs duties are typically ad valorem and range from 0% to 45%, with higher protection levels for selected agricultural and manufactured goods.

Typical import charges
  • Customs duty: ad valorem rates by tariff code, generally 0–45%.
  • VAT: 15% on CIF value plus duty and any excise.
  • Excise duty: levied on alcohol, tobacco, fuel, sugary drinks, and some luxury goods.
  • Anti-dumping/safeguard duties: additional on selected steel, chemical, and textile imports.
  • Port, handling and clearing fees: separate service charges applied at ports of entry.

Recent updates

Recent tariff reviews have tended to maintain protection for sensitive sectors such as clothing, footwear, textiles, sugar, poultry and agriculture, while allowing more liberal access for capital goods and renewable energy equipment.

Key updates include:

  • Continuation of protective duties on apparel and textiles to support local manufacturing.
  • Maintained or increased duties on sugar and poultry to safeguard domestic food producers.
  • Expanded zero-duty treatment for renewable energy infrastructure and certain industrial machinery.
  • Use of anti-dumping measures in steel, chemicals, and textiles to prevent unfairly priced imports.
Revenue context

Customs duties themselves are one component of South Africa’s import tax take. VAT on imports is typically the larger single contributor, while excise duties on alcohol, tobacco and fuel add significant additional receipts.

Estimated sector contributions are approximate, but the strongest import revenue comes from:

  • Automotive imports: large CIF values plus VAT and duty on vehicles and components.
  • Alcohol and tobacco: high excise and VAT collections.
  • Apparel and textiles: protective rates and strong duty receipts.

Overall, import-related tax revenue is likely in the tens of billions of rand annually, with the combination of customs duty, VAT, excise and anti-dumping duties supporting government collections while protecting strategic local industries.
