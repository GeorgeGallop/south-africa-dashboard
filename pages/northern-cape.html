Province profile

Northern Cape

The Northern Cape is South Africa’s largest province by land area, defined by vast semi-arid landscapes, historic diamond mining, and some of the country’s best solar resources.

At a glance

  • Capital: Kimberley
  • Population: About 1.3 million
  • Largest city: Kimberley
  • Known for: Mining and renewable energy

Population

With roughly 1.3 million residents, the Northern Cape is the least populated province. Its towns are widely spaced across a large land area, creating a distinctly rural settlement pattern.

Brief history

The province is closely tied to the diamond rush in Kimberley, the histories of the Nama and San peoples, and frontier-era trade routes across the interior plateau.

Key industries

Mining, solar energy, sheep and goat farming, transport, and tourism form the backbone of the provincial economy.

Major cities & towns

Kimberley, Upington, Springbok, Kuruman, Kathu, De Aar, and Calvinia are key administrative and service centres.

Climate

The Northern Cape is mostly arid to semi-arid, with hot summer days, cold winter nights, and low rainfall. Conditions become more temperate in the far south and along the Orange River.

Natural resources

Diamonds, manganese, iron ore, copper, salt, vast solar exposure, and extensive grazing land make the province strategically important.

Future outlook

The Northern Cape is well placed for growth in renewable energy, green hydrogen, mining beneficiation, and transport corridors that connect inland resources to ports and markets.
