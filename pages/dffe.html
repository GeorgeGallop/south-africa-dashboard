🌿 Department of Forestry, Fisheries & the Environment (DFFE)

South Africa’s department responsible for protecting, conserving and improving the environment and natural resources.

What it is & function
  • Manages and protects South Africa’s environment and natural resources.
  • Leads waste policy and planning through the National Environmental Management: Waste Act framework.
  • Supports programmes that strengthen waste services, compliance, and environmental protection.
Who works there

DFFE is staffed by South African public servants (civil servants) working across specialist clusters such as forestry, fisheries, environmental management, and chemicals & waste policy.

Headcount / establishment: 4,067 permanent posts (establishment figure captured from the DFFE annual report).

Budget (latest available)

For 2025/26, DFFE’s budget allocation is R9.08 billion (Vote 32 allocation).

When it started
  • 2019 (creation): DFFE was created in 2019 via the merger of environmental functions with forestry and fisheries components.
  • 2021 (name alignment): In April 2021, the department adopted the DFFE name formally.

Current initiative (example): NWMS 2026 consultation

Consultation on the Draft National Waste Management Strategy (NWMS) 2026

Through a Government Gazette notice, DFFE published the draft National Waste Management Strategy 2026 for public comment. The draft provides government policy and strategic interventions for the waste sector and is aligned to the UN SDGs (Agenda 2030) and South Africa’s Vision 2030.

Strategic pillars (as described in the draft)
  • Circular Economy & Waste Minimisation
  • Effective & Sustainable Waste Services
  • Capacity Building & Awareness
  • Compliance Monitoring & Enforcement
Priority waste streams highlighted
  • Absorbent Hygiene Products (AHPs)
  • Organic waste
  • Clothing & textiles
  • Automobiles
  • Coal ash
  • Construction & Demolition waste
  • Food waste
