Sibanye-Stillwater Ltd

Global Precious Metals Mining Company — PGM & Gold Smelting, Refining & Downstream Processing

Sibanye-Stillwater is one of the world's largest primary producers of platinum group metals (PGMs) and a significant gold producer, with mining, smelting, and refining operations in South Africa and the United States. The company was formed through the demerger of Gold Fields' South African gold assets in 2013 and has grown through aggressive acquisition to become a diversified global precious metals powerhouse. It operates integrated smelters and refineries that process PGM concentrates into refined metals and chemicals.

History & Origins
  • 2013: Sibanye Gold was founded through the demerger of Gold Fields' mature South African gold assets — primarily Driefontein, Kloof, and Beatrix. The name "Sibanye" means "we are together" in isiZulu/isiXhosa. The demerger created a focused gold mining company with deep-level, high-cost operations.
  • 2015: Sibanye acquired the Rustenburg PGM operations from Anglo American Platinum (the Khuseleka, Khomanani, and Thembelani shafts). This was a transformational deal that diversified the company into PGMs and made it a significant platinum producer overnight.
  • 2016–2017: Acquisition of Aquarius Platinum (which brought the Kroondal, Marikana and Mimosa operations). Then the acquisition of Stillwater Mining Company (Montana, USA) — the only significant PGM mining operation outside of South Africa. Stillwater produces palladium and platinum from the Stillwater and East Boulder mines in the J-M Reef.
  • 2020s: Sibanye-Stillwater became a global precious metals leader. It acquired the Lonmin operations in 2019 (adding the Marikana mine and smelter complex) and expanded into battery metals through acquisitions in nickel, lithium, and copper projects — including the Keliber lithium project in Finland and a nickel refinery in Belgium.
  • 2023–2024: The company faced significant headwinds — a sharp decline in palladium and rhodium prices (which fell >70% from their 2021 peaks), operational challenges at the US operations (Stillwater), and a major strike at the South African gold operations over retrenchments. The company posted a substantial loss in FY2024 and announced a strategic review of non-core assets, including the gold operations.
South African Operations
PGM Operations (Rustenburg & Marikana)

Shafts: Kroondal, K6, Marikana (formerly Lonmin), and the BRPM (Bafokeng Rasimone) joint venture — all on the western limb of the Bushveld Igneous Complex.

Processing: The Marikana smelter complex includes a PGM concentrator, a smelter that produces PGM matte, and the precious metals refinery that converts matte into refined platinum, palladium, rhodium, and gold. The refinery also recovers nickel and copper.

Output: ~1.8 million ounces of 2E PGMs (platinum + palladium) per year from SA operations.

Gold Operations (Witwatersrand)

Driefontein: One of the world's richest gold mines, operating since 1952. Deep-level operations (>3 km).

Kloof: Another major deep-level gold mine in the West Wits line, in production since the 1960s.

Beatrix: Gold mine in the Free State, targeting the Beatrix Reef. Lower grade but mechanised for cost efficiency.

Output: ~500,000 ounces of gold per year. These are among the world's deepest and highest-cost gold mines, and their future is under strategic review.

Smelting & Refining Capabilities

Sibanye-Stillwater's smelting and refining infrastructure is concentrated at two major sites:

  • Marikana Smelter & Refinery (North West): Originally built by Lonmin, this is an integrated PGM processing complex. It receives concentrate from Sibanye and third-party producers, smelts it in an electric furnace to produce PGM matte, and then refines the matte through a series of hydrometallurgical and pyrometallurgical processes to produce >99.95% pure platinum, palladium, and rhodium. The refinery also produces nickel sulphate and copper cathode as by-products. Capacity is ~1 million tonnes of concentrate per year.
  • Stillwater Refinery (US): The US operations (Stillwater and East Boulder mines) produce PGM concentrate that is shipped to a dedicated refinery in Columbus, Montana, for processing into high-purity palladium and platinum.
  • Gold Refinery: Sibanye's gold is refined at the Rand Refinery (Johannesburg) — the largest gold refinery in Africa, in which Sibanye has a significant interest. Rand Refinery produces refined gold bars to London Good Delivery standard.
Why Sibanye-Stillwater Matters
  • Global PGM market share: Sibanye-Stillwater is the world's largest primary producer of palladium and a top-3 producer of platinum. Its decisions on production, capex, and closures directly impact global PGM supply and pricing.
  • Integrated value chain: Unlike pure mining companies, Sibanye owns the full value chain from mine to refinery, keeping higher-value processing in South Africa. The Marikana refinery is one of only a handful of PGM refineries in the world capable of producing all six PGMs at high purity.
  • Employment: ~90,000 employees make Sibanye one of the largest private employers in South Africa. The gold and PGM operations are concentrated in areas (Rustenburg, Carletonville, Welkom) with few alternative sources of employment.
  • Palladium market exposure: Palladium is primarily used in automotive catalytic converters for gasoline engines. The shift toward electric vehicles (which do not need catalytic converters) is a structural risk for palladium demand. Sibanye is actively diversifying into battery metals (lithium, nickel) to hedge against this transition.
  • Battery metals pivot: Sibanye has invested in nickel (the Nornickel asset disposal, a nickel refinery in Belgium) and lithium (Keliber project in Finland, and Rhyolite Ridge in the US), positioning itself as a future-facing metals company beyond PGMs.
