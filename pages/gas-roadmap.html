🗺️ Gas Roadmap for South Africa 2025–2042

IGUA-SA — a strategic pathway to secure, sustainable, and diversified gas supply. Updated April 2026.

The official Gas Master Plan sets policy intent; this roadmap is the “how-to-execute” document — demand stacks, sequenced LNG and West Coast Gas projects, a Presidency-mandated task team, and a fiscal wrap so contracts can actually close.

The gas cliff

Existing supply from Mozambique’s Pande–Temane fields ceases by mid-2028. Sasol methane-rich gas (MRG) can only bridge about two years after that (~23 PJ/year, 2028–2030). There is no other domestic source of scale in that window.

IGUA-SA’s short-term facts:

  • Imported LNG is the only bridging solution.
  • Mozambique is the only viable short-term LNG route — Matola or Inhassoro, connected through the existing ROMPCO pipeline.
  • Richards Bay is a KwaZulu-Natal regional solution, not a national fix for the cliff. It still has a long-term commercial case, sequenced after Mozambique.
  • Industrial demand alone cannot FID an LNG terminal. A gas-to-power anchor plus a fiscal wrap (guarantees, viability-gap funding, long-term offtake) is required.
  • GasHub is the industry-led, open-access, cost-pass-through demand aggregator — necessary, but not sufficient without government support.
First institutional step: a Presidency-mandated National Gas Task Team and Programme Management Office, bringing together DEE, DMRE, National Treasury, DTIC, NERSA, Eskom, Sasol, and IGUA-SA. Immediate priorities: Mozambique LNG and West Coast Gas. Richards Bay LNG and Luiperd–Brulpadda are diversification, not the first national fix.

Supply mix by 2042 (~1,076 PJ/year)

Seven sources, sequenced so the cliff is bridged before West Coast Gas and Luiperd scale.

Mozambique LNG ~272 PJ/yr

FID 2026, COD 2030. Target 140–270 PJ/year. The national short-term LNG route via ROMPCO.

Richards Bay LNG ~175 PJ/yr

FID 2028, COD 2030. Target 130–175 PJ/year. KZN regional terminal, not the cliff fix.

Coega LNG ~52 PJ/yr

Eastern Cape terminal, demand-dependent, later in the 2030s.

West Coast Gas ~469 PJ/yr

FID 2028, COD 2035. Target 285–470 PJ/year. Largest long-term domestic source; pre-feasibility to close by end-2026.

Luiperd–Brulpadda ~65 PJ/yr

FID 2030, COD 2035. Integrated into Mossel Bay / Block 11B/12B.

Distributed / small-scale LNG ~21 PJ/yr

Renergen and others. Gradual 1–20 PJ/year through 2042. Plus Sasol MRG as a two-year bridge.

Economy and policy

The gas economy already contributes about R700 billion (8% of GDP) and supports 75,000 direct jobs, with a 2.7× economic multiplier, a 6× employment multiplier, and 7–10 dependants per job. IGUA-SA argues a competitive supply stack could add up to 3 percentage points of annual GDP growth by the late 2030s.

Policy tables 16.1–16.5 cover strategic foundations, demand/infrastructure alignment, fiscal and commercial frameworks, regional and planning integration, and legal/institutional certainty. Minister Ramokgopa’s line in the document: gas is not merely a bridge, but a pillar in a diversified energy mix.
