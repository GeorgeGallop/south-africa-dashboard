⚡ Electricity & Energy

The most visible and urgent reform stream — breaking Eskom's monopoly, enabling private generation, and building a competitive electricity market.

The goal

End load shedding, reduce the cost of electricity, enable private investment in generation, and restructure Eskom into separate generation, transmission and distribution entities so that a competitive wholesale electricity market can emerge.

Who leads it — the committees
Structure Role Status
Eskom Restructuring Task Team (ERTT) Drives the unbundling of Eskom into three entities: generation (Eskom Generation), transmission (NTCSA) and distribution — enabling private participation and a competitive market. Phase I completed (2026). The President endorsed establishing an independent state-owned transmission entity. Eskom Restructuring Framework approved for independent TSO (Transmission System Operator) transition (Aug 2026).
National Energy Crisis Committee (NECOM) Cabinet-level coordination body that oversees the Energy Action Plan and aligns state actions on generation, transmission and demand-side measures. Active; reports into Cabinet and the Operation Vulindlela Unit.
NERSA (regulator) Issues generation licences, approves tariffs, and administers the registration regime that was liberalised under OV. Administered the removal of the 100 MW licensing threshold.
South African Wholesale Electricity Market (SAWEM) The market framework being established to allow competitive trading between generators, traders and buyers. See the SAWEM section elsewhere in this dashboard for the market design. Regulatory steps underway to establish the framework.
What has happened
  • Licensing threshold removed: The 1 MW threshold was raised to 100 MW in 2021 and then removed entirely, so private generators no longer need a licence.
  • Investment surge: The confirmed renewable energy investment pipeline grew from ~4,000 MW (March 2022) to more than 36,000 MW — over R220 billion in private investment.
  • Competitive procurement restarted: Renewables (REIPPPP), gas and storage procurement programmes reopened and expanded.
  • Transmission expansion: The independent transmission company (NTCSA) was established and the framework for an independent state-owned transmission entity was approved, with transmission corridors prioritised to connect new renewables.
  • Wholesale market design: Work advanced on the market code and institutional framework for the South African Wholesale Electricity Market.
Where it stands now (2026)

60% of electricity reforms are on track, 20% delayed, and the remainder face material obstacles. The hardest part is distribution: restructuring 167 licensed distributors into financially and operationally sustainable regional electricity distributors (REDs).

Load shedding has eased dramatically from 2023 levels as private generation comes online, but the system remains tight as transmission constraints and municipal distribution failures persist.

What is projected
  • The 36,000 MW+ private pipeline, once operational, is expected to structurally end load shedding, lower energy costs and unlock industrial expansion.
  • A competitive wholesale electricity market with multiple generators trading through the system operator.
  • Transmission expansion to connect new wind and solar in the Northern Cape and Eastern Cape.
  • Distribution reform (REDs) to make municipal electricity viable and improve service delivery.
