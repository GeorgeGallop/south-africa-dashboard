⚖️ Constitutional Court

The highest court in all constitutional matters

📑 Overview

The Constitutional Court is the highest court in South Africa for constitutional matters. Established by the Constitution of 1996 (Chapter 8, Section 167), it has the final say on all constitutional matters, including the interpretation of the Bill of Rights, the validity of laws and executive conduct, and disputes between organs of state.

📄 Functions
  • Deciding constitutional matters (the highest authority on constitutional interpretation)
  • Certifying constitutional amendments and bills of rights compliance
  • Resolving disputes between national and provincial governments
  • Confirming declarations of invalidity made by lower courts
  • Determining whether Parliament has fulfilled its constitutional obligations
  • Protecting fundamental rights enshrined in the Bill of Rights
📚 Composition

The Court is composed of 11 justices:

  • Chief Justice — Presides over the Court and the judiciary
  • Deputy Chief Justice — Assists the Chief Justice
  • 9 Constitutional Court Justices — Appointed by the President on recommendation of the Judicial Service Commission (JSC). Justices serve a 12-year non-renewable term
