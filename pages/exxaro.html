Exxaro Resources Ltd

South Africa's Largest Black-Owned Coal Producer — Thermal Coal, Renewable Energy & the Energy Transition

Exxaro Resources is a South African mining and energy company and one of the largest producers of thermal coal in the country. It sits at the centre of South Africa's electricity value chain — its Grootegeluk mine in Limpopo feeds the giant Matimba and Medupi power stations, supplying a meaningful share of the fuel behind Eskom's grid. Founded from the restructuring of the old Kumba Resources, Exxaro is a flagship of black economic empowerment in the mining industry and is deliberately repositioning itself from a pure coal miner into a diversified energy company — building wind and solar capacity through its subsidiary Cennergi while preparing for a future beyond coal.

History & Origins
  • 2006: Exxaro was created from the restructuring of Kumba Resources. As part of a landmark empowerment transaction involving Anglo American's South African coal and mineral sands assets, Kumba's core assets were split — with Kumba Iron Ore listing separately and Exxaro inheriting the coal, mineral sands (Tronox), and base metals businesses. The name "Exxaro" was chosen to reflect the company's transformation into a broad-based black-empowered mining group.
  • 2007: Exxaro completed the acquisition of Eyesizwe Coal — a black-empowered coal company — creating the largest black-owned coal producer on the JSE. This deal added key Mpumalanga operations and cemented Exxaro's empowerment credentials.
  • 2008–2015: The company grew its export coal position, developed the large Mafube joint venture with Anglo American, and expanded at Grootegeluk, which was already one of the largest open-cast coal mines in the world. In 2015, Exxaro acquired Total Coal South Africa, adding further Mpumalanga production and reserves.
  • 2016–2017: Exxaro established Cennergi, its renewable energy subsidiary, initially as a 50/50 joint venture with Tata Power, and brought the Amakhala Emoyeni (Eastern Cape, 134 MW) and Tsitsikamma Community (95 MW) wind farms online — two of South Africa's first large-scale private wind projects under the REIPPPP.
  • 2018–2020: Exxaro executed a major strategic pivot. It unbundled its majority stake in Tronox (the world's largest titanium dioxide producer) to shareholders in 2020, and sold its interest in the Sishen Iron Ore Company (SIOC) back to ArcelorMittal South Africa — completing a full exit from iron ore and mineral sands to focus on coal and energy.
  • 2020–2025: Exxaro consolidated its position as Eskom's largest coal supplier (via Grootegeluk) while building an energy-transition strategy. It took full ownership of Cennergi, announced new solar development plans at its operations, committed to a coal-mines-to-power transition in its Mpumalanga footprint, and began exploring green hydrogen and battery-related minerals as future growth pillars.
South African Operations
Grootegeluk (Limpopo)

Scale: One of the largest open-cast coal mines in the world, with capacity to produce ~40+ million tonnes per year — the single biggest coal mine in South Africa.

Role: Dedicated supply to Eskom's Matimba and Medupi power stations via overland conveyor, plus metallurgical/PCI coal for the export and domestic markets.

Significance: The mine's life-of-mine extends well into the 2040s, making it a strategic anchor for both Exxaro and the security of Eskom's baseload coal fleet in the Waterberg.

Mpumalanga Coal (Leeuwpan, Belfast, Mafube)

Leeuwpan: Open-cast mine producing a mix of Eskom-grade thermal coal and export quality product, with its own rapid-load rail siding.

Belfast: (Acquired via Total Coal SA in 2015) produces high-quality export thermal coal via the Richards Bay Coal Terminal rail network.

Mafube JV: 50/50 joint venture with Anglo American producing export thermal coal in Mpumalanga — one of the key remaining JVs of its kind.

Cennergi — Renewable Energy

Amakhala Emoyeni (Eastern Cape): 134 MW wind farm, one of the largest operational wind assets in South Africa, delivering power under a 20-year PPA to Eskom.

Tsitsikamma Community (Eastern Cape): 95 MW wind farm with meaningful community shareholding.

Direction: Exxaro is expanding Cennergi into new wind and solar projects and is evaluating supplying renewable power directly to industrial customers and its own mines, positioning the group as a diversified energy producer rather than a pure coal miner.

Emerging Growth Areas

Solar at Grootegeluk: Exxaro has announced plans to develop utility-scale solar PV adjacent to Grootegeluk to displace Eskom power use and support the mine's decarbonisation.

Green hydrogen: Exxaro is a participant in South Africa's emerging green hydrogen discussions, particularly around the Waterberg, which has excellent solar, wind and water-access characteristics.

Battery minerals & carbon capture: The company is evaluating diversification into future-facing minerals and carbon capture/utilisation opportunities aligned with the Just Transition.

Why Exxaro Matters for South Africa
  • Eskom's coal backbone: Exxaro is the largest private supplier of coal to Eskom. Grootegeluk's long-life reserves are a core reason the Waterberg has been identified as South Africa's future coal heartland — any change to Exxaro's supply directly affects the reliability of the national grid.
  • Flagship of economic empowerment: Exxaro is South Africa's premier broad-based black-empowered mining company, with Eyesizwe and the Industrial Development Corporation (IDC) as major shareholders. It is regularly cited as a model for BEE ownership in the extractive sector.
  • Employment & regional development: ~13,000 employees and contractors, concentrated in Limpopo (Waterberg) and Mpumalanga — regions with limited alternative employment. Grootegeluk effectively anchors the town of Lephalale.
  • Export earnings: Exxaro's Mpumalanga operations and the Mafube JV feed Richards Bay Coal Terminal, contributing billions of rand in export revenue and helping balance South Africa's current account.
  • Renewable energy pioneer: Through Cennergi, Exxaro was one of the first mining companies in South Africa to own and operate large-scale wind farms — proof that the energy transition can be led by South African capital rather than left entirely to foreign IPPs.
  • Just Transition credibility: Because Exxaro has a credible plan to phase into renewables and low-carbon energy, it is a key voice in the Just Transition — the political and economic challenge of moving Mpumalanga and Waterberg away from coal without destroying jobs.
The Road Ahead — Exxaro's Forecast for Investment in SA

Exxaro's public strategy — set out in its Future-Fit / energy-transition roadmap — is to evolve from a "coal mining company" into a "diversified energy and minerals company" by roughly 2030. The company's own forecasts guide its investment decisions as follows:

Medium Term (2025–2030): Coal still funds the transition
  • Coal investment continues — extending Grootegeluk and sustaining Mpumalanga operations to meet Eskom demand and export contracts, while funding the pivot.
  • Renewable build-out — further wind and solar capacity via Cennergi, including solar for self-supply at Grootegeluk and potential merchant/industrial PPAs.
  • Waterberg development — Exxaro sees the Waterberg as a strategic area for coal, renewables and green-hydrogen industrialisation over the next decade.
Long Term (2030+): Low-carbon energy & minerals
  • Declining coal share — as Eskom retires coal plants, Exxaro anticipates coal declining as a share of revenue, with a managed, value-preserving wind-down in Mpumalanga.
  • Green hydrogen — leveraging Waterberg's solar and transmission access for green-hydrogen industrial projects.
  • Diversified minerals — exploration and investment in battery/energy-transition minerals to give the group a future-facing resource base.
  • Just Transition projects — repurposing mine land and infrastructure (e.g. for solar farms) to sustain jobs as coal production falls.

Investment signal: Exxaro is signalling multi-billion-rand capital programmes in South Africa over the next decade — anchored by the ~R100+ billion it has historically invested in the Waterberg complex — with an increasing share directed to renewables and low-carbon infrastructure. The company's forecast is effectively that South Africa's energy future is still built in the Waterberg and Mpumalanga — but with solar, wind and hydrogen alongside coal rather than instead of it overnight.
