📘 Parliament of South Africa

The legislative authority of the Republic

📑 Overview

Parliament is the legislative branch of the South African government, consisting of two houses: the National Assembly (lower house) and the National Council of Provinces (upper house). It is responsible for making and passing laws, scrutinising the executive, and representing the people of South Africa.

📄 Functions
  • Passing legislation (ordinary bills, constitutional amendments, money bills)
  • Scrutinising and overseeing executive action (portfolio committees, questions to ministers)
  • Approving the national budget and appropriations
  • Representing provinces through the NCOP (each province has 6 delegates regardless of size)
  • Holding the President and cabinet accountable
  • Participating in constitutional amendments (requires super-majority)
📚 Structure
🎨 National Assembly (NA)

400 members elected by proportional representation. The majority party forms government. The NA elects the President, passes laws, and holds the executive accountable through portfolio committees.

🇺🇦 National Council of Provinces (NCOP)

90 delegates (6 per province + 6 from SALGA). Represents provincial interests in the national legislature. Must approve legislation affecting provinces (Section 76 bills).
