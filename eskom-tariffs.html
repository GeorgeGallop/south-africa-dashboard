Eskom Tariffs (2025/26)

Comprehensive overview of Eskom's electricity tariff structure — with detailed Megaflex costs, a USD/ZAR cost estimator, and editable data via CSV.

Overview

Electricity tariffs in South Africa are made up of multiple components: network (distribution/transmission) charges, energy charges (kWh), and regulatory/market-related line items. Tariff frameworks are set by the National Energy Regulator of South Africa (NERSA).

Eskom's main tariff categories are Megaflex (large urban >1 MVA), Miniflex (medium urban <1 MVA), HomeFlex (residential), and BusinessFlex (commercial). This page focuses on Megaflex — the most granular tariff with full Time-of-Use and seasonally-varying rates.

Tariff Categories — Which "Bucket" Applies

Megaflex — Large urban customers with Notified Maximum Demand (NMD) > 1 MVA. Fully unbundled TOU with seasonal differentiation. This is the most detailed cost structure, shown in full below.

Miniflex — Medium urban customers (NMD < 1 MVA). Simplified TOU. Cost data can be structured similarly to Megaflex when available.

HomeFlex — Residential customers (Homelight 10A, 20A, 60A, Homepower). Typically inclining-block or TOU options.

BusinessFlex — Commercial customers. Includes TOU and demand charges. Data pending.

What Drives Tariff Increases?
  • Recovering system reliability: maintenance, spares, and fleet recovery costs.
  • Capital investment: new generation, grid reinforcement, and network resilience.
  • Fuel and operational costs: volatility in key cost inputs and efficiency performance.
  • Exchange rates and inflation: costs linked to imported equipment/services.
  • Debt and financing constraints: pressure on cost recovery and affordability.
  • Policy and social objectives: balancing cost-reflective tariffs with affordability.
