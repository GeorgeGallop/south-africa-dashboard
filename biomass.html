🌿 Biomass

Biomass power generation in South Africa — organic waste-to-energy, wood pellet co-firing, sugar bagasse, and landfill gas projects.

Overview

Biomass is a growing component of South Africa's renewable energy mix. The country's three main biomass sources are:

  • Sugar bagasse — the fibrous residue left after crushing sugarcane, burned in cogeneration plants in KwaZulu-Natal and Mpumalanga to produce both process heat and electricity.
  • Woody biomass — invasive alien plant clearing (Working for Water programme) and sawmill residue used for wood pellet production and direct combustion.
  • Landfill gas — methane captured from decomposing organic waste in municipal landfills, used for electricity generation (small scale).

The REIPPP Programme (Renewable Energy Independent Power Producer Procurement) has awarded contracts to several biomass and landfill gas projects. Total installed capacity from biomass remains small (~100-200 MW) compared to solar and wind, but it provides baseload and dispatchable renewable power — a key advantage over intermittent sources.

Key Data
  • Bagasse cogeneration: ~80-100 MW currently operating at sugar mills (e.g., Tongaat Hulett, RCL Foods). Potential for 500+ MW with more efficient plant.
  • Wood pellet exports: South Africa exports ~200,000 tonnes/year of wood pellets to Europe for co-firing in coal power stations.
  • Landfill gas to energy: ~10-20 MW from projects at Durban (Bisasar Road, Mariannhill), Johannesburg (Robinson Deep), and Cape Town.
  • Employment: Biomass projects are labour-intensive in feedstock collection, creating thousands of jobs in rural and township areas.
