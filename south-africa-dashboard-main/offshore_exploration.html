🌊 Offshore Gas Exploration

A dedicated look at South Africa’s offshore gas prospects, drilling activity, and the gap between exploration potential and near-term baseload gas plans.

Why offshore exploration matters

Offshore gas is a strategic priority because it can add a domestic supply dimension to South Africa’s gas ambitions. However, the current exploration picture is one of limited volumes, expensive deep-water activity, and a long timeline before any material commercial production can support power plants.

That means offshore exploration is important, but it is not the same as having a ready-made baseload gas system today.

Current exploration picture

South Africa is exploring offshore gas in the Orange Basin and Outeniqua Basin, but domestic supply remains very constrained. A small number of exploratory wells have found gas-bearing structures, yet they have not delivered the large, rapid volumes needed for a future baseload network.

  • Early discoveries confirm there is some offshore resource potential.
  • Deep-water blocks are expensive to develop and deliver only slowly.
  • Known offshore reserves are currently insufficient to support large-scale gas-fired power on their own.
Baseload gas and the supply gap

South Africa wants gas-fired plants to provide firming capacity, but the domestic resource base cannot yet guarantee that supply. Offshore exploration is therefore positioned as a medium-term source rather than the immediate foundation for baseload gas.

This creates a supply gap that is being filled by imported LNG through regasification terminals while exploration continues.

Regasification as the bridge

The country is building LNG import and regasification capacity precisely because offshore and onshore domestic gas cannot meet near-term demand. These terminals are the practical response to the mismatch between ambitious gas-fired power plans and low domestic supply.

What this means for investors
  • Offshore exploration reduces long-term supply risk but does not eliminate short-term dependence on imports.
  • Gas-fired plant developers must assume regasification terminals and pipeline links will be part of the supply stack.
  • Commercial development will likely follow a hybrid model of domestic offshore gas plus imported LNG.
  • Policy should keep offshore exploration moving, but not delay the infrastructure needed today.
