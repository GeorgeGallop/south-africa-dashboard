🌾 Agriculture

South Africa's agricultural sector — a dual economy of commercial farming and smallholder agriculture, producing food for the nation and high-value exports for the world.

Overview

South Africa's agriculture is one of the most diversified on the African continent. It operates as a dual economy: a highly developed commercial farming sector alongside a large smallholder and subsistence farming base. The sector contributes roughly 2-3% of GDP directly (with agro-processing bringing the broader agri-complex to ~12% of GDP), employs approximately 850,000 people directly, and supports millions more in the value chain.

The country is a net food exporter, shipping agricultural products to over 100 countries. Major export categories include citrus fruits, wine, table grapes, maize, apples & pears, and wool. The agricultural trade surplus has grown significantly, reaching over $7 billion annually in recent years.

South Africa's climate ranges from Mediterranean (Western Cape) to subtropical (KwaZulu-Natal and Limpopo) to semi-arid (Northern Cape and Free State), enabling a remarkable diversity of crops. The country is divided into nine agricultural production regions, each with distinct soil types, rainfall patterns, and farming systems.
