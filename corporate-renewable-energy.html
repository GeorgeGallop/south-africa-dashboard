🏢 Corporate Renewable Energy Programs

South African companies are investing billions in renewable energy to secure reliable, affordable electricity. With solar LCOE at R0.50–0.60/kWh and Eskom tariffs rising at ~12.5% annually, the economics are driving an irreversible shift. The 2021 licensing reform (100 MW threshold) unleashed a flood of private generation. This page tracks the major JSE-listed companies that have announced renewable energy plans in 2026.

🎯 What's Built (Operational)

Of the ~9,200 MW registered by NERSA, an estimated ~5,500–6,000 MW is operational (generating power). The remainder is under construction or in commissioning. The RE Monitoring Report tracks operational performance of registered plants.

🔧 Under Construction

An estimated ~2,500–3,000 MW is under construction. NERSA's quarterly registrations include facilities at various stages — pre-licensing, financial close, and construction. The investment value of Q1 2025/26 alone was R51.9bn.

📈 Behind-the-Meter (Off-Grid)

The NTCSA estimates ~8,400 MW of behind-the-meter solar. This is separate from NERSA's registered capacity and may partially overlap. These are rooftop and ground-mounted systems that do not feed into the grid — invisible to Eskom's demand data.

Note: The 19,677 MW headline figure (often cited by SAPVIA) includes all projects ever registered with NERSA across all years, not just those currently active. The 7,459 MW figure (Dec 2025) represents registrations since the 2021 reform. About 1,800 MW was added in Q4 2025/26 alone. NERSA's quarterly statements are the best source for tracking the rate of new registrations. The difference between the two figures represents pre-2021 registrations (mostly REIPPPP utility-scale projects) plus double-counting across data sources.

🔎 What's Driving the Corporate Energy Shift?

Four factors converged to make corporate renewable energy the dominant trend in South Africa's power sector:

📈
Eskom Tariffs

900% increase since 2008. Peak rate R6.85/kWh. Blended average R1.40–1.80/kWh. Tariffs rise at ~12.5% pa vs CPI ~5%.

🌎
Solar Costs Collapsed

Solar LCOE fell from R3.50/kWh (2011) to R0.50–0.60/kWh (2026). Solar + battery: R0.90–1.20/kWh. Payback: 3–5 years.

📄
2021 Licensing Reform

Schedule II amendment raised the generation threshold to 100 MW without a licence. Unleashed the embedded generation boom.

⚠️
Load Shedding Risk

329 days of load shedding in 2023. GDP loss estimated at R500bn+ without diesel OCGTs. Companies cannot rely on the grid.

🔗 How Corporate RE Programs Interact with SAWEM

Corporate renewable programs take three main forms, each with different implications for the wholesale market:

1. Behind-the-Meter

Solar PV + battery on-site. Reduces Eskom purchases directly. No grid interaction. Most common for mines and factories. Impact: Reduces Eskom's revenue but also reduces demand — lowers the SMP in the short term but increases the legacy charge as fixed costs spread over fewer MWh.

2. Wheeled PPAs

Corporate signs PPA with off-site IPP. Energy is fed into the grid at the IPP's location and withdrawn at the corporate's site via the NTCSA. Examples: Impala/Discovery Green, Sasol's 600 MW. Impact: Requires the NTCSA wheeling framework and Market Code. Reduces Eskom Generation's market share directly. The CPA's vesting contracts compete with wheeled power.

3. Direct SAWEM Participation

Corporate becomes a Balance Responsible Party (BRP) or trader in SAWEM. Can trade in the Day-Ahead Market, Intraday, and Balancing Mechanism. Can sign financial CfDs with generators. Impact: Fully integrated into the market — the corporate's demand and the IPP's generation are separate market positions that net out financially. This is the model SAWEM is designed for.
