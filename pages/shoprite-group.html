Shoprite Holdings Ltd

Africa's Largest Supermarket Retailer — The Engine of South African Everyday Retail

Shoprite is Africa's biggest supermarket group and South Africa's largest private-sector employer. From an eight-store grocery chain bought in 1979, it has grown into a retail empire spanning supermarkets, pharmacies, furniture, financial services, ticketing and on-demand delivery — anchored by a store network that places it within five kilometres of roughly 85% of South African households.

History & Origins
  • 1979: The Shoprite Group was founded when the PEP Stores group (led by Christo Wiese) purchased a small Cape Town grocery company with eight stores from the Geller and Rogut families. This was the seed of what would become Africa's largest retailer.
  • 1980s: Under the leadership of Whitey Basson — who joined in 1979 and became one of South Africa's most celebrated retail executives — Shoprite grew rapidly through a focus on price leadership and serving lower- and middle-income consumers. The company listed on the JSE in 1986.
  • 1990–1991: International expansion began with the first Namibian store (1990). In 1991, Shoprite acquired the national Checkers supermarket chain, adding a mid-to-upper-income format to the group.
  • 1995–1997: First stores opened in Lusaka, Zambia (1995). That year Shoprite also acquired Sentra, the central buying house for 550 owner-managed supermarkets, launching its franchise division. In 1997 the group bought the struggling OK Bazaars from South African Breweries for a nominal R1 — adding 139 OK stores and 18 Hyperamas overnight.
  • 2000–2005: Expansion across the continent: Zimbabwe and Uganda (2000), Madagascar, Tanzania and Mauritius (2002), plus the first Nigerian store in Lagos (2005). The group also acquired Foodworld, Computicket (South Africa's largest ticket seller) and launched the first Shoprite LiquorShop.
  • 2008: Shoprite was added to the JSE Top-40 Index, cementing its status as a blue-chip anchor of South African capital markets.
  • 2011–2012: Acquired Metcash's franchise division (Friendly, Seven-Eleven formats). In 2012 Shoprite became the first South African retailer to enter the Democratic Republic of Congo.
  • 2017: Whitey Basson retired after 37 years. Pieter Engelbrecht, former COO with 20+ years in the business, became CEO — leading the group's digital transformation.
  • 2020–2022: A strategic refocus: the group exited Uganda, Madagascar and Kenya (2021) and the DRC (2022) after sustained losses, choosing to concentrate capital on its core South African market and strongest regional positions (Namibia, Botswana, Zambia, Eswatini, Lesotho, Malawi, Mozambique). It also divested Hungry Lion in 2020.
  • 2024–2026: The group opened 68 stores in Africa in a single year (Oct 2024, 53 of them in SA). By 2025 it had 3,417 stores in 10 countries; that figure reached 3,655 stores in 8 African countries by March 2026. The Checkers Sixty60 on-demand platform became a national phenomenon.
The Shoprite Business Model & Brand Family

Shoprite operates a multi-format model that spans the entire income spectrum:

  • Shoprite: The flagship brand — Africa's biggest food retailer, focused on price leadership and everyday essentials.
  • Usave: A stripped-down, low-cost format serving lower-income and rural communities with basics at the lowest prices.
  • Checkers & Checkers Hyper: Mid-to-upper-income supermarkets (including hypermarkets), home of the premium Sixty60 on-demand service and the popular woo-woo rewards programme.
  • Sixty60: On-demand delivery of groceries and alcohol within ~an hour at in-store prices — powered by Pingo, the group's own last-mile delivery subsidiary. H1 FY2026 sales: R11.9 billion, up 34.6%, making it the most significant digital retail platform in South Africa.
  • OK Franchise / OK Furniture: Franchised convenience, grocery and furniture formats across the region.
  • House & Home: Homeware, furniture and appliances.
  • Medirite & Transpharm: Retail pharmacies and pharmaceutical wholesale — healthcare access across income levels.
  • Uniq, Petshop Science, Little Me, Checkers Outdoor: Standalone specialty formats launched under Engelbrecht — apparel, pet, baby and outdoor retail at supermarket prices.
  • Computicket & Computicket Travel: South Africa's largest ticketing platform.
  • Financial services: Money Market (money transfers, bill payments), CredX (personal loans), GetPaid, Shoprite Send, and the Xtra Savings rewards programme with 35.1 million members — one of the largest loyalty databases in Africa.
  • Freshmark & Red Star: In-house fresh produce procurement/supply and catering & hospitality wholesale.
  • Rainmaker Media: A retail media agency using the group's shopper data.
  • K'nect: Cellular products and the group's own MVNO, K'nect mobile.
Significance to South Africa
  • Largest private-sector employer: ~170,000 employees — more than most public sector departments, and a critical jobs engine in a high-unemployment economy.
  • Food security & affordability: The group returns billions to customers at the point of sale (R16.5 billion in FY2025) and held selling-price inflation to just 2.3% in its core Supermarkets RSA segment — a major force containing the cost of food for low-income households.
  • Market leadership: Africa's largest supermarket group and among the top ~90 retailers globally by revenue. It dominates South African grocery retail alongside Pick n Pay, Woolworths, Spar and Massmart.
  • Digital & financial inclusion: Sixty60, Xtra Savings and its money services bring e-commerce, loyalty and basic financial products to the mass market — including customers who banks traditionally ignore.
  • Supply chain & agriculture: Freshmark's procurement and the group's scale support thousands of local suppliers and farmers across the region.
  • Blue-chip JSE anchor: As a Top-40 constituent, Shoprite is a bellwether for the South African consumer economy — its sales are treated as a proxy for household spending and economic confidence.

What Shoprite Says About Its Future in SA

Under CEO Pieter Engelbrecht, the group's stated strategy ("the road ahead is clear") is built on four pillars, all centred on South Africa:

  • Omnichannel retail at scale: "We are clear in terms of our pursuit of building out our omnichannel retail platform in South Africa as a basis for our future growth, leveraging the Group's core South African corporate-owned store base which places us within five kilometres of 85% of South African households." The physical network is the moat; digital (Sixty60, online, data) is the growth engine layered on top.
  • Reallocating capital to core SA supermarkets: The group says it will "reallocate capital to core South African supermarket operations to build a smarter, customer-focused, omnichannel retailer that uses data and technology to optimise retail operations, to grow our share-of-wallet and strengthen our long-term market leadership." This explicitly follows the painful learnings of its 2020–2022 African exits.
  • Disciplined CAPEX, data-led pricing and digital acceleration: Priorities are disciplined capital spending, data-driven pricing, continued digital acceleration, and "expansion into adjacencies" (specialty formats like Uniq, Petshop Science, Little Me; financial services; retail media) to grow the share of shoppers' wallets.
  • "Invests early and executes consistently": The group's self-described advantage — a consistent track record of building infrastructure (distribution centres, its own last-mile fleet, digital platforms) before competitors, then executing relentlessly on price and availability.
Bottom line: Shoprite's future in South Africa is one of deepening — more omnichannel reach, more formats, more share of wallet — rather than the sprawling continental expansion of the early 2000s. It is betting that the domestic market, served through an unrivalled store network and a best-in-class digital platform, is the surer path to long-term value; and that keeping families' food costs low through price leadership will keep it at the centre of South African life.
