🏴 Economic Inclusion for All Bill

The Democratic Alliance's proposed legislation to replace race-based empowerment with poverty-based economic inclusion criteria.

📄 Overview

The Economic Inclusion for All Bill is the Democratic Alliance's flagship legislative proposal aimed at fundamentally restructuring South Africa's empowerment framework. Rather than using racial classification as the primary criterion for economic transformation, the bill proposes that eligibility for empowerment programmes be determined by household income, education level, and geographic deprivation — regardless of race.

🎯 Key Objectives
  • Replace B-BBEE: The bill would repeal the Broad-Based Black Economic Empowerment Act and replace it with a poverty-based empowerment system.
  • Reach the truly disadvantaged: Argues that current B-BBEE benefits a small, already-connected black elite while missing the poorest South Africans of all races.
  • Simplify compliance: Replace the complex B-BBEE scorecard system (ownership, management, skills development, enterprise development, socio-economic development) with a transparent, income-based qualification.
  • Non-racialism: Ground empowerment in the Constitution's non-racial principles — treating South Africans as individuals, not racial categories.
📚 The DA's Argument
John Steenhuisen, 2023: "The Economic Inclusion for All Bill says: if you are poor, you qualify. It does not matter what race you are. The current B-BBEE system has created a small, politically connected elite that has grown rich while the majority of South Africans — black, white, coloured, and Indian — remain trapped in poverty. Our bill targets poverty, not skin colour."

📈 Criticism & Opposition
  • ANC opposition: The ANC argues the bill would reverse the gains of B-BBEE and undermine the historical imperative to redress apartheid's racial economic legacy.
  • EFF rejection: The EFF considers the bill a neoliberal attempt to avoid meaningful economic transformation and maintain white economic dominance.
  • BEE elite resistance: Established black business interests who have benefited from B-BBEE have lobbied against any reform that would reduce their preferential access.
  • Implementation concerns: Critics argue that a means-tested system would be more administratively complex than the current race-based system.
