🧭 National Prosecuting Authority (NPA)

Public prosecutions

📑 Overview

The National Prosecuting Authority (NPA) is South Africa's prosecuting body, established under the National Prosecuting Authority Act of 1998. It is responsible for instituting and conducting criminal proceedings on behalf of the state, and plays a central role in the fight against corruption, organised crime and gender-based violence.

📄 Functions
  • Instituting and conducting criminal prosecutions
  • Decision-making on whether to prosecute in line with the Prosecution Policy
  • Investigating corruption through the Investigating Directorate (ID)
  • Managing the Asset Forfeiture Unit (AFU) to seize proceeds of crime
  • Overseeing the Witness Protection Programme
  • Coordinating specialised units such as the Sexual Offences and Community Affairs (SOCA) unit
