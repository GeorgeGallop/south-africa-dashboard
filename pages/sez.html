🏗️ Special Economic Zones (SEZs)

Geographically designated areas set aside for targeted economic activities, offering investors sector-specific infrastructure, preferential tax rates, and a coordinated government facilitation environment. Named tenant investments are logged below and on the SAWEM map / tables.

Zones in South Africa

South African Special Economic and Industrial Development Zones. Source: the dtic, "South Africa's Special Economic Zones" brochure (2021).

Overview

The South African Government seeks to transform the economy into a globally competitive industrial economy. One of the critical tools for accelerating the country's industrial development agenda is the Special Economic Zone (SEZ) Programme, mandated by the SEZ Act (Act 16 of 2014), proclaimed on 9 February 2016.

SEZs are a tool to help:

  • Promote industrial agglomeration,
  • Build the required industrial infrastructure,
  • Promote coordinated planning among key government agencies and the private sector, and
  • Guide the deployment of other necessary development tools.

There are currently 13 designated SEZs under the SEZ Act 16 of 2014. Namakwa (Aggeneys) was designated on 21 May 2024; Fetakgomo-Tubatse (Steelpoort) was designated on 22 May 2026 (GN 7506, GG 54718). Platinum Valley (Bojanala) remains an application / pending designation. Coega is the largest zone (9 003 ha, 14 campuses plus the NMBLP VW supplier park in Kariega) and published 59 named operational investors in 2026.

What receives preferential treatment?

Designation under the SEZ Act gives access to serviced industrial land, an operator, and (where proclaimed) a Customs Controlled Area. The 15% corporate rate is a separate Finance-Minister approval under s12R of the Income Tax Act — it is not automatic for every designated zone. Since GG 41758 of 6 July 2018 the six s12R zones are Coega, Dube TradePort, East London IDZ, Maluti-a-Phofung, Richards Bay IDZ and Saldanha Bay. Atlantis, OR Tambo, TASEZ, Nkomazi, Musina-Makhado, Namakwa and Fetakgomo-Tubatse sit at the standard 27% rate unless a new s12R gazette is issued.

Coega CDC's investor incentives page packages the national instruments as follows (coega.co.za/site/sez-investments/incentives):

Incentive What it means
15% preferential corporate tax (s12R) Qualifying companies in the six gazetted s12R zones pay 15% instead of 27%. Coega is one of those six. TASEZ, Atlantis, OR Tambo, Nkomazi, MMSEZ, Namakwa and FTSEZ do not currently carry the rate.
Building allowance (s12S) Accelerated allowance on new and unused buildings used for qualifying trade inside a designated SEZ (typically 10% a year), lowering the net cost of constructing or improving facilities.
Employment Tax Incentive A cost-sharing arrangement with government that reduces the cost of hiring young and first-time workers, available nationwide and used heavily inside SEZs.
Customs Controlled Area (CCA) Duty-free importation of production-related raw materials, machinery and assets, plus VAT relief on qualifying CCA supplies — the main working-capital incentive for export manufacturers. Coega's one-stop shop (OSISC) administers CCA with SARS.
SEZ Fund / infrastructure dtic SEZ Fund top-up for bulk infrastructure and, in some zones, top-structure support (e.g. Atlantis Quantum V3 sod-turning April 2025).

Each incentive has its own qualifying tests in the SEZ Act, the Income Tax Act (s12R / s12S), the Employment Tax Incentive Act, and the Customs Duty Act. CDC also runs an on-site investor one-stop shop.

Dube TradePort SEZ KwaZulu-Natal Adjacent to King Shaka International Airport — aerospace, electronics, medical devices and pharma, clothing and textiles, automotive components, logistics. The Dube AgriZone specialises in perishables, horticulture, aquaculture and floriculture.
Richards Bay IDZ (RBIDZ) KwaZulu-Natal Linked to the deep-water Port of Richards Bay — metals beneficiation (aluminium, iron ore, titanium), renewable energy, agro-processing, marine industry development, ICT. Aims to become SA's primary energy hub for LNG and green energy.
OR Tambo SEZ Gauteng Multi-precinct SEZ at OR Tambo International Airport — jewellery and diamond beneficiation, precious metals processing, pharmaceuticals, electronics, agro-processing. The Springs Precinct targets logistics and supply chain.
Tshwane Automotive SEZ (TASEZ) Gauteng Africa's first automotive city — OEM supply chains, automotive component manufacturing and assembly, and new energy vehicle (NEV) production.
Maluti-a-Phofung SEZ (MAPSEZ) Free State Multi-sector SEZ on the N3 corridor between Johannesburg and Durban — light and heavy manufacturing, logistics and warehousing, cross-docking, vehicle distribution, automotive supply, agro-processing, chemical blending.
Platinum Valley SEZ (Bojanala) North West Bojanala Platinum District, the western limb of SA's platinum belt (~40% of world platinum production) — PGM beneficiation, fuel cells and hydrogen technology, platinum recycling, autocatalyst manufacture, mining capital equipment, renewable energy, agro-processing. Pending designation — not yet gazetted.
Musina-Makhado SEZ (MMSEZ) Limpopo Two-site zone on the N1 corridor — North Site (near Musina): light manufacturing, agro-processing, cold storage, logistics leveraging the Beitbridge border. South Site (near Makhado): metallurgical and mineral beneficiation including ferrochrome, ferromanganese and titanium. Currently implementing a turnaround plan.
Nkomazi SEZ Mpumalanga Border town of Komatipoort on the Maputo Development Corridor — agro-processing of citrus, subtropical fruits and aromatic plants, nutraceuticals, logistics and freight, alternative energy including gas-to-power.
Namakwa SEZ Northern Cape Aggeneys, designated 21 May 2024 (1 270 ha). Northern Cape's first SEZ — zinc/lead smelting and mineral beneficiation anchored by Vedanta's Gamsberg mine and planned smelter, plus manufacturing, agro-processing and renewables.
Fetakgomo-Tubatse SEZ (FTSEZ) Limpopo Steelpoort, Sekhukhune District — PGM, chrome and vanadium beneficiation, mining inputs, green energy, agro, automotive supply. Designated 22 May 2026 (GN 7506, GG 54718), 1 010 ha between Samancor and Lion Ferrochrome. Pipeline 46 companies / ~R52.6 bn.
Strategic context

Across South Africa's SEZs, a consistent pattern emerges: purpose-built industrial platforms, deep integration with ports and airports, and preferential access to AfCFTA and SADC markets. These zones are not speculative projects but functioning ecosystems with established tenants, supply chains and logistics performance.

The zones are concentrated in strategic locations — deep-water ports (Coega, Saldanha Bay, Richards Bay), international airports (OR Tambo, King Shaka), and key freight corridors (N1, N2, N3, N4) — positioning them as the location of choice for investment in mineral beneficiation, agro-processing, automotive, green technology and petrochemical industries.
