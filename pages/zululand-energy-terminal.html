⚓ Zululand Energy Terminal & Eskom’s Richards Bay gas plant

Two linked projects at Richards Bay: South Africa’s first LNG import terminal at Berth 207, South Dunes, developed by Zululand Energy Terminal (ZET), and Eskom’s planned 3 000 MW mid-merit combined-cycle plant in the Richards Bay Industrial Development Zone. On 5 June 2026 they signed a Heads of Agreement making Eskom the terminal’s foundation customer. That is not yet a terminal-use agreement, a gas supply contract, or a construction go-ahead.

What the deal actually is

South Africa has talked about Richards Bay LNG-to-power for a decade. The June 2026 HOA is the first time the state utility that wants the megawatts and the consortium that won the port concession put a named commercial framework on paper.

It is two projects that only work together:

  1. The molecules and the jetty — Zululand Energy Terminal. LNG ships into Richards Bay, storage (first a floating storage unit, later an onshore tank), onshore regasification, then a short high-pressure pipe into the existing Lilly gas line near Empangeni. Open access: other industrial users can book capacity too. That is how ZET tries to bank a ~R15 billion terminal.
  2. The turbines — Eskom’s 3 000 MW Richards Bay plant. A combined-cycle station in the RBIDZ, mid-merit, 25-year life, PSP-financed, burning the regasified LNG. Eskom’s quote is that foundation-customer status is the “critical enabler” for that programme, with long-term contracting meant to cut LNG price volatility.
Chicken-and-egg, named. ZET cannot take FID without a creditworthy offtaker. Eskom cannot run 3 GW of CCGT without a terminal. The HOA is the offtake signal. It is not yet the offtake contract. ZET’s own language after the signing is that the next documents are a Terminal Use Agreement and financial close.

Dan Marokane (Eskom GCE): gas is the bridge fuel that gives dispatchable backup as coal declines and wind/solar grow; foundation-customer status “minimise[s] volatility and support[s] system reliability while aligning with the IRP 2025 objectives.” Oliver Naidu (ZET director / Vopak South Africa president): Eskom as an anchor customer “strengthens the commercial foundation of the terminal.”

The terminal (ZET) — what is being built

Site: South Dunes precinct, Port of Richards Bay, City of uMhlathuze, Berth 207. Open-access import, storage and regasification. Services listed by ZET / Creamer: receiving, storage, regasification, marine bunkering, vessel reloading, truck loading, and gas transmission — i.e. not only Eskom’s plant.

Phase 1 (current planning basis, commercial operation 2030):

  • Floating storage unit (FSU) of at least 170 000 m³ (Vopak’s February 2025 note also quoted a 135 000–174 000 m³ band).
  • Onshore regasification of about 3 million tonnes a year (~400 million standard cubic feet a day).
  • Marine works for the FSU, jetty-to-shore cryogenic lines, seawater abstraction/discharge, a gas distribution hub, optional truck loading, bulk services.

Phase 2: onshore LNG tank of about 220 000 m³ (may replace the FSU) plus more send-out, taking the terminal to about 4.5 mtpa (~600 mmscfd). Creamer’s June 2026 sheet says Phase 2 could also COD as early as Q4 2030 if customers and FID support it.

The pipe: a new 24-inch, 80 bar buried (1–1.5 m) transmission line, about 25 km from the RBIDZ gas hub to the existing Lilly pipeline near Empangeni. Isolation valves, pigging, overpressure protection. That is the inland industrial offtake path (Secunda / Gauteng users facing the Mozambique “gas cliff”), not a new Richards Bay–Johannesburg trunk on day one.

Customers, including Eskom, buy their own LNG cargoes and book capacity on ZET. The terminal is a tolling / open-access facility, not Eskom’s private jetty. That is why the ExxonMobil HOA sits beside the Eskom HOA: one is molecules, one is demand.

The power plant — Eskom’s 3 GW in the RBIDZ

This is the plant that has been in EIAs since 2019: a mid-merit combined-cycle station, gas primary, diesel as emergency backup in the original application, 3 000 MW installed, 25-year life, inside the Richards Bay IDZ so it sits next to the port, Hillside aluminium, Foskor, and the coal terminal rather than in a greenfield town.

IRP 2025, as quoted in the 5 June joint statement, wants 6 000 MW of gas by 2030, split:

  • 3 000 MW under the Gas IPP programme — see GIPPPP.
  • 3 000 MW delivered by Eskom — this project.

Eskom’s three stated operational reasons in the joint statement:

  1. Energy security and renewable support — dispatchable capacity while coal falls and wind/solar rise.
  2. Grid stabilisation and less diesel — sit alongside batteries and pumped hydro; cut OCGT diesel at Ankerlig / Gourikwa for mid-merit hours.
  3. The gas cliff — Pande–Temane / ROMPCO supply to industry is expected to tighten toward 2030. The plant is power-first, but the same terminal is meant to keep industrial gas in the country.

Delivery model is PSP, not a classic Eskom-on-balance-sheet Medupi. International capital, a long-term offtake, and (still to be named) private partners. That is also why the SCA set-aside of the old EA matters: lenders will want a clean authorisation, not a 2019 permit the appeal court called a nullity.

The court problem the HOA does not solve

On 17 September 2025 the Supreme Court of Appeal upheld the appeal by the South Durban Community Environmental Alliance (SDCEA) and groundWork against the Minister of Forestry, Fisheries and the Environment (479/2023 [2025] ZASCA 134, Dambuza JA). The 23 December 2019 environmental authorisation for Eskom’s Richards Bay CCGT is set aside. Eskom must start a fresh application once the public-participation defects are cured.

What the court actually found, in short:

  • Public participation on the environmental impact report was run in English in a municipality where most residents speak isiZulu — a large part of the affected public was shut out.
  • Climate-change impacts, need and desirability, alternatives (including renewables), and cumulative effects were not properly assessed under NEMA.
  • The SCA substituted the Minister’s decision rather than sending it back for another rubber stamp.

Eskom’s 18 September 2025 statement (Alfred Seema) accepted the judgment, restated the case for gas as flexible backup, and said it was considering next steps. Reuters, reporting the June 2026 HOA, noted the plant had been stalled by that court order.

Two environmental processes, not one. The SCA case is about Eskom’s power station. ZET’s terminal and the 25 km Lilly spur have their own applications (EA, atmospheric emissions licence, water-use licence, coastal-waters discharge, dumping-at-sea) with CES as EAP — still under way as of the 26 June 2026 project sheet. Winning an HOA does not revive the 2019 plant permit.

What is not signed yet

  • Terminal Use Agreement — the capacity booking that actually underwrites the jetty. Named by Naidu as the next step after the HOA.
  • LNG supply (SPA) — ExxonMobil is an HOA, not volumes, price, or FOB/DES terms. Eskom still has to buy cargoes (or have a PSP partner buy them).
  • FID / financial close on the terminal (now spoken of as 2028) or on the power station.
  • EPC contract — EOI only, closed July 2026.
  • A valid environmental authorisation for the 3 GW plant.
  • Named private partners for the PSP plant, or a published PPA/offtake structure into SAWEM / Eskom Gx.

How this sits next to GIPPPP and the old Richards Bay page

Do not collapse three things into one:

  • Richards Bay LNG-Power is the long-running concept — port + CCGT + inland gas. This page is the live 2026 commercial structure (ZET concession + Eskom foundation customer).
  • GIPPPP is the other 3 000 MW: competitive IPP gas, not Eskom Gx. IRP 2025 treats them as a pair.
  • Coega / Ngqura and any East London small-scale TNPA process are separate ports. ZET is Richards Bay only.
