🤝 Bilateral Trading

South Africa's trade relationships with the world — export partners, import sources, trade agreements, and how geopolitics shapes who we trade with and who gets left out.

Overview

South Africa is a medium-sized trading nation with total merchandise trade (exports + imports) of ~$200 billion annually. The country runs a modest trade surplus — exports consistently exceed imports, driven by commodity exports (gold, PGMs, coal, iron ore, manganese, vehicles) and agricultural products (citrus, wine, fruit).

South Africa's trade relationships have evolved significantly since 1994. Under apartheid, the country faced extensive trade sanctions (1960s-1990s) that limited partners primarily to sympathetic regimes. Post-1994, South Africa re-entered the global trading system, joining the World Trade Organization (WTO) in 1995 and signing a series of bilateral and regional trade agreements that reshaped its trade geography.

The most important shift: trade with China and Asia has grown from almost nothing in 1994 to making up ~40% of total trade in 2024. The EU remains South Africa's largest trading bloc, but its share has declined. Trade with the rest of Africa has grown significantly but remains below its potential. Trade with the United States has been shaped by preferential access under AGOA (African Growth and Opportunity Act), which is subject to periodic political renewals.
