Visa and Immigration

Attracting scarce skills, cutting visa bureaucracy, and growing tourism - through backlog clearance, the eVisa system, visa waivers and the Trusted Employer Scheme.

The problem

South Africa's visa system was a binding constraint on growth: a backlog of more than 300,000 applications, some taking years to process, and visa categories that made it hard for companies to hire foreign skills. The hospitality sector lost billions in potential tourism revenue because visitors from key markets could not get visas easily.

What has happened
  • Backlog cleared: More than 300,000 outstanding visa applications were processed and cleared.
  • eVisa expansion: The eVisa system was expanded from 14 to 34 countries, with additional adjudicators to speed processing.
  • Visa waivers: Visa-free access for visitors from 135 countries, with negotiations to add 12 more.
  • Point-of-entry PPPs: Public-private partnerships for land ports of entry launched to modernise border infrastructure.
Where it stands now (2026)

Visa and immigration reforms sit between completed and on track. The backlog is cleared, TES Phase II is live, and the ETA expansion continues. Residual challenges include turnaround times for complex applications and digital system reliability.

What is projected
  • A fully digital ETA system covering the majority of visitor markets.
  • The Trusted Employer Scheme scaling to cover more employers and skills categories, easing the skills shortage.
  • Modernised border management through PPPs at land ports.
  • Tourism growth as friction at ports of entry falls - supporting the attract skills, grow tourism objective.
