PASA

Petroleum Agency South Africa — the upstream oil and gas promotion and licensing agency, owned via CEF

PASA is the designated agency for onshore and offshore petroleum. It promotes acreage, receives applications for reconnaissance, technical cooperation, exploration and production rights, holds the national data, and advises the Minister. It is a wholly owned CEF subsidiary — 100% state, no listed stock — but it is not being merged into SANPC. A national oil company and a licensing agency are supposed to stay on opposite sides of the table.

Owner: CEF SOC Ltd (100% Republic of South Africa)
Reports to: Minister of Mineral and Petroleum Resources, via CEF
Statute: Originally designated under the MPRDA; mandate strengthened by the Upstream Petroleum Resources Development Act (UPRDA), fully enacted late 2024
Functions: Promotion, licensing, monitoring, compliance, data management, technical advice to the Minister
SANPC merger: Outside it. PASA stays a separate CEF subsidiary.

PASA

Upstream licensing · Orange Basin · Karoo · data

What it is

When Soekor was split in the early 2000s, the commercial exploration company became PetroSA and the acreage-management / promotion function became Petroleum Agency SA. That split is the whole point: the state as player (PetroSA / now SANPC) should not also be the state as referee. PASA sits in the CEF group for historical and PFMA reasons, but its product is licences and geological data, not barrels.

The UPRDA, in force from late 2024, restates and hardens that role: licensing, monitoring, compliance, data and technical oversight, plus advice to the Minister on reconnaissance permits, technical cooperation permits (TCPs), exploration rights and production rights.

What it is used for

  • Orange Basin — the west-coast deepwater play where TotalEnergies, Shell, and others have been active; PASA is the agency that administers the blocks.
  • South Coast / Block 11B/12B — Brulpadda and Luiperd (TotalEnergies-operated discoveries). PASA holds the regulatory file; PetroSA/SANPC may sit as a partner. They are not the same entity.
  • Karoo shale gas — PASA's resource evaluation has put recoverable estimates around 209 Tcf (down from older 370 Tcf headlines). The 2025 lifting of the shale-gas exploration moratorium puts PASA back in the onshore licensing seat.
  • National petroleum data — seismic, wells, and resource assessments that IOCs buy access to when they enter a bid round.

Who deals with it

International oil companies (TotalEnergies, Shell, Impact, Eco Atlantic and others), smaller explorers, and the state's own SANPC/PetroSA. The "buyer" of PASA's service is the applicant who wants a right, and the Minister who needs a technically competent agency. There is no private owner of PASA.

Governance tension is built in: PASA and SANPC both report up through CEF to the same minister. A clean regulator would sit in the department, not in the holding company of the national oil company. That is an unresolved design issue, not a reason to pretend PASA is a commercial subsidiary like SFF.

The future

PASA's relevance tracks whether South Africa actually drills. Orange Basin wells, a decision on 11B/12B development after TotalEnergies' pauses, and any real Karoo exploration programme will all run through this agency. If the UPRDA licensing system is slow or litigated, PASA becomes the bottleneck. If SANPC is allowed to lean on its sister company for acreage, the referee/player line blurs. Either way it remains a CEF subsidiary the SANPC merger was deliberately designed to leave alone.
