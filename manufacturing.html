South Africa's Manufacturing

A national overview of the industries that transform raw materials into products, create jobs, and drive exports.

What this page covers

South Africa’s manufacturing sector stretches far beyond a single factory or process. It combines the country’s mineral, chemical and agricultural strengths into differentiated industry clusters, from metals and fuel conversion to automotive, food systems and construction materials.

This page lays out the major manufacturing domains, the key value-chain subsections you should cover, and the full national context that makes them important.

South Africa’s manufacturing landscape
  • Minerals and metals processing: turning ore into metals, alloys and ferroalloys.
  • Steel and alloy production: producing steel, stainless steel, ferromanganese, ferrochrome and vanadium products.
  • Petrochemicals and synthetic fuels: converting coal and gas into liquid fuels, polymers and chemicals.
  • Automotive and transport equipment: assembling vehicles, engines and heavy-duty components.
  • Food and beverages: processing agricultural output into packaged goods, beverages and export products.
  • Building materials: producing cement, glass, plastics, bricks and other construction inputs.
  • Chemicals and industrial gases: making acids, fertilisers, gases, solvents and mining reagents.
  • Capital goods and machinery: manufacturing equipment for mining, power, agriculture and manufacturing itself.
The subsections to cover

Manufacturing in South Africa is best understood as a set of connected subsections. The first one is smelting, which is already covered on the dedicated Smelting page.

  • Smelting: heat-driven extraction of metal from concentrate. Open the Smelting page.
  • Refining and purification: producing commercially pure metals and chemical intermediates.
  • Alloying, steel and ferroalloy production: blending metals for steel, stainless steel and speciality alloys.
  • Petrochemical conversion: making fuels, polymers, solvents and synthetic chemicals.
  • Product manufacturing: turning intermediates into items such as cars, food products, chemicals and construction materials.
  • Logistics and export services: ports, transport, energy and supply chains that keep manufacturing viable.
How manufacturing fits the national economy

South Africa’s manufacturing sector is a bridge between raw resource wealth and market-ready products. It includes factories that add value to mining output, plants that convert coal into fuels and chemicals, and assembly lines for vehicles and consumer goods.

When you cover the full manufacturing story, the key questions are:

  • What raw materials are entering the factory gate?
  • What processes turn those inputs into intermediate or final products?
  • Which subsectors are core to exports and domestic demand?
  • How do energy, logistics and South Africa’s skills base support manufacturing?
