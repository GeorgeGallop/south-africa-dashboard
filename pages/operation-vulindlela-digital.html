Digital Communications

Ending more than a decade of spectrum delay, cutting mobile data costs, and accelerating the digital transformation of government.

The problem

South Africa was one of the last countries in the world with no high-demand spectrum assigned - the result of a decade of litigation and policy deadlock between ICASA, the Minister and mobile operators. This raised the cost of mobile data, slowed 4G/5G rollout, and made South Africa's digital economy uncompetitive.

What has happened
  • Spectrum auctioned: The high-demand spectrum (700 MHz, 800 MHz, 2.6 GHz, 3.5 GHz) was finally auctioned in March 2022, raising over R14 billion and ending the decade-plus delay.
  • Analogue switch-off: Migration to digital terrestrial television completed for frequencies above 694 MHz, freeing the digital dividend spectrum.
  • Mobile data costs declined: Data prices fell measurably after the spectrum auction, and a data-services market inquiry (CompCom) kept pressure on operators.
  • Digital government: MyMzansi and MzansiXchange are being deployed to put government services online.
Where it stands now (2026)

Digital infrastructure is 80% on track. The core spectrum reforms are largely complete; remaining work concentrates on digital public infrastructure (identity, payments, data sharing) and the digital transformation of government services.

What is projected
  • 5G rollout accelerated by the newly available spectrum.
  • Further declines in data prices as competition deepens.
  • A functioning digital identity and payments layer for government services (MyMzansi/MzansiXchange).
  • E-government services reducing red tape and transaction costs for citizens and businesses.
