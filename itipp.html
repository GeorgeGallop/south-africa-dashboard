⚡ ITIPP — Independent Transmission Infrastructure Procurement Programme

South Africa's flagship programme to bring private sector investment, expertise, and efficiency into the expansion of the national high-voltage transmission grid.

📄 What Is ITIPP?

The Independent Transmission Infrastructure Procurement Programme (ITIPP) is a government-led procurement programme administered by the Department of Electricity and Energy (DEE) in collaboration with the National Transmission Company South Africa (NTCSA) and with regulatory oversight from NERSA.

ITIPP was established in response to a fundamental constraint: the NTCSA cannot finance and build the ~14,000 km of new transmission lines needed over the next decade purely through its balance sheet and government funding. The private sector must be brought in to bridge the gap. ITIPP is the vehicle for doing so.

Programme website: www.itp-projects.co.za

The programme is mandated by the Transmission Determination issued by the Minister of Electricity and Energy, which gives effect to the Transmission Development Plan (TDP) and authorises the procurement of Independent Transmission Projects (ITPs).

🎯 Why ITIPP? — The Grid Challenge
📈 The Scale of the Problem
  • ~14,000 km of new high-voltage transmission lines required in the next 10 years
  • ~R210 billion total estimated investment needed for TDP 2024-2034
  • Current NTCSA capex capacity: ~R10-12 billion/year from tariff revenues
  • Funding gap: ~R8-10 billion/year that must come from alternative sources
💡 Why Private Sector?
  • Capital: Private developers can raise debt and equity faster than the fiscus
  • Efficiency: PPP-style procurement incentivises on-time, on-budget delivery
  • Expertise: Global transmission developers bring specialised engineering and project management capability
  • Risk transfer: Construction, cost overrun, and operational performance risks are transferred to the private partner
⚙️ How ITIPP Works — The Procurement Process

ITIPP follows a structured, phased procurement framework modelled on South Africa's successful Renewable Energy Independent Power Producer Procurement Programme (REIPPPP) and international transmission PPP best practices.

1. TDP Identification

The NTCSA identifies specific transmission projects in the annual Transmission Development Plan (TDP). Projects suitable for ITIPP are ring-fenced — typically large-scale "greenfield" corridors, new collector substations in Renewable Energy Development Zones (REDZs), or strategic reinforcements where private finance adds the most value.

2. Ministerial Determination

The Minister of Electricity and Energy issues a Transmission Determination under Section 34 of the Electricity Regulation Act. This determination specifies the amount of transmission capacity to be procured, the technology (overhead lines, substations, cable, HVDC), and the procurement timeline. It is published in the Government Gazette.

3. NERSA Concurrence

The Ministerial Determination requires concurrence from NERSA. NERSA reviews the determination to ensure it is consistent with the Integrated Resource Plan (IRP), the TDP, and the national interest. NERSA also considers the impact on transmission tariffs and end-user electricity prices.

4. Request for Proposals (RFP)

The Department of Electricity and Energy (DEE) issues a public Request for Proposals (RFP), inviting private developers (or consortia) to bid for the right to finance, build, and operate each Independent Transmission Project (ITP). The RFP sets out technical requirements, qualification criteria, bid submission deadlines, and the evaluation framework.

5. Evaluation & Preferred Bidders

Bids are evaluated on a combination of:

  • Tariff/Price: The proposed transmission availability payment (R/month per MW of capacity made available)
  • Technical capability: Proven experience in building and operating HV transmission lines
  • Black Economic Empowerment: B-BBEE credentials and local content commitments
  • Financial close capability: Proven ability to raise project finance

Preferred bidders are announced and enter into exclusive negotiation periods.

6. Implementation Agreement & Financial Close

The preferred bidder signs an Implementation Agreement (or Transmission Service Agreement) with the NTCSA and the DEE. This agreement sets out performance standards, availability targets, tariff escalation formulae, default remedies, and termination provisions. The developer then reaches financial close — securing debt and equity commitments from lenders and shareholders. Construction begins.

7. Construction & Commissioning

The developer constructs the transmission facility in accordance with the technical specifications and grid code requirements. The NTCSA provides technical oversight and connection approval. Once completed, the facility is tested and commissioned — connected to the national grid and available for dispatch.

8. Operations & Availability Payments

The developer operates and maintains the transmission facility for the duration of the concession (typically 20-30 years). The NTCSA pays a transmission availability payment — a fixed monthly or annual fee that depends on the facility being available to transmit power (not on how much power actually flows). This payment structure is standard for transmission PPPs worldwide: the developer bears availability risk, not volume risk.

Geographic Focus Areas

The majority of ITIPP projects are concentrated in three strategic areas:

  • Northern Cape & Western Cape corridor: The solar PV and wind-rich Northern Cape (including the Upington and Springbok REZs) requires massive transmission capacity to export power to the Cape Town and coastal load centres. This is the single largest driver of new transmission in the TDP.
  • Eastern Cape wind corridor: The Eastern Cape has South Africa's best onshore wind resources. New lines from the Eastern Cape to KwaZulu-Natal and the Western Cape are needed to unlock this potential.
  • Limpopo & Mpumalanga (coal-to-clean transition): As coal plants retire (Medupi, Kusile, and older stations), new transmission is needed to connect replacement generation — including gas, solar, wind, and battery storage — in the northern and eastern regions.

The TDP 2024 identifies 14,000 km of new transmission lines over 10 years, but ITIPP Stage 1 covers roughly 2,000-3,000 km of the highest-priority corridors. Subsequent stages will expand coverage as the programme matures.

Key Technical Specifications
  • Voltages: Predominantly 400 kV and 765 kV AC, with potential 533 kV HVDC for very long-distance bulk corridors
  • Substations: New GIS (gas-insulated switchgear) or AIS (air-insulated) substations with capacities ranging from 200 MVA to 2,000 MVA
  • Series compensation: Many long lines require series capacitor banks to increase power transfer capacity and stability limits
  • Reactive power support: STATCOM, SVC, or synchronous condenser installations at key nodes for voltage stability
🔗 Relationship with NTCSA & SAWEM

ITIPP does not replace the NTCSA. The NTCSA remains the owner and operator of the majority of the transmission network. ITIPP is a complementary procurement programme through which specific, ring-fenced transmission assets are developed by the private sector and either:

  • Owned and operated by the private developer under a long-term concession, with the NTCSA paying an availability payment (BOOT model — Build, Own, Operate, Transfer), or
  • Built by the private developer and transferred to NTCSA for operation (BOT model — Build, Operate, Transfer)

The exact ownership and operational model varies by project and is determined during the procurement process. In all cases, the NTCSA retains overall system planning and operational coordination — the privately-owned transmission lines and substations are integrated into the NTCSA's grid control and dispatch systems.

For SAWEM, the ITIPP is vital: without the transmission capacity that ITIPP will unlock, new generation capacity (especially renewables in remote areas) cannot reach load centres, and the competitive wholesale market will be constrained by grid bottlenecks.

🕑 Timeline & Current Status
  • 2024: TDP 2024 published; Ministerial Determination issued; NERSA concurrence obtained; ITIPP Stage 1 formally launched
  • 2025: RFI (Request for Information) issued to interested developers. Market engagement workshops held. Draft RFP developed and shared with stakeholders
  • 2026 (current): Final RFP expected to be issued. Bid submission, evaluation, and preferred bidder announcement anticipated within the year
  • 2027-2028: Financial close and commencement of construction for Stage 1 projects
  • 2029-2031: Stage 1 projects progressively commissioned and connected to the grid
  • Beyond 2030: ITIPP Stage 2 and further stages — covering additional corridors from the TDP
