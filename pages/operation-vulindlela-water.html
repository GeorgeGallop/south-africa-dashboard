Water

Fixing water-use licensing, water services delivery and bulk water infrastructure - the reform stream that is currently 100% on schedule under Operation Vulindlela.

The problem

South Africa is a water-scarce country. Businesses, farms and municipalities faced a water-use licensing backlog of thousands of applications, with processing times taking around 300 days. Municipal water losses exceed 40% in many areas, and several metros face the risk of day-zero supply interruptions. Bulk water infrastructure has been under-funded for years.

What has happened
  • Licence backlog cleared: Processing times cut from ~300 days to 90 days; 70%+ of licences now processed within 90 days via the re-engineered online Water Use Licence Application and Authorisation System (WULAAS).
  • WATERCOM established: The National Water Crisis Committee was set up and is now operational, driving the National Water Action Plan.
  • NWRIA operationalised: The new National Water Resources Infrastructure Agency is up and running.
  • Legislation: The Water Services Amendment Bill advanced; the Revised Raw Water Pricing Strategy implemented.
  • Private participation: Expanded project pipelines under the Water Partnerships Office and IFISA.
Where it stands now (2026)

Water is the best-performing reform stream - 100% on schedule. WATERCOM provides the central coordination that previously lacked, and the licensing reform is already delivering measurable economic value.

What is projected
  • The online licensing system is projected to unlock R43 billion to GDP per year for the next five years.
  • Bulk water infrastructure investment via NWRIA and blended finance (WPO/IFISA) to secure supply for metros and industry.
  • Municipal water services reform (Phase 2) to reduce non-revenue water losses.
  • Water Services Amendment Bill to strengthen regulation of water services authorities.
