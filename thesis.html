Prerequisites for HMMs

What you need to know before diving into Hidden Markov Models
Why Prerequisites Matter

Hidden Markov Models combine probability, sequences, and dynamic systems. A solid grasp of the topics below will make HMMs much easier to understand.

1. Basic Probability Concepts

Probability is the foundation of HMMs. You should be comfortable with:

  • Conditional Probability: The probability of event A happening given that B has already occurred.
    P(A|B) = P(A and B) / P(B)
  • Joint Probability: Probability that two or more events happen together.
  • Marginal Probability: The probability of an event regardless of other variables (obtained by summing or integrating over other variables).
  • Chain Rule: Decomposing joint probabilities: P(A,B,C) = P(A) × P(B|A) × P(C|A,B)
2. Bayes' Theorem

One of the most important tools in probabilistic modeling. It lets you update beliefs with new evidence.

P(A|B) = [P(B|A) × P(A)] / P(B)

In HMMs, we constantly use Bayes' theorem to infer hidden states from observations.

3. Markov Property (The Markov Assumption)

This is the core idea behind Markov Chains and HMMs.

Definition: The future state depends only on the current state, not on the sequence of past states.

P(S_{t+1} | S_t, S_{t-1}, ..., S_1) = P(S_{t+1} | S_t)

This "memoryless" property greatly simplifies computation in sequential models.

4. Markov Chains

A Markov Chain is a sequence of states where each state transition follows the Markov property.

Key elements:

  • States (e.g., Sunny, Rainy)
  • Transition probabilities (probability of moving from one state to another)
  • Initial state probabilities

Hidden Markov Models are an extension of Markov Chains where the states are not directly observable.

5. Sequences and Time Series

HMMs are designed for data that has order and evolves over time or steps.

Examples include:

  • Speech (sequence of sounds)
  • Text (sequence of words)
  • Stock prices or weather over days
  • DNA sequences in bioinformatics
6. Basic Linear Algebra (Helpful but not mandatory at first)

Understanding matrices helps when working with:

  • Transition matrices
  • Emission matrices
  • Matrix multiplication for efficient calculations
