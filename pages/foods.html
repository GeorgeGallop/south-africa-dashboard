🌽 Field Crops & Grains

Maize is South Africa's largest field crop by area and volume. Grown primarily in the Free State, Mpumalanga, North West, and KwaZulu-Natal, annual production averages 14-16 million tonnes. South Africa is typically a net exporter of maize (particularly white maize for human consumption and yellow maize for animal feed). The country is the largest maize producer in Africa.

Wheat is grown mainly in the Western Cape and Free State. Annual production of ~1.5-2 million tonnes falls short of domestic consumption (~3 million tonnes), making South Africa a net importer of wheat. The gap is filled by imports from the EU (mainly Germany, Poland, and the Baltic states).

Soybeans have seen explosive growth, expanding from ~500,000 tonnes in 2010 to over 2 million tonnes annually. This growth is driven by demand for protein meal in animal feed and by the crushing industry. Most production is in Mpumalanga and the Free State.

Other grains: Sunflower seed (600,000-800,000 tonnes/year), sorghum (100,000-200,000 tonnes), groundnuts (50,000-75,000 tonnes), and dry beans are produced in rotation with maize in the summer rainfall regions. Sunflower oil is a major domestic edible oil source.

🍊 Horticulture — Fruit & Nuts

South Africa's fruit industry is world-class, built on counter-seasonal advantage (Southern Hemisphere) and sophisticated cold-chain logistics.

🍇 Deciduous Fruit

Apples & Pears: South Africa is the second-largest exporter of fresh pears globally and a major apple exporter. The industry is concentrated in the Elgin, Grabouw, and Ceres regions of the Western Cape, with ~50,000 hectares under cultivation. Annual apple production is ~1 million tonnes; pear production ~500,000 tonnes.

Table grapes: The Northern Cape (Orange River region) and Western Cape produce ~300,000 tonnes annually, 80% of which is exported to the EU and UK during the November-May window.

Stone fruit: Peaches, plums, apricots, and nectarines are grown in the Western Cape. Significant volumes are canned (see Agro-Processing).

🍊 Citrus

Citrus is South Africa's largest fruit export category by value — over R30 billion in annual exports. The country is the world's second-largest exporter of fresh citrus after Spain. Oranges dominate (60% of volume), followed by soft citrus (clementines, mandarins), lemons, and grapefruit.

Growing regions: Eastern Cape (Sundays River Valley, Addo, Patensie), Limpopo (Mookgophong, Zebediela, Hoedspruit), Western Cape (Citrusdal, Paarl), and Mpumalanga (Kiepersol, Nelspruit). The industry is export-driven, with the EU absorbing ~40% of exports, the Middle East ~20%, and Asia (including China) a rapidly growing market.

Avocados: Production has more than doubled in the past decade to ~150,000 tonnes, driven by surging global demand. Limpopo and Mpumalanga are the primary regions. The Hass variety dominates. Exports go mainly to the EU and UK.

Nuts: Macadamia production has grown exponentially — South Africa is now the world's largest producer of macadamias, with ~60,000 tonnes annually. Pecan nuts are also grown in the Northern Cape and Free State.

🍌 Subtropical Fruit

Bananas: Grown in KwaZulu-Natal, Limpopo, and Mpumalanga. ~400,000 tonnes/year consumed almost entirely domestically.

Mangoes: Limpopo and KwaZulu-Natal produce ~100,000 tonnes annually. Significant volumes go into juice concentrate, dried mango, and nectars.

Pineapples: Grown in the Eastern Cape (Bathurst area) and KwaZulu-Natal. Most produce is canned or juiced.

🍷 Viticulture & Wine

South Africa has been producing wine since the 17th century. The Western Cape is the wine heartland, with the Stellenbosch, Paarl, Franschhoek, and Robertson valleys being the most famous regions. The area under vines is ~90,000 hectares.

Annual wine production averages 8-10 million hectolitres, making South Africa the 8th-largest wine producer in the world. The industry is export-oriented, with ~40% of production exported to the UK, Germany, the EU, the US, and increasingly to China and Africa.

Key varietals: Chenin Blanc (the most planted variety — South Africa is the world's largest grower of Chenin), Sauvignon Blanc, Chardonnay, Cabernet Sauvignon, Shiraz, and Pinotage (South Africa's signature variety, a cross between Pinot Noir and Cinsaut).

The wine industry is a major employer in the Western Cape, providing ~270,000 direct and indirect jobs. Wine tourism is a significant contributor to the tourism economy, with cellar tours, wine festivals, and restaurant destinations throughout the Cape winelands.

🐄 Livestock & Animal Products

Beef: South Africa has a large beef cattle herd of ~14 million head. The beef industry is concentrated in the Free State, Northern Cape, Eastern Cape, and Limpopo. The Brahman, Bonsmara, and Afrikaner breeds are dominant. Feedlot and grass-fed systems coexist. South Africa is broadly self-sufficient in beef, with small volumes exported to neighbouring countries.

Sheep & Wool: South Africa's sheep population is ~20 million, concentrated in the Karoo, Northern Cape, and Eastern Cape. The country is one of the world's leading producers of fine Merino wool, exporting the vast majority to China and Italy. Mutton and lamb are important in domestic meat consumption, particularly in the Western and Northern Cape.

Goats: The Boer goat breed was developed in South Africa and is now exported worldwide. The goat population is ~6 million, primarily in the Eastern Cape and Limpopo. Goat meat (chevon) is popular in traditional and emerging markets.

Poultry: The poultry industry is South Africa's largest agricultural sub-sector by value. Annual production exceeds 2 million tonnes of chicken meat, supplying ~70% of domestic consumption (the balance is imported, mainly from Brazil and the US, subject to anti-dumping duties). The industry is concentrated in Mpumalanga, the Free State, and the Western Cape. Major players: Astral Foods, RCL Foods, Quantum Foods.

Eggs: South Africa produces ~600 million eggs per month, making it the largest egg producer in Africa. The industry is highly commercialised with vertically integrated producers.

Pork: The pig herd is ~1.6 million, with production concentrated in the North West, Mpumalanga, and Free State. Self-sufficiency is ~80%, with imports filling the gap (mainly EU ham and bacon cuts).

🥛 Dairy

South Africa's dairy sector produces ~3-3.5 billion litres of milk annually. The industry is concentrated in the Western Cape, Eastern Cape, KwaZulu-Natal, and the Free State. The dairy herd is ~1.2 million cows, with Holstein and Jersey breeds dominant.

Key dairy products: fresh milk, UHT milk, cheese (cheddar, gouda, mozzarella — ~120,000 tonnes/year), yoghurt, butter, and milk powder. The industry is largely self-sufficient, with some imports of specialised cheese and butter blends. However, the sector is challenged by input cost increases (feed, electricity) and volatile retail margins.

Major players: Clover, Parmalat (Lactalis), Woodlands Dairy, and Lancewood.

🍬 Sugar

South Africa is the leading sugar producer in Africa (excluding Sudan and Egypt). Annual production is ~2-2.5 million tonnes, from sugarcane grown in KwaZulu-Natal (80%) and Mpumalanga (20%). The industry employs ~65,000 direct workers and supports 1 million livelihoods through the value chain.

Sugarcane is grown by both large estates (such as those owned by Tongaat Hulett and Illovo) and ~20,000 small-scale growers. Raw sugar is processed at 14 sugar mills and then refined for domestic consumption (South Africa consumes ~1.5 million tonnes/year) and for export. The remaining ~500,000-800,000 tonnes is exported to the African continent, the US (under the AGOA quota), and the EU.

The sugar industry also produces molasses (used in bioethanol and animal feed) and bagasse (burned in cogeneration plants to produce electricity — see Biomass).

🥦 Vegetables

South Africa's vegetable production is diverse and largely geared toward the domestic fresh market and processing industry.

Potatoes: The largest vegetable crop by volume — ~2.5 million tonnes annually, grown across the country. Free State, Mpumalanga, and Western Cape are the main regions. The potato value chain is highly sophisticated, with large cold storage and grading facilities.

Onions, tomatoes, carrots, cabbage: Grown year-round in different regions, supplying fresh produce markets and processing into sauces, soups, canned vegetables, and frozen products.

Greenhouse vegetables: Protected agriculture is growing rapidly, with large investments in high-tech greenhouses for tomatoes, peppers, cucumbers, and berries — mainly in Gauteng, Limpopo, and the Western Cape.

🐟 Fisheries & Aquaculture

South Africa's fishing industry includes inshore and deep-sea fisheries. Major species: hake (the largest fishery, mainly for export — South Africa is one of the world's leading hake exporters), Cape lobster, squid, abalone, small pelagics (anchovy, sardine — for fishmeal and fish oil for the animal feed industry), and tuna.

Aquaculture is a small but growing sector, producing ~15,000 tonnes annually of rainbow trout (mainly for the domestic market), abalone (exported to East Asia), and oysters. Marine aquaculture (salmon, mussels) is concentrated in Saldanha Bay and St Helena Bay in the Western Cape. Freshwater aquaculture (tilapia, catfish, carp) is still limited but has significant growth potential in Limpopo and Mpumalanga, supported by government programmes.
