Transitional Generation Pricing & Vesting Contract Framework

NERSA’s shock absorber for SAWEM: a cost-reflective strike price and two-way vesting settlement so generators and customers are not thrown straight into a free market.

What it is

The framework sits between today’s MYPD-style regulated generation revenue and a competitive SAWEM. Dispatch still follows the market (SRMC offers set the clearing price). What changes is the money: a NERSA-set strike price is used as a benchmark, not a cap or a floor. Vesting contracts between NTCSA’s Central Purchasing Agency and generators (and, downstream, distributors) then credit or debit the difference on a vested volume so realised revenue moves toward a cost-reflective benchmark instead of swinging with every market spike.

NERSA describes the transition as about five to ten years, stepping down toward 2030. Contract prices, duration and unwind stay under NERSA approval.

How the Arnot example works

The figure below is NERSA’s integrated regulatory outcome demonstration. Same plant, same month, same costs — only the market price changes.

  • Benchmark (Parts 1–4): capacity R653.4m + ancillary R64.2m + SRMC energy R437.8m + 18% GRRB R208.0m = R1 363.4 million. Divided by 1 070 000 MWh of sent-out energy gives a strike of R1 274.3/MWh.
  • Vested volume: 70% of sent-out energy = 749 000 MWh. The other 30% is pure market exposure.
  • Scenario A — market R900/MWh (below strike): strike − market = R374.3/MWh. Vesting settlement = 374.3 × 749 000 = R280.5 million CREDIT. Realised generator revenue (RGRO) = R1 961.1 million. Upward vesting partially bridges the gap to the benchmark.
  • Scenario B — market R1 600/MWh (above strike): market − strike = R325.7/MWh. Vesting settlement = R243.9 million DEBIT. RGRO = R2 185.7 million. Downward vesting clips the inframarginal windfall.

Key takeaways on the figure: dispatch stays market-based; the strike is a reference, not a market cap; settlements are symmetric; RGRO is regulated recovery plus market exposure; the point is stability while the market is still thin.

What to watch
