LNG — Liquefied Natural Gas

LNG is South Africa’s fastest route to meaningful gas volumes while domestic offshore and onshore supply is developed. This section covers the full LNG value chain: overview, regasification infrastructure, and floating storage regasification units (FSRUs).

What LNG means for South Africa

South Africa has very limited domestic gas production today. LNG imports bridge that gap by bringing globally traded gas to the country through coastal terminals. The LNG is shipped in cryogenic tankers, regasified on arrival, and fed into pipelines for power generation and industrial use.

The 2024 Gas Master Plan identifies LNG import and regasification capacity as the near-term anchor for the entire gas strategy — without it, the country cannot supply the gas-fired power plants and industrial users that the energy transition depends on.

LNG value chain overview

The LNG value chain in South Africa follows four stages:

  • Liquefaction & shipping — LNG is liquefied at source (e.g. Qatar, Mozambique, US Gulf) and transported in specialised cryogenic carriers to South African ports.
  • Regasification — at the receiving terminal, the LNG is converted back to gas via heat exchange and delivered into the pipeline network (→ full regasification section).
  • Transmission & storage — gas flows through trunk pipelines to industrial clusters and power plants, with buffer storage for demand balancing.
  • End use — gas-fired power generation, industrial heat, petrochemical feedstock, and potentially hydrogen production.
Proposed LNG import locations
  • Saldanha Bay (Western Cape) — the priority site identified by the Gas Master Plan for a large-scale LNG import terminal and gas hub.
  • Richards Bay (KwaZulu-Natal) — proposed for additional import or FSRU capacity to serve coastal industrial demand and power plants.
  • Durban / KwaZulu-Natal — another candidate location for an LNG terminal serving the industrial and petrochemical cluster in the region.
Who is involved
  • DMRE — policy, licensing and regulatory framework.
  • Transnet National Ports Authority — port site planning and logistics for coastal terminals.
  • PetroSA — strategic gas hub planning and offtake design.
  • International LNG specialists such as Shell, TotalEnergies, Karpowership, and Golar LNG — potential terminal developers and operators.
  • EPC firms with LNG experience: Worley, TechnipFMC, McDermott, Saipem, Fluor.
