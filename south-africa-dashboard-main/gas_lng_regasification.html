Regasification

The process of converting imported LNG back into gaseous form and delivering it into South Africa's pipeline network for power generation and industrial use.

What regasification involves

LNG arrives in South Africa at approximately −162 °C in pressurised cryogenic tankers. Regasification terminals receive the cargo, store the LNG in insulated tanks, and convert it back to gas by warming it — typically using seawater heat exchangers or submerged combustion vaporisers. The resulting natural gas is then compressed into transmission pipelines.

Regasification can take place onshore (dedicated import terminals with storage tanks) or offshore (using FSRU vessels that do the regasification at sea). Both models are under active consideration in South Africa.

Key regasification projects
  • Saldanha Bay LNG Terminal — the priority large-scale onshore regasification terminal identified in the Gas Master Plan. Located on the Western Cape coast, it is designed to serve both the Cape Town metro and the inland industrial corridor with pipeline-grade gas.
  • Richards Bay Regasification — an onshore or near-shore terminal proposed for KwaZulu-Natal to supply gas to the Richards Bay industrial complex and potential gas-to-power plants.
  • Durban / KwaZulu-Natal regas capacity — additional terminal capacity to support the petrochemical cluster and coastal demand nodes.
Timeline and readiness
  • 2024–2026: Environmental impact assessments, site selection, port readiness studies and front-end engineering for Saldanha and Richards Bay terminals.
  • 2025–2027: Procurement and contracting for terminal construction or FSRU deployment.
  • 2026–2028: First regasification capacity expected online, synchronised with gas-to-power plant commissioning.
How regasification connects to the wider gas market

Regasification terminals are the bridge between the global LNG spot market and South Africa's domestic gas network. The speed at which these terminals can be built or deployed determines how quickly gas-fired power plants can operate at baseload. Without regas capacity, the LNG imported into South African ports cannot reach the power plants and factories that need it.
