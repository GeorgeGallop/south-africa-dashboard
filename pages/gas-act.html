⚖️ Gas Act, 2001 (No. 48 of 2001)

The Statutory Foundation of South Africa's Piped Gas Industry

The Gas Act is the primary piece of legislation governing South Africa's piped gas industry. It promotes the orderly development of gas transmission, storage, distribution, liquefaction and re-gasification, and establishes the national regulatory framework — creating a regulator as the custodian and enforcer of the rules that govern how gas is transported, traded and sold.

What the Act Sets Out To Do (The Objects)

Section 2 of the Act lists ten objects that give the law its purpose:

1. Orderly Development

Promote the efficient, effective, sustainable and orderly development and operation of gas transmission, storage, distribution, liquefaction and re-gasification facilities.

2. Facilitate Investment

Create the conditions to attract private and public investment into the gas industry.

3. Safety & Environment

Ensure the safe, efficient, economic and environmentally responsible transmission, distribution, storage, liquefaction and re-gasification of gas.

4. Transformation (HDSA)

Promote companies in the gas industry owned or controlled by historically disadvantaged South Africans, through licence conditions, so they can become competitive.

5. Equitable Service

Ensure gas services are provided on an equitable basis and that the interests and needs of all parties are taken into consideration.

6. Skills & Employment Equity

Promote skills among employees and promote employment equity in the gas industry.

7. Competitive Markets

Promote the development of competitive markets for gas and gas services.

8. Regional Trade & Access

Facilitate gas trade between the Republic and other countries, and promote access to gas in an affordable and safe manner.

The Rules — What Requires a Licence

Section 15 is the heart of the Act. No person may, without a licence issued by the regulator:

1. Construct Gas Facilities

Construct gas transmission, storage, distribution, liquefaction and re-gasification facilities — or convert existing infrastructure into such facilities.

2. Operate Gas Facilities

Operate gas transmission, storage, distribution, liquefaction or re-gasification facilities.

3. Trade in Gas

Buy and sell gas as a commodity (excluding the construction and operation of transmission, storage and distribution systems).

Certain activities in Schedule 1 are exempt from the licence requirement but must still be registered with the regulator (s.15(2), s.28). Production and importation of gas must also be registered (s.28).

How a Licence Is Obtained — The Process

1

Apply (s.16)

Submit a prescribed application including company details, owners, financial/technical abilities, facility description, customer/tariff plans, and gas specification.

2

Advertise (s.17)

Publish a notice in at least two newspapers in the area, in two official languages (one must be English), allowing objections by affidavit.

3

Objections (s.18)

The regulator must furnish the applicant with substantiated objections; may direct plan changes for health, safety or environmental compliance.

4

Decision (s.19)

The regulator must decide within 60 days. Separate licences are issued for construction, operation, and trading.

The Key Licence Conditions (s.21)

  • Non-discrimination & open access: Third parties must have access on commercially reasonable terms to uncommitted capacity in transmission pipelines and storage facilities.
  • No cross-subsidisation: Vertically integrated companies must manage gas transmission, storage, distribution, trading, liquefaction and re-gasification activities separately — with separate accounts and no cross-subsidisation.
  • Interconnection: Licensees must allow interconnections with the facilities of suppliers, transmitters, storage companies, distributors, reticulators and eligible customers where technically feasible.
  • Exclusive distribution areas: A distributor may be granted an exclusive geographic area (for a particular range of gas specifications), but must supply any person in that area on request if economically viable.
  • Price regulation: Maximum prices for distributors, reticulators and consumers must be approved by the regulator where there is inadequate competition (as contemplated in the Competition Act).
  • Transformation: Licensees must provide information on the participation of historically disadvantaged South Africans in their activities.
  • Safety advisory: Trading licensees must provide an advisory service on the safe and efficient use, handling and storage of gas to customers other than eligible customers.
  • Obligation to supply: All customers in a licensed distribution area (except eligible customers and reticulators) must purchase their gas from the licensed distribution company for that area.
  • Maintenance: Licensees must maintain their facilities in a fully operative condition and meet fixed time periods for becoming operational.

Enforcement — What Happens If You Breach the Rules

Compliance Notice (s.26(1))

If a licensee contravenes a licence condition or the Act, the regulator serves a notice directing compliance within a reasonable period.

Financial Penalty (s.26(2))

If the notice is not complied with, the regulator sits as a tribunal and may impose a fine of up to R2,000,000 per day for each day the contravention continues.

Court-Ordered Suspension (s.27)

The regulator may apply to the High Court for an order suspending or revoking a licence where grounds justify it (e.g., failure to carry out the activity for which the licence was granted).

Reviews & Appeals

An aggrieved person may challenge licence conditions (s.21(2)) and penalties (s.26(4)) before the High Court. Decisions must be in writing, reasoned, and available to the public (s.10).

The regulator may also investigate complaints from customers about gas supply, unreasonable or excessive prices and tariffs, and unreasonable differences in supply (s.31); or act as mediator or arbitrator in disputes (s.30).

Note: While the Gas Act remains the foundational statute for the piped gas industry, its regulatory functions are now performed by NERSA (National Energy Regulator of South Africa) following the 2004 amendment. Upstream exploration and production are separately governed by the Mineral and Petroleum Resources Development Act (MPRDA), while National Environmental Management Act (NEMA) governs the environmental authorisation of fracking activities (see Hydraulic Fracturing Regulations).
