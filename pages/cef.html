Central Energy Fund (CEF)

The state's holding company for oil, gas, strategic fuel and energy minerals — 100% government-owned

CEF SOC Ltd is a Schedule 2 state-owned company. The Republic of South Africa is the only shareholder. The Minister of Mineral and Petroleum Resources holds the shares on behalf of the state. PetroSA is not the parent — it is one of CEF's subsidiaries, alongside SFF, iGas, PASA, AEMFC and the newly operational SANPC. SAPREF, the Durban refinery formerly owned by Shell and BP, was sold to CEF for R1 and is now state-owned too.

Legal form: CEF SOC Ltd — Schedule 2 public entity
Shareholder: Republic of South Africa (100%). No listed stock, no private controlling owner.
Shares held by: Minister of Mineral and Petroleum Resources (DMPR), on behalf of the state
Mandate: Central Energy Fund Act 38 of 1977 — security of energy supply through exploration, acquisition, development, marketing and partnerships
Founded: 2 November 1954 (current statutory form 1977)
Head office: Sandton / Midrand (CEF House)
Also manages: the Equalisation Fund on behalf of government
Private companies around it: Sasol at Natref, traders and storage JVs sit next to the state's assets. The fund itself is 100% government.

The hierarchy

What CEF is for

CEF exists because the apartheid state built a parallel oil-and-fuel system under sanctions, and the democratic state kept the holding company. The 1977 Act is still the mandate: finance and promote coal, liquid fuels, oil, and "any other form of energy." In practice the group covers:

  • Upstream oil and gas — PetroSA's offshore fields and Ghana; PASA's licensing of the Orange Basin and South Coast.
  • Midstream gas — iGas's stake in the Mozambique–Secunda ROMPCO pipeline.
  • Strategic stocks and storage — SFF's Saldanha caverns and Milnerton tanks.
  • Refining — PetroSA's Mossel Bay GTL (care and maintenance) and SAPREF in Durban (acquired, idle).
  • Energy minerals — AEMFC coal for Eskom.
  • The Equalisation Fund — a levy/refund mechanism on petroleum products, administered by CEF for the state, not a commercial subsidiary.

The 2025–26 reshaping

Two moves define the current group. First, Cabinet's 2020 decision to merge PetroSA, SFF and iGas into a single South African National Petroleum Company. That merger became operational in 2025: 402 staff transferred in phase one, assets moved under a lease-and-assign model so PetroSA's legacy liabilities stay ring-fenced, and CEF committed about R5 billion of phased funding. Treasury's 2026 Vote 34 still lists the old subsidiaries and SANPC, because the legal consolidation is not finished.

Second, CEF bought SAPREF from Shell Downstream SA and bpSA for a nominal R1 after the April 2022 Durban floods knocked the plant offline. Competition Commission approval came in September 2024. The site — land, tanks, process units, Island View pipelines, the single buoy mooring — is now a CEF asset. Restart and a possible capacity lift from ~180,000 bpd are the stated plan; environmental liabilities are the reason Shell and BP were willing to walk away for a rand.

PASA and AEMFC stay outside the oil-company merger. PASA is the upstream regulator/promoter. AEMFC is the state's miner. Both still report up through CEF to the Minister.

What CEF is not

CEF is not listed. It is not a private oil major. Sasol's Natref stake, commercial fuel traders, and storage joint ventures sit in the same industry and sometimes on adjacent sites — they do not own the fund. The kids (PetroSA, SFF, iGas, SANPC, PASA, AEMFC, SAPREF) are the same: no listed stock, no private controlling owner.
