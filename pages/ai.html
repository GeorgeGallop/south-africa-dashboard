Why SA? Strong fibre connectivity, the continent's most developed enterprise market, financial-services demand, a stable legal environment and POPIA data-residency requirements make South Africa the natural hub.

Data centre build-out

South Africa is the largest data centre market in Africa, with ~55 existing facilities and rapid hyperscale expansion driven by cloud-first enterprise strategies, AI workloads and the entry of hyperscalers. Johannesburg is the dominant hub (>70% of capacity), followed by Cape Town.

Operator Position
Teraco (Digital Realty) Largest provider; JB1–JB4 (Johannesburg), CT1–CT2 (Cape Town). CT2 expansion to 50MW critical IT load.
Vantage Data Centers Major hyperscale campuses (~100MW+ of capacity across Johannesburg and Cape Town).
Open Access Data Centres (OADC) Rapid national rollout (JHB, CPT, Durban, Gqeberha) targeting enterprise and AI workloads.
Africa Data Centres Pan-African operator with facilities in Johannesburg and Cape Town, expanding for AI.
Others Uptime, CtrlS, Atlas Tower, NTT, Vodacom/Maziv, BCX, MetroFibre Networx / Altron, etc.

Hyperscale segment: valued at ~USD 1.34bn (2025), forecast to reach USD 3.66bn by 2031 — an 18.27% CAGR, among the fastest-growing in the world.

The investment wave — what's happening now
  • Microsoft's R5.4bn AI announcement (March 2025): Microsoft expands its SA cloud and AI infrastructure by end-2027, on top of its earlier long-term commitment (reported R20.4bn). Announced at the "Building an AI Economy" event in Bryanston — build-out of data centres, AI capacity, and land acquisition for future growth.
  • Google's Johannesburg region (Jan 2024): ~R2.5bn+ local investment; Google now routes Africa traffic through SA and has expanded AI services (Gemini, Vertex AI) locally.
  • AI-ready infrastructure: Providers are building high-density GPU-ready facilities. Teraco, Vantage and OADC are racing to meet AI/ML server demand.
  • Government push: SA's national AI policy/plan and the Presidential Commission on the Fourth Industrial Revolution signal official backing; data centres are increasingly treated as strategic economic infrastructure.
  • R6+ trillion potential: McKinsey estimates AI could add substantial value to South Africa's GDP by 2030 if adoption accelerates.

Sources: Microsoft, AWS, Google Cloud, Teraco, ResearchAndMarkets/Africa Data Centres database, MyBroadband, DC market reports (2025–26).

Challenges & watch items
  • Electricity: high-density AI data centres need firm power — load-shedding history makes PPAs, wheeling and embedded generation critical.
  • Skills gap: demand for AI/ML engineers far outstrips local supply.
  • Data localisation vs. cost: POPIA encourages local hosting, but AI training often still leverages global regions.
  • Cape Town Azure region: Microsoft's pause there showed infra decisions can shift — Johannesburg is now the undisputed hub.
