Province profile

Eastern Cape

The Eastern Cape combines a strong coastal economy with deep rural heritage, famous liberation history, and major transport corridors linking Gqeberha, East London, and the inland heartland.

At a glance

  • Capital: Bhisho
  • Population: About 7.1 million
  • Largest city: Gqeberha
  • Known for: Auto manufacturing, agriculture, tourism

Population

The province is home to roughly 7.1 million people, with a large share of residents living in rural areas and along the coastal metro corridors. It remains one of South Africa’s most populous provinces.

Brief history

The Eastern Cape is rooted in Xhosa heritage, frontier-era conflicts, missionary settlements, and powerful liberation movements. It is also the birthplace of several national leaders, including Nelson Mandela.

Key industries

Automotive production in Gqeberha and East London, agriculture, agro-processing, tourism, education, and port-linked logistics are central to the provincial economy.

Major cities & towns

Gqeberha, East London, Mthatha, Bhisho, Makhanda, Komani, and Cradock anchor the main urban and service hubs.

Climate

Coastal areas have a mild to subtropical climate, while the interior is drier and more continental. Rainfall is higher along the coast and escarpment than in the Karoo interior.

Natural resources

Fertile agricultural land, livestock, freshwater catchments, coastal fisheries, wind potential, and scenic landscapes support both economic activity and conservation.

Future outlook

The Eastern Cape’s outlook is tied to expanding logistics, renewable energy, automotive investment, and stronger rural value chains. Better roads, ports, and industrial support could unlock more jobs across the province.
