South32 / Samancor

Globally Diversified Mining & Metals Company — Manganese, Chrome & Aluminium Operations in Southern Africa

South32 is a globally diversified mining and metals company spun out of BHP Billiton in 2015. Its Southern African operations are centred on manganese mining (Kalahari Basin), ferrochrome smelting (Samancor Chrome), and aluminium smelting (Hillside in Richards Bay, Mozal in Mozambique). The company is a critical supplier of manganese and chrome alloys to the global steel industry and a major employer in South Africa's industrial heartland.

History & Origins
  • 2015: South32 was created via the demerger of a portfolio of non-core assets from BHP Billiton. The name "South32" references the 32 degrees south latitude that passes through most of the company's assets in Australia and Southern Africa. The listing on the ASX, JSE, and LSE was one of the largest demergers in mining history.
  • Pre-demerger history (BHP Billiton era): The South African assets — Samancor Manganese, Samancor Chrome, Hillside Aluminium — were originally part of BHP Billiton's South African portfolio. Samancor was formed in the 1970s as a state-backed chrome and manganese producer. Hillside Aluminium (Richards Bay) was built in the 1990s as a strategic investment to beneficiate South African coal (for power) into aluminium for export.
  • 2015–2020s: As an independent company, South32 streamlined the portfolio, selling non-core assets and focusing on its four key commodities: manganese, aluminium, copper, and zinc. In 2024, the company announced a strategy pivot toward copper and future-facing commodities, while maintaining its South African manganese and chrome as high-margin cash generators.
Southern African Operations
South Africa Manganese (Kalahari Basin)

Mamatwan Mine — open-cut manganese mine operating since the 1960s, producing high-grade lumpy manganese ore.

Wessels Mine — underground manganese mine, producing higher-grade ore for specialised alloy markets.

Importance: South Africa holds ~80% of the world's known manganese reserves, and South32 is one of the largest global producers of both manganese ore and alloy. Manganese is essential for steelmaking (it removes impurities and adds strength). No functional steel industry exists without manganese.

Samancor Chrome (Ferrochrome Smelters)

Plants: Ferrochrome smelters at Rustenburg (North West) and Brits (North West), using submerged arc furnace (SAF) technology to convert chromite ore into high-carbon ferrochrome.

Capacity: ~1.4 million tonnes of ferrochrome per year.

Importance: Chrome is essential for stainless steel production. South Africa holds ~70% of global chromite reserves. Samancor Chrome is one of the world's largest ferrochrome producers, supplying stainless steel mills in China, Europe, and the United States.

Hillside Aluminium (Richards Bay)

Type: Primary aluminium smelter with capacity of ~720,000 tonnes per year.

Feedstock: Imports alumina (aluminium oxide) from Australia and Brazil. Hillside uses the Pechiney AP-30 smelting technology.

Importance: Hillside is the largest aluminium smelter in Africa and a major source of export revenue. It consumes ~1,200 MW of electricity (from Eskom), making it one of the largest single electricity users in the country. The smelter operates at a competitive disadvantage due to high Eskom tariffs.

Mozal Aluminium (Mozambique)

Type: Primary aluminium smelter near Maputo, capacity ~580,000 tonnes per year. South32 holds 47% ownership.

Power: Draws electricity from the Cahora Bassa hydroelectric dam on the Zambezi River — one of the lowest-carbon aluminium smelters in the world.

Strategic role: Mozal is South32's most competitive aluminium asset, benefiting from low-cost renewable power and proximity to export markets through Maputo port. It is operated by South32 and is a significant contributor to the Mozambique economy.

Smelting & Manufacturing Role

South32's Southern African operations are at the heart of the region's smelting and alloy manufacturing capacity:

  • Manganese alloy smelting: The company operates manganese sinter plants and alloy furnaces at its Metalloys facility (Meyerton, Gauteng), producing silicomanganese and ferromanganese used in steelmaking. This is a key step in beneficiating manganese ore into higher-value products before export.
  • Ferrochrome smelting: Samancor's submerged arc furnaces in Rustenburg and Brits convert chromite ore into ferrochrome, which is the critical input for stainless steel manufacturing. The furnaces consume large amounts of electricity (ferrochrome is an energy-intensive process), making the business highly sensitive to Eskom tariffs and supply reliability.
  • Aluminium smelting: Hillside is a world-class aluminium smelter using the prebake anode technology. Aluminium produced here is exported primarily to Asia and Europe, competing with smelters in the Middle East and China.
Why South32 Matters for South Africa
  • Manganese and chrome are strategic minerals: South Africa's dominance in global manganese and chrome reserves means South32's operations are geopolitically significant. Disruption to South32's production would impact global steel supply chains.
  • Export revenue: Manganese and ferrochrome are among South Africa's top mineral export earners. South32 together with other producers contributes billions of rand in foreign exchange.
  • Smelting capacity: South32 operates some of the largest smelters in Africa — if they close due to uncompetitive energy costs, South Africa loses its downstream processing capacity and becomes a raw-ore exporter only.
  • Employment: ~7,000 direct employees in South Africa and Mozambique, with tens of thousands more in the supply chain and transport logistics (Transnet rail and port services).
  • Energy transition exposure: Aluminium and manganese are "future-facing" commodities — aluminium for lightweight vehicles and renewable energy infrastructure, manganese for battery chemistry (lithium manganese oxide, LMO, and NMC cathodes). South32 is positioning for growing demand in these markets.
