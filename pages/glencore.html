Glencore

Global commodity trader and miner — coal, chrome, ferroalloys and the Cape Town refinery

Glencore plc is a Swiss-listed trading house with deep South African roots: both the current CEO (Gary Nagle) and the long-time architect (Ivan Glasenberg) are South African. In this country it is not one mine. It is thermal coal in Mpumalanga, the Glencore–Merafe chrome venture, vanadium, and — through Astron Energy — the Milnerton crude refinery and a national petrol-station network that used to be Caltex.

⛽ The Cape Town refinery: Astron Energy (formerly Chevron South Africa / Caltex) owns the Milnerton plant — one of the last crude refineries still running in the country.

Glencore

Glencore plc · JSE: GLN · the SA book is coal, chrome and Astron

How Glencore got here
  • Trading house, then miner: Marc Rich & Co (later Glencore) made its name moving oil and metals. The industrial footprint in South Africa arrived mainly through Xstrata — chrome furnaces, coal mines — which Glencore merged with in 2013.
  • Coal: Glencore Operations South Africa runs (and in some cases co-owns with ARM) Mpumalanga thermal mines including Goedgevonden (Glencore 74% / ARM 26%) and the PCB complex. Output is split between Richards Bay export and Eskom/domestic. 2025 attributable SA thermal production was 16.6 Mt.
  • Chrome: The Glencore–Merafe Chrome Venture is the other half of South Africa's ferrochrome duopoly (with Samancor). Mines on both Bushveld limbs feed Lion, Wonderkop and Boshoek. In 2025 the mines kept producing; the furnaces largely did not. See Chrome smelting.
  • Downstream oil (2018–19): Glencore South Africa Oil Investments bought 75% of Chevron South Africa — the Caltex retail network, Durban lubes plant, and the Cape Town (Milnerton) refinery — and renamed the company Astron Energy.
The South African book
Coal — Mpumalanga

Thermal coal for export and Eskom. Goedgevonden is the flagship JV with ARM; Glencore is the operator. Rail to Richards Bay is the constraint, same as every other Mpumalanga miner. Group thermal coal in SA was roughly flat in 2025; export tonnes rose, domestic tonnes fell.

Chrome & ferrochrome — Bushveld

Attributable chrome ore ~3.6 Mt in 2025. Ferrochrome collapsed to 436 kt (−63%) after Wonderkop, Boshoek and (partly) Lion were taken down. The 62c/kWh Eskom tariff in 2026 is the restart bet. Vanadium pentoxide (~18 million lb) sits in the same ferroalloys department.

Astron Energy — Cape Town

The only remaining coastal crude refinery still in private hands and actually running. ~100,000 bpd at Milnerton, plus ~700 Astron-branded forecourts (ex-Caltex) and a Durban lubricants plant. Separate page: Astron Energy.

Marketing

The Swiss parent is still a trader first. South African coal, chrome ore and (when the furnaces run) ferrochrome move through Glencore's book into China, India and the Atlantic basin. That is why ore kept shipping when the smelters stopped: the trading desk had a bid.

Why it matters here

Glencore is one of the few private groups that sits on both sides of South Africa's energy-minerals problem: it digs the coal that Eskom burns, it runs the furnaces Eskom's tariff almost killed, and it owns a refinery that the state does not. SAPREF is now CEF's; Natref is Sasol/TotalEnergies; Astron is Glencore. If Milnerton ever stops, the Western Cape is on imported product with no local crude unit behind it.

It is also the operator in the chrome story this dashboard already tracks: mines running, furnaces idle, ore to China. A Glencore decision to restart or not restart Lion and Wonderkop is a national ferrochrome decision, not just a mine-plan update.
