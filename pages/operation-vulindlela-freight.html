Freight Logistics

Reversing the collapse of rail and ports under Transnet opening the network to private participation and competition via the Freight Logistics Roadmap.

The problem

Transnet's rail and port performance collapsed over the past decade, driven by underinvestment, cable theft, vandalism, locomotive availability failures and operational mismanagement. Rail volumes fell from about 226 Mt in 2017/18 to well under 150 Mt, forcing freight onto roads and driving up the cost of exports. Port congestion at Durban, Ngqura and Cape Town repeatedly disrupted supply chains.

What has happened
  • Private train operators: The latest Network Statement was published to grant third-party operators access to the freight rail network; Rail Access Agreements concluded with 11 train operating companies.
  • Port terminals: Private sector participation introduced in container terminals; an implementation plan developed for TNPA corporatisation.
  • National Rail Bill: A draft National Rail Bill was finalised to modernise the legislative framework for rail transport and entrench open access.
  • Operational recovery: Improved locomotive availability and corridor-specific recovery plans on coal and chrome lines.
Where it stands now (2026)

Freight logistics is the weakest stream: 60% of reforms are delayed, only 40% on track. Analysts note that institutional and operational constraints at Transnet continue to limit the pace and scale of reform. The heavy-tonnage exports (coal, iron ore, chrome) remain well below historical capacity.

What is projected
  • Vertical separation of rail infrastructure (TRIM) and operations, and of port infrastructure (TNPA) from terminal operators - enabling competition and private capital.
  • Rail volumes recovering toward corridor targets, taking trucks off the road and cutting logistics costs.
  • Private operators scaling on the general freight and container networks.
  • Port efficiency improvements to make South Africa's export corridors globally competitive.
