Refining & Purification

Stage 4 of the process chain — turning crude metal and matte into commercially pure metals and chemical intermediates ready for alloying and sale.

What refining is

Refining, or purification, is the stage that takes the crude metal or matte produced by smelting and removes the remaining impurities to deliver a product of commercial or specified purity. Where smelting separates metal from bulk slag, refining separates metal from the other metals and chemicals it is still mixed with — often to purities of 99% to 99.99%.

The techniques are broadly similar across metals but the route differs sharply by commodity: precious metals are separated by solvent extraction and precipitation, base metals by electrorefining, and reactive or specialty metals by electrolysis, distillation or chemical conversion. Refining is frequently the highest-value, most technically demanding link in the chain.

The refining workflow
  • Feed preparation: crude metal, matte or spent electrolyte is prepared for the chosen refining route, often by grinding, roasting or dissolution.
  • Leaching and dissolution: the metal is dissolved in acid, alkali or molten salt so that it can be separated chemically.
  • Separation: solvent extraction, ion exchange, precipitation or distillation split the target metal from the others present in solution.
  • Pyrometallurgical refining: fire refining, cupellation, distillation and vacuum degassing purify metals that are best treated at high temperature.
  • Electrorefining: an impure anode is dissolved in an electrolytic cell and the pure metal plates onto the cathode — the standard route for copper, nickel and precious metals.
  • Electrowinning: metal is plated out of solution onto a cathode — used for zinc, manganese, copper and refinery by-products.
  • Product finishing: the refined metal is cast into ingots, bars, flakes or sponge, or converted into a chemical form such as oxide, sulphate or salt.
Key refining technologies
Technology How it works Common applications
Solvent extraction An organic solvent selectively pulls one metal out of an aqueous solution, then releases it back in a purified strip stream PGM separation, cobalt, nickel, rare earths, zinc
Electrorefining Impure metal dissolves at the anode and plates as pure metal at the cathode in an electrolytic cell Copper, nickel, gold, silver, lead
Electrowinning Metal in solution is deposited on the cathode by passing a current through the electrolyte Zinc, manganese, copper, cobalt
Chemical precipitation Reagents are added to drop a target compound out of solution as a solid PGM salts, nickel, cobalt, rare-earth carbonates
Ion exchange Resins capture specific ions from solution and release them during elution Uranium, rare earths, ultra-pure metals
Distillation & sublimation Differences in boiling point separate volatile metals and chemicals Zinc, mercury, titanium tetrachloride
Fire & chemical refining Oxidation, chlorination or flux treatment removes residual impurities at high temperature Copper anodes, gold doré, lead bullion
Refining by commodity
Platinum Group Metals (PGMs)

PGM matte is converted and leached to dissolve the base metals, leaving a precious-metals residue that is separated by solvent extraction and precipitation into platinum, palladium, rhodium, ruthenium, iridium and gold. South Africa hosts the world's largest such complex at Anglo American Platinum's Precious Metals Refinery in Springs, with Impala Refining Services nearby and a base-metals refinery at Polokwane.

Gold

Gold doré is treated by chlorination, smelting and electrolytic or chemical refining to reach 99.99% purity. Rand Refinery in Germiston — the world's largest single-site precious-metals refinery — and the independent ERSA works in Wadeville are the country's main gold refineries.

Copper and base metals

Copper is fire-refined then electrorefined into 99.99% cathode. Palabora's copper complex produces anodes and rod, while nickel and cobalt are refined by solvent extraction and electrowinning.

Zinc

Zinc concentrate is roasted, leached and purified by solvent extraction before electrowinning into special high-grade (SHG) zinc metal — the route used at the (care-and-maintenance) Skorpion refinery and planned for the Gamsberg complex.

Vanadium

Vanadium intermediates are chemically refined into vanadium pentoxide flake and ammonium metavanadate at operations such as Vametco, feeding steel and energy-storage markets.

Manganese

Manganese Metal Company (MMC) in Mbombela is the only South African producer of refined electrolytic manganese metal and is adding high-purity manganese sulphate for batteries.

Rare earths and titanium

Rare-earth concentrates are cracked and separated into individual oxides; titanium feedstock is refined into TiO₂ pigment. Nyanza Light Metals is building Africa's only sulphate-process TiO₂ pigment plant at Richards Bay.

Why refining matters

Refining captures the highest per-unit value in the metals chain and is the stage most often located close to the final customer rather than the mine. Historically, much of South Africa's matte and concentrate has been refined offshore, exporting value that could be earned domestically.

It is also intensely energy- and skills-intensive: electrolytic refining consumes large amounts of electricity, and precious-metals refining depends on scarce metallurgical and chemical expertise. Building and keeping refining capacity onshore is a stated objective of South African mineral-beneficiation policy.
