Rio Tinto

Richards Bay Minerals — South Africa’s largest mineral-sands miner and titanium-slag processor

Rio Tinto’s South African book is not iron ore and not PGMs. It is heavy mineral sands: dunes of ilmenite, rutile and zircon north of Richards Bay, mined by dredge, split in a mineral-separation plant, then smelted into titanium slag and pig iron. The operating company is Richards Bay Minerals (RBM) — 74% Rio Tinto, 24% B-BBEE. It is the country’s largest mineral-sands producer and one of the few places here where mining and furnace processing sit on the same site.

What mineral sands actually are

Coastal dunes in northern KZN hold a mix of heavy minerals washed out of the hinterland over geological time. The valuable fraction is:

  • Ilmenite (FeTiO3) — the bulk of the ore. Smelted into titanium slag (feedstock for TiO2 pigment: paint, plastics, sunscreen, paper) plus a pig-iron by-product.
  • Rutile (TiO2) — higher-grade titanium mineral, sold as concentrate.
  • Zircon (ZrSiO4) — ceramics, foundry sands, nuclear cladding. High-value, smaller volume.

Unlike a Bushveld underground mine, this is a placer: the “orebody” is a dune. Mining is dredging and hydraulic mining, not a shaft. Processing is gravity / magnetic / electrostatic separation, then furnaces. Tronox (Namakwa Sands, Fairbreeze) and MRC (Tormin) do a similar chain on the west coast and KZN; RBM is the largest.

The South African book — mining and processing on one lease
Mining — Zulti North (and Tisand)

RBM currently runs four mines on the Zulti North lease, on the dune belt ~20 km north-east of Richards Bay. The older Tisand lease (just north of the port) is largely depleted and now a tailings / stockpile home for the separation plant.

Primary concentrate (heavy-mineral concentrate) is pumped or hauled to the mineral separation plant ~35 km from the mining plants, which splits zircon, rutile and ilmenite.

Processing — MSP + smelters

The same Zulti North complex houses the smelting facility. Ilmenite is reduced in furnaces to titanium slag (the export product that pigment plants buy) and a metallic iron stream.

That furnace step is why RBM is a large, baseload Eskom customer — and why tariff relief is an existential issue. RBM has said that without cheaper power it would already be preparing to close the smelters; 2026 could be a third successive year of losses at smelting and processing.

Zulti South — the replacement orebody

Zulti North is depleting. The next dune block is Zulti South. Rio Tinto approved it in April 2019 (~$463m), then suspended it in January 2020 after violent community unrest. In 2021 RBM general manager Nico Swart was assassinated on his way to work. The project sat idle for six years.

On 2 March 2026 Rio Tinto lifted the suspension and approved a $473 million (~R8.5 billion) first phase. China Harbour Engineering Company (CHEC) — already on Rio’s Simandou iron-ore project in Guinea — is EPC. Construction was due to start in Q1 2026 and take 30 months, with first commercial production in Q4 2028. The stated aim is to hold zircon and ilmenite supply and stretch mine life to 2050. A second phase sits in the long-term plan.

The go-ahead came even though Rio Tinto had put Iron & Titanium (RBM, plus Quebec iron/titanium and US borates) under strategic review — i.e. it could still sell the business. The investment is a life-of-mine decision, not a statement that RBM stays in the group forever.

Power, tariffs and why the furnaces matter
  • RBM wheels renewable energy over Eskom’s grid: Bolobedu solar (Limpopo) is already supplying the KZN operations; Khangela Emoyeni (140 MW wind, Northern/Western Cape) is in construction, ~460 GWh/year, commercial operation targeted late 2026. A third PPA is in the mix.
  • Smelting is still a baseload load. RBM’s growth strategy GM has said that without a fair Eskom tariff the company would already be preparing to shut the smelters.
  • That is the same energy-intensity story as ferrochrome — except here the product is titanium slag, not chrome metal. Processing is not optional: raw ilmenite is a lower-value export.
How this sits next to the other mineral-sands names

South Africa’s heavy-mineral chain is short: RBM (Rio Tinto) at Richards Bay; Tronox at Namakwa Sands (Brand-se-Baai / Saldanha) and Fairbreeze (Mtunzini); MRC at Tormin (West Coast) and the stalled Xolobeni dunes on the Wild Coast. RBM is the only one with a large on-site titanium smelter. The others mostly ship concentrate.
