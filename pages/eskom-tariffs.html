🔌 Eskom Tariffs: Explained Simply

The confusing bits, decoded for normal humans

💡
What's a Tariff?

Think of it like an airline ticket. The price depends on when you fly (time of day), how you book (season), and what class (voltage level).

Eskom's tariff is a bundle of separate charges: the energy itself, the grid to deliver it, the capacity to keep power plants running, and various levies.

⏰
Time-of-Use (TOU)

Peak (🔴 most expensive) — Weekdays 6-9am, 5-8pm. When everyone's using power.

Standard (🟢 mid) — The rest of the working day.

Off-Peak (⚪ cheapest) — Nights and weekends. When demand is lowest.

🌡️
High vs Low Season

High Demand Season (Jun–Aug, winter) — more expensive because heaters are running.

Low Demand Season (Sep–May) — cheaper rates.

Peak rates in winter can be 6× more than off-peak in summer!

⚡
Voltage & Distance

Higher voltage = cheaper per kWh (more efficient transmission). Large factories connect at >132kV and pay less.

Closer to Joburg = cheaper. Beyond 900km adds ~3% transmission surcharge.

🧾
What's Actually in Your Electricity Bill?

A single c/kWh rate hides a lot of complexity. Here's what you're really paying for.

The "energy charge" (Peak/Standard/Off-Peak) that's charted above is only one piece of your total bill. Eskom's Megaflex tariff bundles 10+ separate charges into what you see on your invoice. For a typical large customer, the Active Energy Charge makes up about 60-70% of the total — the rest is fixed and capacity charges you pay regardless of how much power you use.

Charge Component 2025/26 Rate What It Pays For
⚡ Active Energy Charges — You only pay these when you consume power. Vary by time-of-day and season.
🔴 Peak Energy Charge Cost of generating electricity during high-demand hours (6-9am, 5-8pm weekdays). Generators running at full throttle — expensive fuel, older plants.
🟢 Standard Energy Charge Generation cost during normal working hours. Base-load plants running efficiently.
⚪ Off-Peak Energy Charge Cheapest electrons — overnight and weekends when demand is low and only the most efficient plants run.
🏗️ Network & Capacity Charges — Paid on your maximum demand (kVA), not on energy used. These cover the grid.
Generation Capacity Charge R 9.40 / kVA / month Reserving power plant capacity to meet your peak demand. Think of it as a "seat fee" for the power station, even if you don't use it fully.
TUoS (Transmission Network Charge) R 48.10 / kVA / month Transmission Use-of-System — the high-voltage power lines that carry electricity across provinces. Eskom's ~28,000 km of transmission lines.
DUoS (Distribution Network Charge) R 12.50 / kVA / month Distribution Use-of-System — the lower-voltage network that delivers power to your specific location. The poles and cables on your street.
Urban Low Voltage Subsidy R 0.00 / kVA / month (<500V) Cross-subsidy from higher-voltage customers to those connected at low voltage (<500V) who cost more to serve. Zero at <500V because you're already paying the full low-voltage cost.
📋 Fixed & Service Charges — Per-day fees for being connected.
Service Charge R 198.52 / day (>1 MVA) Meter reading, billing, call centre, IT systems. Flat daily fee regardless of usage. For Key Customers it's R 1,118.46/day.
Administration Charge R 19.37 / day Account administration, credit management, and regulatory compliance costs.
💰 Levies, Subsidies & Penalties — Policy-driven charges.
Legacy Charge Stranded costs from old power stations and past investment decisions. This is the "historical baggage" charge — paying for plants that may no longer run but haven't been written off. Commonly called the "load-shedding tax."
Electrification & Rural Subsidy (NN) Cross-subsidy to connect rural households and schools to the grid. Urban customers pay extra so rural areas can have electricity.
Affordability Subsidy Subsidy for low-usage residential customers (Homelight 20A). Large customers help keep power affordable for poor households.
Ancillary Services (DSM) Demand Side Management — paying large customers to reduce usage during peak times. Cheaper than building new plants.
Ancillary Services (ASF) Ancillary Service Fees — grid stability services (frequency control, voltage support, reserves). Like insurance for the grid.
Energy Management Charge NERSA regulatory levy — funding the energy regulator that oversees Eskom.
Environmental Levy Tax on electricity from fossil fuels (coal). Government's carbon pricing mechanism. Applied to non-municipal customers.
Reactive Energy Charge 22.80 c/kVArh Penalty for poor power factor (below 0.96). If your factory's electrical equipment causes inefficiency on the grid, you pay extra.
Excess NMD Penalty 3× normal demand charge Triple penalty if you exceed your Notified Maximum Demand. Eskom planned capacity for you — going over costs 3×.
💡 Bottom line: For 2025/26 at Peak times, the Active Energy Charge () is the headline number, but the Legacy Charge (), Generation Capacity (R 9.40/kVA), and Network Charges (TUoS + DUoS = R 60.60/kVA) add significant fixed costs.
