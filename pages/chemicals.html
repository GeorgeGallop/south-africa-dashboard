What this industry is

Chemicals here means the plants that turn coal, gas, phosphate rock, air and imported sulphur into molecules other factories cannot do without: ammonia, nitric acid, polymers, solvents, explosives, industrial gases and blended fertiliser. Stats SA parks most of this under coke, petroleum, chemical products, rubber and plastics. CAIA (the Chemical and Allied Industries’ Association) and InvestSA have put local chemical manufacturing around R360 billion of sales, about 3.4% of GDP and the third-largest manufacturing sector (about 23% of manufacturing). TIPS, using a slightly tighter “basic chemicals + petroleum” cut, had that pair at about 12% of manufacturing value-add by 2019; the broader chemicals cut is closer to a fifth of factory output.

The industry is vertically concentrated. Sasol’s Secunda and Sasolburg complexes are the source of a large share of South Africa’s basic petrochemicals, polymers and merchant ammonia. Around that sit a handful of integrated groups — AECI, Omnia, Foskor, Afrox — plus multinational speciality houses. Downstream plastics conversion, coatings and consumer chemicals are a long tail of smaller plants.

Read it as a stack, not a list of companies. Feedstock in at the top, formulated product out at the bottom. Green hydrogen and green ammonia sit on the same stack — they swap the source of the hydrogen, they do not invent a new industry.

How the industry is stacked

Five layers, from the mine and the pipeline to the bag, the blast hole and the ship.

1

Feedstock

  • Secunda coal into gasifiers
  • Mozambique gas on ROMPCO into Sasolburg
  • Foskor phosphate rock
  • Imported sulphur; air for N2 and industrial gases
  • Remaining crude / refinery streams
2

Primary / basic

  • Syngas → fuels, olefins, solvents, waxes
  • Haber–Bosch ammonia (Sasol)
  • Sulphuric and phosphoric acid
  • Chlor-alkali
  • Air-separation gases (Afrox)
3

Intermediates

  • Polymers (PE, PP, PVC)
  • Nitric acid, ammonium nitrate
  • MAP / DAP from phosphoric acid
  • Explosives intermediates
  • Fluorochemicals (Pelchem)
4

Formulated

  • Mining explosives (AECI, Omnia BME)
  • Fertiliser blends (Omnia, Kynoch)
  • Water treatment, coatings, crop protection
  • Soaps, detergents, consumer
5

End markets

  • Mining reagents and blast
  • Farms — see Fertilizer
  • Packaging and plastics conversion
  • Liquid fuels
  • Export: acid, polymers, future green NH3

The same ammonia molecule is fertiliser, explosives and (soon) marine fuel. That is why the green-ammonia projects attach to an existing industry rather than sitting off to the side.

Where it sits

Inland complexes
  • Secunda (Mpumalanga) — Sasol CTL. Fuels and chemicals are one plant, not two. TIPS (2024) treats Secunda as the heart of SA’s petrochemicals, plastics, ammonia, fertiliser and explosives chains.
  • Sasolburg (Free State) — gas-based chemicals, Natref, polymers, ammonia into the fertiliser trade.
  • Phalaborwa — Foskor phosphate rock.
  • Gauteng speciality belt — AECI Chamdor, water-treatment and formulated plants around Johannesburg.
Coastal nodes
  • Richards Bay — Foskor acid/MAP, sulphur and ammonia imports, fertiliser export berth.
  • Umbogintwini (KZN) — AECI sulphuric acid and industrial chemicals, a 1908 site.
  • Saldanha — iron-ore port plus the lead green-ammonia / bunkering case. See the hydrogen page.
  • Coega — Hive Hydrogen green-ammonia plant (export scale).
  • Boegoebaai (Northern Cape, planned) — green-hydrogen hub from a greenfield port.

The grey-to-green hinge

South Africa already makes a lot of grey hydrogen — from coal and gas, mostly inside Sasol — and sells grey ammonia into fertiliser and explosives. Green hydrogen is the same molecule from renewable-powered electrolysis. Green ammonia is the same NH3 with that hydrogen. The plants, ports, offtakers and Haber–Bosch know-how already exist; the missing pieces are cheap renewable power, water, and offtake long enough to bank a coastal loop.

That is the Chemicals child page: production process, Saldanha and Coega, bunker carriers, market pricing, and the South Africa–EU iron-ore green corridor. Fertilizer stays under Agriculture because the farm-gate N-P-K story is a demand page, not a plant-construction page.
