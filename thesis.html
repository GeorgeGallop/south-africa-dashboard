Prerequisites for Economic Evolutionary Analysis

Understanding OPF Buildouts and Scenarios in the South African Wholesale Electricity Market (SAWEM)
Why These Prerequisites Matter

Analyzing the economic evolutionary trajectory of South Africa’s power system requires combining power systems engineering, economic dispatch, and scenario-based modeling. A solid grasp of the topics below will help you interpret OPF (Optimal Power Flow) results across different SAWEM futures.

1. Fundamentals of Power Systems Economics

The electricity market is driven by the interaction of supply costs, demand, and infrastructure constraints.

  • Economic Dispatch: Minimizing total generation cost while meeting demand and respecting transmission limits.
  • Merit Order Curve: Ranking generators by marginal cost (from lowest to highest).
  • Locational Marginal Pricing (LMP): Price at each node reflecting energy, congestion, and losses.
  • Levelized Cost of Electricity (LCOE): Long-term cost metric for comparing technologies.
2. Optimal Power Flow (OPF)

OPF is the core optimization tool used to model efficient power system operation and buildout.

Minimize: Σ Ci(Pi)
Subject to: Power balance, line flow limits, generator limits, voltage constraints

Variants include:

  • DC-OPF (linearized, faster)
  • AC-OPF (non-linear, more accurate)
  • Security-Constrained OPF (N-1 reliability)
  • Multi-period / Stochastic OPF (for planning across time and uncertainty)
3. Scenario Planning & Evolutionary Pathways

Future power system trajectories are explored through carefully designed scenarios.

Key dimensions for SAWEM scenarios:

  • Renewables Buildout: Pace of wind, solar PV, and battery storage addition
  • Coal & Gas Retirement: Timing of Eskom fleet decommissioning
  • Demand Growth: Electrification of transport, industry, and hydrogen production
  • Transmission Expansion: New corridors to unlock renewable-rich regions (Northern Cape, Eastern Cape)
  • Carbon Pricing / Policy: Impact of carbon tax and Just Energy Transition Investment Plan (JET IP)
4. Market Design Concepts in SAWEM

South Africa is transitioning toward a competitive wholesale market.

  • Day-Ahead and Real-Time markets
  • Ancillary services procurement
  • Capacity mechanisms and strategic reserves
  • Bilateral contracting vs. market clearing
5. Economic Metrics for Trajectory Evaluation

When comparing OPF buildout scenarios, track these indicators:

  • Total System Cost (CAPEX + OPEX)
  • Cost of Unserved Energy (Value of Lost Load)
  • CO₂ emissions trajectory
  • System adequacy (Loss of Load Expectation - LOLE)
  • Average wholesale price and price volatility
  • Regional economic impacts (job creation in RE zones vs. coal regions)
6. Basic Optimization & Modeling Literacy

Helpful for interpreting results:

  • Linear Programming (LP) and Mixed-Integer Linear Programming (MILP)
  • Understanding shadow prices / dual variables
  • Handling uncertainty (robust optimization, stochastic programming)
Recommended Quick Review Resources
  • IRENA & CSIR reports on South African power system modeling
  • Eskom Transmission Development Plan (TDP)
  • National Energy Regulator of South Africa (NERSA) documents
  • "Power System Economics" by Steven Stoft
  • PyPSA or MATPOWER tutorials for hands-on OPF modeling

Once you're comfortable with these concepts, you're ready to analyze detailed OPF buildout results and evaluate long-term economic evolutionary trajectories for the South African power market under different SAWEM scenarios.
