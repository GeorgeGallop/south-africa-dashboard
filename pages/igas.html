iGas

South African Gas Development Company — the state's gas-infrastructure agent, a CEF subsidiary

iGas (the South African Gas Development Company SOC Ltd) is the government's designated agent for developing the hydrocarbon gas industry, including piped gas, LNG and LPG. It is 100% owned by CEF. Its commercial jewel is the state's shareholding in ROMPCO, the 865 km Mozambique–Secunda gas pipeline. Like PetroSA and SFF, it is being folded into SANPC.

Legal form: SOC Ltd, wholly owned by CEF (100% state)
Mandate: Develop SA's gas industry — pipelines, LNG, LPG, offtake
Core asset: Shareholding in ROMPCO (Republic of Mozambique Pipeline Investments Company) — Temane/Pande to Secunda
Pipeline: ~865 km high-pressure gas transmission
ROMPCO partners: South African state (via iGas), Mozambican state, Sasol — governments have described a 40 / 40 / remainder split
Cash: ROMPCO dividends are a material CEF Group income line (CEF has cited ~R940 million in a recent budget year, ±20%)

iGas

ROMPCO · LNG / LPG development · SANPC merger

What it is

iGas was created so the state had a gas company that was not PetroSA (upstream/GTL) and not Sasol (the private incumbent on the Mozambique pipe and the inland gas grid). Its job is infrastructure and market development: get gas from where it is produced (Mozambique, future LNG terminals, maybe the South Coast) to where South African industry can burn it.

The one asset that already pays is ROMPCO. The pipeline carries gas from the Temane/Pande fields near Vilanculos to Secunda, where it feeds Sasol's chemicals complex and, downstream, Gauteng industrial users. iGas is how the South African government sits on that pipe. Sasol remains a commercial partner and operator-adjacent player; it does not own iGas.

What it is used for

  • ROMPCO dividends and governance — the state's return on the Mozambique gas corridor, and its seat at the table when volumes, tariffs and expansion are decided.
  • New gas delivery — LNG import, LPG, and other pipeline projects that the Gas Master Plan and IPP gas programme assume someone on the state side will sponsor.
  • Komati / gas-to-power enablement — CEF has publicly tied additional power-purchase volumes to the ROMPCO corridor (an 800 MW figure has been cited as already unlocked, with more PPAs discussed).

Who buys / who offtakes

The gas in the pipe is bought by Sasol and industrial users in Mpumalanga and Gauteng, not by households. iGas's own "customer" is the CEF Group (dividends) and the state (energy-security policy). Future LNG offtakers would be gas-to-power IPPs, petrochemicals, and the same inland industrial belt. There is no listed iGas share and no private controlling owner.

The Mozambique problem

ROMPCO only has value if Mozambique keeps producing. Temane/Pande are mature. The Gas Master Plan already treats a late-2020s "Mozambique cliff" as a planning risk. That is why iGas's development book (LNG at Richards Bay / Coega, South Coast gas, Karoo in the far background) matters more than the historical 25% then 40% stake in a depleting pipe. Without new molecules, iGas is a dividend stream in runoff.

SANPC transfer

iGas is one of the three CEF oil-and-gas subsidiaries Cabinet ordered merged in June 2020. From 1 April 2025, transportation assets previously held by iGas (and SFF) have been described as integrated into SANPC under lease-and-assign. Employees moved in the first SANPC transfer wave. iGas remains a legal SOC until the consolidation is complete; the operating gas-infrastructure role is intended to live inside SANPC.
